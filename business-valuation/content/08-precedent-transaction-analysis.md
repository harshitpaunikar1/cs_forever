# Precedent Transaction Analysis

## Overview

Precedent transaction analysis values a company by looking at prices paid in past M&A deals for similar targets. Unlike trading comps, these prices include the control premium that buyers paid to take over the business. It tells you what acquirers — not passive investors — are willing to pay.

---

## Why It Matters

In an M&A process, the seller cares about what buyers will offer, not what the stock market prints. Precedents set the expectation floor and ceiling. Using the wrong precedents — stale, or in a different cycle — makes a deal look over- or underpriced and can derail negotiations.

## Key Principles

- Pick recent transactions — within 2–3 years — to match current market conditions.
- Match deal type: strategic vs. financial buyer prices differ.
- Include the control premium paid above the pre-deal share price.
- Adjust multiples for deal size, synergies, and market cycle.
- Fewer, well-matched precedents beat a long list of loose ones.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Control Premium** | Extra price a buyer pays over market value for majority control. |
| **Strategic Buyer** | A company in the same or adjacent industry, buying for synergies. |
| **Financial Buyer** | A private equity or similar fund buying for financial returns. |
| **Announced Deal Value** | Headline equity or enterprise value disclosed at deal announcement. |

## Use Case

An investment banker defending a sell-side price builds a table of 8 recent comparable takeovers to justify a 30% control premium to the board.

## Scenario

> A regional cement company was being pitched to buyers. Trading comps implied 8x EV/EBITDA. But three recent regional cement takeovers had all cleared 11x. The banker used precedents to anchor 10.5x in negotiations, adding 600 crore to the final sale price over what trading comps would have suggested.

## Examples

- Pharma takeovers in 2023 cleared at a median 15x EBITDA — used as a floor for a new sale.
- A fintech acquired at 8x revenue sets the bar for the next deal in the same segment.

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Precedent Transaction Analysis (PTA) values a company by looking at what acquirers actually paid for similar targets in past M&A deals — inclusive of the control premium, synergy value, and auction dynamics baked into the announced price. For an IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader advising on M&A, justifying a bid, or defending a sale valuation, PTA anchors negotiations in observed cash-on-the-table rather than passive public-market prints. Decision: what price floor/ceiling should we fight for, walk away from, or pay — given what the last 2–3 years of comparable deals actually cleared at.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Precedent Filter Funnel
**Purpose:** Shrink 500 raw deals into 6–10 defensible comps.

```
      ALL M&A DEALS (last 5 yrs, global)     ~5,000
              |
              v
       SAME / ADJACENT INDUSTRY              ~400
              |
              v
       SAME SIZE BAND (+/- 2x EV)            ~120
              |
              v
       SAME CYCLE (<3 yrs, same regime)      ~40
              |
              v
       SAME STRUCTURE (control, cash/stock)  ~18
              |
              v
       DATA QUALITY (EBITDA disclosed)       ~8-10
              |
              v
       >>> DEFENSIBLE PRECEDENT SET <<<
```

**Components:** Industry code (GICS/NAICS) → EV band → vintage → stake/consideration type → disclosure filter.
**IT/AI worked example:** AI-coding-assistant sale. Start with 420 software deals 2022–2024 → filter to DevTools/AI-infra (~55) → $200M–$2B EV band (~22) → post-2023 rate-regime (~14) → >50% control, cash-dominant (~9) → disclosed ARR multiple (~7). Final set: GitHub×Copilot-era peers.
**Trigger:** Whenever starting a fairness opinion, sell-side book, or bid-defence memo.

### 3.2 Strategic vs Financial Buyer Pricing Matrix

| Dimension | Strategic (Trade) Buyer | Financial (PE) Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Typical premium over market | 30–45% | 20–30% |
| Multiple paid vs trading comps | +2 to +4 turns | +1 to +2 turns |
| Rationale | Revenue + cost synergies | Leverage + operational levers + exit |
| Hold period | Indefinite | 4–6 years |
| Deal structure | Mostly cash or stock | Cash (LBO) + rollover |
| Sensitivity to cycle | Medium (strategic fit) | High (debt markets) |

**IT/AI example:** Mid-market SaaS with $40M ARR. Strategic (say, Salesforce-class) may pay 10–12× ARR because cross-sell lifts LTV 30%. PE would cap near 7–9× ARR unless leveraged buyout math works.
**Trigger:** Before auction launch — sequences bidder outreach and sets reserve.

### 3.3 Control Premium Bridge

```
UNAFFECTED SHARE PRICE (30-day avg before rumour)   100
      + Illiquidity / float relief                  + 5
      + Minority-to-majority (control)              + 20
      + Expected synergies (buyer-specific)         + 10
      + Auction / competitive tension               +  7
      = ANNOUNCED OFFER PRICE                       = 142
           (42% control premium)
```

**Components:** Unaffected price (rumour-free), liquidity uplift, control value, synergy share, auction premium.
**IT/AI example:** Listed IT-services firm trades at ₹800 pre-rumour. Offer ₹1,140 = 42% premium. Bridge: +5% liquidity (thin float), +20% control, +10% synergies (overlap in BFSI vertical), +7% auction (3 bidders).
**Trigger:** Defending offer price to independent directors or proxy advisers (ISS/Glass Lewis).

### 3.4 Cycle-Adjusted Multiple Table (Optional)

| Vintage | SaaS EV/ARR median | Adjustment to 2024 market | Usable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 15.0× | −45% (ZIRP regime) | No — stale |
| 2022H1 | 11.0× | −25% | Caution |
| 2023 | 7.0× | −5% | Yes |
| 2024 | 6.5× | baseline | Yes |

## 4. Formulas

### 4.1 Control Premium %
```
Control Premium % = (Offer Price − Unaffected Price) / Unaffected Price
```
**Threshold:** 25–40% typical for US/EU; 15–30% India listed; >50% signals auction or scarce asset.
**Example:** Cement target, unaffected ₹420, offer ₹546 → 30% premium. Aligned with sector median.

### 4.2 Announced Enterprise Value
```
Announced EV = Equity Consideration + Assumed Debt − Acquired Cash
            (+ Minority Interest + Preferred − Non-core asset value)
```
**Threshold:** Always reconcile to press release; if >5% off, check debt definition.
**Example:** AI-infra target — equity paid $1.2B, assumed debt $150M, cash $80M → EV = $1,270M. If LTM revenue $100M → 12.7× revenue.

### 4.3 Synergy-Adjusted EV/EBITDA
```
Synergy-Adj. EV/EBITDA = (EV + PV of Net Synergies) / LTM EBITDA
```
**Threshold:** Strategic should pay +1 to +3 turns above trading; >+5 turns = buyer overpaying.
**Example:** EV $2,000M, LTM EBITDA $200M → 10× unadj. Add PV synergies $300M → synergy-adj multiple = 11.5×. Still defensible if trading comps at 9×.

### 4.4 Implied Multiple at Bid
```
Implied EV/EBITDA = Bid EV / Target LTM EBITDA
Implied EV/ARR   = Bid EV / Target ARR
```
**Threshold:** Compare with precedent median ± IQR; bid within IQR = defensible.
**Example:** AI startup $60M ARR, acquired at $720M EV → 12× ARR. Trading SaaS comps at 6.5×. Implied control + strategic premium ≈ 85% over trading — justified only with credible synergy case.

### 4.5 Average / Median / IQR of Precedent Multiples (Optional)
```
Median = middle value of sorted multiples
IQR    = P75 − P25  (use IQR, not range, to flag outliers)
```
**Example:** 8 recent pharma deals: multiples 12, 13, 14, 15, 15, 16, 17, 22. Median = 15, IQR = 3. The 22× is a one-off scarcity deal — drop or footnote.

### 4.6 Quick check — Cement scenario
Trading 8× EV/EBITDA → 3 recent takeovers cleared 11× → banker anchors 10.5× → on LTM EBITDA of ₹240 cr, uplift = 2.5 turns × ₹240 cr = ₹600 cr extra sale consideration.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use recent precedents (≤2–3 yrs, same rate/liquidity regime) | Don't mix cycles (2021 ZIRP deals with 2024 high-rate) |
| Segregate strategic from financial buyers in medians | Don't combine strategic + financial in a single median |
| Reconcile announced equity to EV using assumed-debt and cash | Don't forget assumed-debt adjustments — skews EV by 10–30% |
| Read the merger agreement for structure, earn-outs, CVRs | Don't use announced value without deal-structure detail |
| Scale multiples to target size (size discount/premium) | Don't extrapolate mega-deal multiples to a small target |
| Flag regulatory overhang priced in (break fee, CVR, gap) | Don't ignore regulatory risk baked into headline price |
| Prefer 6–8 well-matched comps over 25 loose ones | Don't pad the list to look rigorous — weak comps dilute median |
| Cross-check with trading comps and DCF | Don't rely on PTA alone — it amplifies cycle distortions |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — AI/SaaS target (pro)
Series-D AI agent platform, $50M ARR, advising founder on sell-side. Build 6-deal set (2022–2024): DevTools/AI-infra control deals, $300M–$1.5B EV. Cycle-adjust 2022 deals down ~25%. Median synergy-adj EV/ARR = 9×; IQR 7.5–11×. Set walk-away at 7× ($350M), target 9× ($450M), stretch 11× ($550M). Tools: **PitchBook** for private-deal multiples, **CapIQ Precedent Screen** for filters, **Mergermarket** for colour on auctions.

### Scenario 2 — IT-services rollup (pro)
India-listed IT-services (₹4,000 cr revenue) receives approach. Strategic (global SI) vs Financial (PE+platform). Control-premium bridge: unaffected ₹900; strategic at ₹1,260 (+40%), PE at ₹1,125 (+25%). Decomposition: control = 20%, synergies = 15% (strategic only), auction = 5%. Guidance to board: run dual-track — maximise competition, expect strategic to clear.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE (failed deal)
2024 sell-side for vertical-SaaS (HR-tech), $30M ARR. Banker anchored on **2021 SaaS takeover multiples at 15×+ ARR**, pricing expectation ₹3,700 cr. 2024 market clears at 6–7× ARR; buyers walked after round 2. Seller held out 9 months; outcome: deal failed, two top engineering leads left (retention cost ~₹12 cr), advisory fees sunk (~₹18 cr), deferred funding round forced down-round (−30% valuation, ~₹400 cr of paper loss), lost momentum with customers. **Quantified cost of using stale precedents: ~₹430 cr + 9 months.** Lesson: cycle-adjust or pick fresh comps.

**Tools commonly used:** Mergermarket, PitchBook, S&P CapIQ Precedent Transactions screen, Bloomberg M&A (MA<GO>), SDC Platinum, Refinitiv DealScan, 451 Research (for tech).

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Define** the subject target (industry GICS, size band, geography, deal rationale) in a one-page brief.
2. **Pull** raw deal list from Mergermarket + CapIQ + PitchBook (dedupe by deal ID).
3. **Filter** through the Precedent Filter Funnel (industry → size → cycle → structure → data quality).
4. **Classify** each deal: strategic vs financial, control vs minority, cash vs stock vs earn-out.
5. **Compute** LTM EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue, EV/ARR; adjust for earn-outs, CVRs, assumed debt.
6. **Cycle-adjust** older multiples to current regime (interest-rate, sector index proxy).
7. **Segment** medians & IQRs separately for strategic vs financial; note outliers with footnotes.
8. **Triangulate** with trading comps + DCF; write a memo with recommended bid/walk-away and auction sequencing.

## 8. Content Quality Audit

**Covered well in source:**
- Core premise: prices paid include control premium, different from stock prints.
- Principles: recent, matched deal type, control premium, size/synergy/cycle adjustments.
- Key terms (Control Premium, Strategic, Financial, Announced Deal Value).

**Underplayed / missing from source:**

- Deal-structure adjustments (all-stock vs cash, collars, walk-rights).
- Earn-outs and CVRs that **hide the real price** — headline EV overstates cash paid.
- Explicit cycle-adjustment methodology (index scaling).
- Cross-border premium (FX, withholding tax, inversion math).
- Regulatory overhang priced in (break fees, CVRs for antitrust risk).
- Minority vs majority deal treatment and why minority deals should be excluded or re-weighted.
- Synergy capitalisation pitfalls — PV of synergies is easy to overstate, common auditor finding.

**Supplement with:**

## 9. Quick-Recall Card
- PTA anchors M&A price in what acquirers *actually paid* — includes control premium + synergies + auction tension, not just fundamentals.
- Pick 6–10 recent (≤3 yrs), same-cycle, same-structure deals; segregate strategic from financial buyers.
- Always bridge: unaffected price → liquidity → control → synergy → auction → announced price.
- Reconcile announced equity → EV via assumed debt and cash; read the merger agreement for earn-outs/CVRs.
- Triangulate with trading comps and DCF; PTA alone amplifies cycle distortions.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: **"Given what acquirers actually paid for targets like ours in the last 24 months — cycle-adjusted and structure-adjusted — what is the defensible bid/ask we should anchor negotiations on, and where do we walk away?"**
