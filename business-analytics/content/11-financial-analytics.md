# Financial Analytics

## Overview
Financial analytics applies quantitative methods to financial data for better planning, forecasting, risk management, and performance evaluation. It spans areas like budgeting, profitability analysis, credit risk assessment, and investment valuation. The discipline helps finance teams move beyond historical reporting to forward-looking, data-driven financial management.

---

## Why It Matters
Financial decisions carry high stakes and long-lasting consequences. Whether it is setting a budget, evaluating an acquisition, or managing cash flow, financial analytics provides the rigor and evidence needed to make these decisions with greater precision and reduced uncertainty.

## Key Principles
- Integrate data from multiple sources including accounting systems, market feeds, and operational databases for a complete financial picture
- Build scenario-based models that test financial outcomes under best-case, worst-case, and most-likely conditions
- Separate leading indicators from lagging indicators to enable proactive financial management
- Quantify risk in monetary terms so stakeholders can weigh potential losses against expected returns

## Key Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Variance Analysis** | The comparison of actual financial results to budgeted or forecasted figures to identify and explain deviations |
| **Cash Flow Forecasting** | The process of estimating future inflows and outflows of cash to ensure the business can meet its obligations |
| **Return on Investment (ROI)** | A ratio that measures the gain or loss generated by an investment relative to its cost, expressed as a percentage |
| **Sensitivity Analysis** | A technique that examines how changes in one or more input variables affect a financial outcome, used to assess risk and uncertainty |

## Use Case
A CFO uses financial analytics to build a rolling 13-week cash flow forecast, giving the leadership team early warning of potential liquidity gaps two months before they would occur.

## Scenario
> A mid-size manufacturer is considering opening a new plant. The finance team builds a discounted cash flow model incorporating construction costs, projected demand, and three economic scenarios. The analysis shows the investment pays back in four years under the base case but takes seven years under the pessimistic scenario. The board uses this insight to negotiate more flexible lease terms before committing.

## Examples
- Running a profitability analysis by product line to identify which products contribute most to overhead coverage and which should be repriced or discontinued
- Using Monte Carlo simulation to estimate the probability distribution of a project's net present value before approving the capital expenditure

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Framework 1: NPV / IRR / Payback Decision Triad
**Purpose:** Screen capital investments with complementary metrics — no single metric is enough.

**Text Diagram:**
```
                       NPV
                     (DCF-based $)
                         │
         ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐
         │               │               │
       IRR            PAYBACK          SENSITIVITY
     (implicit %    (quick screen)    & SCENARIO
      return)                         (risk bands)

  Decision rule stack:
   1. NPV > 0 at firm WACC (+ risk premium)?       — necessary
   2. IRR > hurdle rate?                            — supportive
   3. Payback < threshold (strategic choice)?      — liquidity guard
   4. NPV stays > 0 under plausible downside?      — risk check
   5. Monte Carlo P(NPV > 0) > ~70%?               — robustness
```

Components:
- **NPV:** the "right" answer per corp-finance theory; size of the win in $
- **IRR:** implicit return; comparable across projects; cautious with multi-sign cash flows
- **Payback:** liquidity guard; strategic for high-uncertainty contexts
- **Sensitivity + scenario:** robustness

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** SaaS decides whether to build a self-serve platform ($6M upfront, 5-year horizon).
- NPV @ 12% WACC: +$4.2M
- IRR: 21%
- Payback: 3.4 years
- Sensitivity: NPV stays positive if conversion drops from 4% to 3%; goes negative if < 2.5%
- Monte Carlo: P(NPV > 0) = 82%

Decision: green-light. Risk mitigation: include a Phase 1 gate on conversion rate before full build.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Any capex or product-investment >$500k.

### Framework 2: Variance Analysis Decomposition
**Purpose:** Turn "we missed plan" into actionable attribution by driver.

**Text Diagram:**
```
  TOTAL REVENUE VARIANCE
       │
       ├── Price Variance     = (Actual Price − Budget Price) × Actual Qty
       │
       ├── Volume Variance    = (Actual Qty − Budget Qty) × Budget Price
       │
       └── Mix Variance       = Σ over segments: ΔMix × segment-price effect

  TOTAL COST VARIANCE
       │
       ├── Input Price Variance   = (Actual unit cost − Budget unit cost) × Actual volume
       │
       ├── Efficiency Variance     = (Actual units − Standard units) × Standard cost
       │
       └── Overhead Variance        = Fixed-cost plan vs actual
```

Components:
- Each variance has a named owner: Pricing → Sales; Volume → Sales/Marketing; Input Price → Procurement; Efficiency → Operations

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** SaaS Q3: revenue $12M vs budget $14M. Variance $-2M.
- Price Variance: actual ARPU $195 vs budget $210; × 5,000 customers = −$0.8M
- Volume Variance: 5,000 customers vs 5,500 budget; × $210 = −$1.05M
- Mix: Enterprise share 18% vs 25%; effect ≈ −$0.15M

Decision: price erosion (discounting) is the #2 issue; volume is #1. Action: investigate discounting controls (why are AEs cutting price?) and lead-gen gap simultaneously.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Every month-end FP&A review where "we missed revenue" comes up without diagnosis.

### Framework 3: Sensitivity Tornado + Scenario Framework
**Purpose:** Rank which inputs most move the output; frame extreme-case outcomes.

**Text Diagram:**
```
         Impact on NPV ($M)
    -4  -3  -2  -1   0   +1  +2  +3  +4
       ├────────────┬─────────────┤
  Churn│███████████───[baseline]──────  ← most sensitive
  CAC   │    ████████────[base]───
  Price │         ██████───[base]───
  Growth│             ████──[base]─
  Discount rate │       ██──[base]─  ← least sensitive

Scenario table:
 Input      │ Pessimistic │ Base      │ Optimistic
 ──────────┼──────────────┼──────────┼─────────────
 Churn       │ 3%/mo       │ 2%/mo     │ 1.5%/mo
 CAC          │ $7k         │ $5k       │ $4k
 Price        │ $140        │ $180      │ $220
 Growth      │ 15% YoY     │ 25% YoY   │ 40% YoY
 NPV          │ −$2.3M      │ +$4.2M    │ +$12.1M
```

Components:
- **Tornado chart:** ranks sensitivity of each driver (hold others constant)
- **Scenario table:** bundles drivers into coherent stories

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** CFO reviews an AI platform investment. Tornado shows churn is 2× as sensitive as CAC; 5× more sensitive than discount rate. Action: focus diligence on retention assumptions and on contract-term risk before committing.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Investment committees; board approval for capex; product-investment gate reviews.

## 4. Formulas

### Formula 1: NPV
**Formula:** `NPV = Σ CF_t / (1 + r)^t` for t = 0, 1, 2, ..., T

**Variables:**
- CF_t = cash flow in period t (CF_0 typically negative investment)
- r = discount rate (usually WACC for standard projects)
- T = project horizon

**Why this formula exists:** Standard test for value creation. NPV > 0 means the project beats the cost of capital.

**How to interpret the output:**
- NPV > 0 → invest
- NPV < 0 → reject (or revisit assumptions)
- NPV ≈ 0 → indifferent; pivot on strategic fit

**Worked example:** 5-year platform investment; CF_0 = −$6M; CF_1 to CF_5 = [$1M, $2M, $3M, $3M, $2M]; r = 12%.
- NPV = −6 + 1/1.12 + 2/1.12² + 3/1.12³ + 3/1.12⁴ + 2/1.12⁵ = −6 + 0.89 + 1.59 + 2.13 + 1.91 + 1.13 = **+$1.65M**.

Decision: positive NPV at WACC 12% → invest.

**Data source:** Cash flows from FP&A model (Excel / Anaplan / Pigment); WACC from corporate finance.

---

### Formula 2: IRR
**Formula:** IRR is the rate `r*` that makes `Σ CF_t / (1 + r*)^t = 0`. No closed form; solved numerically.

**Variables:**
- Same cash flows as NPV
- r* = IRR

**Why this formula exists:** Expresses project return as a rate, comparable to the hurdle rate.

**How to interpret the output:**
- IRR > WACC + risk premium → invest (usually)
- Multiple sign changes in cash flows → IRR may have multiple roots (caution)
- For mutually exclusive projects → use NPV; IRR can mislead on scale

**Worked example:** Same CFs as NPV example. Solve numerically: IRR ≈ 18%. At hurdle 15% → green light.

**Data source:** Excel `IRR()`, Python `numpy.irr` (deprecated → use `numpy_financial.irr`), Google Sheets `IRR()`.

---

### Formula 3: WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital)
**Formula:** `WACC = (E/V) × Re + (D/V) × Rd × (1 − t)`

**Variables:**
- E = market value of equity, D = market value of debt, V = E + D
- Re = cost of equity (CAPM)
- Rd = cost of debt (pre-tax)
- t = marginal tax rate

**Why this formula exists:** Blends equity and debt costs for the firm's hurdle rate.

**How to interpret the output:**
- WACC typical range for tech: 8–14%
- Higher WACC → higher hurdle for investments → fewer pass NPV test
- Capital-structure changes move WACC

**Worked example:** Firm: E = $800M, D = $200M; Re = 13% (CAPM); Rd = 6% pre-tax; t = 25%.
- WACC = 0.8 × 13% + 0.2 × 6% × 0.75 = 10.4% + 0.9% = **11.3%**.

**Data source:** E from market cap or VC-implied; D from balance sheet; Re from CAPM using internal β; Rd from debt rates; t from tax-line.

---

### Formula 4: Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)
**Formula:** `CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO`

**Variables:**
- DSO = Days Sales Outstanding = (Accounts Receivable / Revenue) × 365
- DIO = Days Inventory Outstanding = (Inventory / COGS) × 365
- DPO = Days Payable Outstanding = (Accounts Payable / COGS) × 365

**Why this formula exists:** Measures how many days cash is locked in operations. Lower is better.

**How to interpret the output:**
- Negative CCC (Amazon, Dell historically) → funded by suppliers; cash-generating operations
- 0–30 days → strong
- 30–60 → typical
- \> 90 → cash-intensive; look for working-capital optimisation

**Worked example:** SaaS company: DSO 55, DIO 0 (no inventory), DPO 30. CCC = **55 − 0 + 0 = 25 days adjusted** (actually 55 − 30 = 25). If DSO reduced to 40 via billing automation, CCC drops to 10 days → $2M cash freed on $50M revenue.

**Data source:** Balance sheet and P&L; computed monthly in FP&A dashboard.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Rely on payback alone — ignores value after payback | Use NPV as primary; payback as complement |
| Use IRR on non-conventional cash-flow profiles without care | Switch to NPV for multi-sign cash flows |
| Report point estimates only | Show tornado + scenario + Monte Carlo |
| Apply firm WACC to every project | Adjust for project-specific risk (higher WACC for riskier, e.g. R&D) |
| Mix up cost of debt pre- and post-tax | Always use after-tax Rd in WACC |
| Treat budget as untouchable | Adopt rolling forecasts and driver-based planning |
| Analyse variance without naming owner | Assign each variance an accountable leader + corrective-action date |
| Ignore working-capital effects | Track CCC as rigorously as P&L lines |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: SaaS CFO Evaluating a Platform Build vs Buy
**Situation:** $50M ARR SaaS debates building an in-house data-integration layer ($8M, 18 months) vs buying a vendor ($2M/year for 5 years).

**Applicable framework/metric:** NPV + Sensitivity + Hurdle.

**Analysis:**
- Build: CF₀ −$8M; savings $3M/yr for years 3–5; NPV @ 12% ≈ +$0.4M; IRR 13%
- Buy: CF₀ 0; cost $2M/yr; equivalent NPV ≈ −$7.2M (pure cost)
- vs. not acting (no savings): 0 NPV

Sensitivity on build: if launch delayed 6 months → NPV drops to −$1.1M.

**Decision rule:** Prefer higher-NPV option if delta > 15% AND execution risk manageable.

**Action (Monday morning):** Build, with strict schedule milestones. Stage-gate the project: if 12 months in the prototype doesn't hit spec, switch to vendor.

---

### Scenario 2: Consulting Firm Reviewing Client Capex Discipline
**Situation:** A retailer client approves 80% of proposed capex. Return data shows 35% destroy value.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Hurdle rate + NPV + Sensitivity.

**Analysis:**
- Root cause: projects evaluated on payback only; no NPV discipline.
- Introduce: all capex > $1M requires NPV at WACC + 3% risk premium; tornado chart showing top 3 sensitivities.
- Institute formal post-audit of closed projects at 12 and 24 months.

**Decision rule:** No capex > $1M approved without tornado + base/bear/bull NPV.

**Action:** Roll out updated capex approval template. Train FP&A team on Monte Carlo. Post-audit findings reviewed annually by CFO and board.

---

### Scenario 3 (Anti-example): IRR-on-Autopilot Defeats NPV
**Situation:** A PE firm ranks 4 projects by IRR alone. Selects highest IRR (42%) over a larger NPV project (IRR 22%, NPV +$18M vs high-IRR NPV +$3M).

**Applicable framework/metric:** NPV vs IRR for mutually exclusive projects.

**Analysis (what goes wrong):**
- IRR is scale-free — small early wins can show high IRR but tiny NPV
- Choosing by IRR sacrificed $15M of value

**Cost of this mistake:** $15M foregone NPV over 5 years; PE fund return suffers.

**Decision rule:** For mutually exclusive projects, NPV > IRR as the decision metric.

**Action:** Rework investment-committee deck template: NPV front-and-centre; IRR shown as supporting; scale (investment size) explicit.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Standardise NPV + IRR + Payback + Sensitivity template** — Excel / Pigment / Anaplan deck. Required for any investment >$500k.
2. **Compute and publish firm WACC annually** — with project-adjustments for R&D, M&A, new-geography categories.
3. **Install 13-week rolling cash-flow forecast** — updated weekly; one-page exec summary.
4. **Variance analysis with named owners** — month-end P&L decomposition into price, volume, mix, input-cost, efficiency. Owner for each.
5. **Move from annual budget to driver-based rolling forecast** — Pigment / Anaplan / Cube.dev; quarterly re-forecast.
6. **Monte Carlo on capex decisions** — Python (simpy) or Crystal Ball / @Risk; report P(NPV > 0) alongside point estimate.
7. **Track cash-conversion cycle monthly** — flag DSO/DIO/DPO anomalies.
8. **Post-audit closed projects** — 12-month and 24-month reviews; compare actual vs planned NPV.
