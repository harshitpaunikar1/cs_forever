# Equity Analysis

## Overview
Equity analysis is the evaluation of a company's stock to determine whether it represents a good investment. Analysts study financial statements, industry trends, competitive positioning, and management quality to form a view on whether a stock is likely to rise or fall in value. The goal is to make informed decisions about buying, holding, or selling shares.

---

## Why It Matters
Stocks represent ownership in real businesses, and their long-term prices reflect the underlying health and growth of those businesses. Equity analysis helps investors cut through market noise and focus on what truly drives stock prices: earnings, cash flow, competitive advantages, and growth prospects. Without this analysis, investors are simply guessing which stocks will perform well.

## Key Principles
- Financial statement analysis reveals a company's profitability, efficiency, leverage, and liquidity
- Comparing a company's financial ratios to industry peers highlights relative strengths and weaknesses
- Qualitative factors such as management quality, brand strength, and competitive moats are just as important as the numbers
- Earnings growth and the sustainability of that growth are the primary long-term drivers of stock prices

## Key Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Earnings Per Share** | A company's net profit divided by the number of outstanding shares, showing profit attributable to each share |
| **Price-to-Earnings Ratio** | The stock price divided by earnings per share, indicating how much investors pay for each dollar of earnings |
| **Return on Equity** | Net income divided by shareholders' equity, measuring how efficiently a company uses investor capital to generate profit |
| **Free Cash Flow** | Cash generated by operations minus capital expenditures, representing cash available to shareholders and creditors |

## Use Case
An investment fund screens for stocks with consistently high return on equity and low price-to-earnings ratios relative to their sector, then conducts deep-dive analysis on the top candidates before adding them to the portfolio.

## Scenario
> Sofia is researching a retail chain for a potential investment. She reviews the company's income statement and notices that revenue has grown steadily for five years while profit margins have expanded. She also checks that management has been buying back shares, which boosts earnings per share. Satisfied with both the numbers and the company's competitive position, she adds the stock to her watchlist.

## Examples
- Analyzing a technology company's revenue growth rate and comparing it to competitors to assess whether its premium valuation is justified
- Reviewing a bank's loan-loss provisions and capital ratios to evaluate its financial health before investing in its stock

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Equity analysis is the structured evaluation of a company's stock (financials, industry, competitive position, management) to form a view on whether the underlying business will compound earnings and cash flow faster than the market expects.
For an IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader, the lens is threefold: (a) evaluating their own employer stock (RSU/ESOP concentration), (b) assessing pitch-deck-stage or listed peer/competitor companies, (c) sanity-checking the competitive moat of vendors and partners they bet the product roadmap on.
The decision is binary-with-sizing: buy/hold/trim the position, and at what weight — driven by earnings durability, ROIC vs WACC, moat strength, and management capital-allocation track record.

## 2. Jargon & Terminology

| # | Term | Plain-English Definition | IT/AI/Product/Consulting Lens |
|---|------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | EPS (Basic vs Diluted) | Net income per share; diluted includes options, RSUs, convertibles | Diluted is the honest number for SaaS firms with heavy stock-based comp |
| 2 | P/E (Trailing vs Forward) | Price / past or next-12-month EPS | Forward P/E is what the market is paying for consensus AI growth |
| 3 | PEG | P/E divided by earnings growth % | Anchors "is this 40x P/E justified by 35% growth?" |

| 7 | ROIC | NOPAT / Invested Capital | The real compounding engine; compare vs WACC |
| 8 | DuPont Decomposition | ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier | Reveals whether ROE is margin-led, asset-light, or leverage-led |
| 9 | FCF | Operating Cash Flow − CapEx | Cash available to shareholders; harder to fake than EPS |
| 10 | FCF Yield | FCF / Market Cap | Inverse of a cash-flow P/E; > 5% is attractive |

| 12 | Revenue Growth | YoY top-line change | The oxygen for every growth-stock thesis |

| 16 | Burn Multiple | Net Burn / Net New ARR | < 1 great, > 2 inefficient growth |

| 20 | 10-K / Annual Report | Annual regulator filing | The primary source; read it before any broker note |
| 21 | MD&A | Management Discussion & Analysis section of annual report | Where tone, caveats, and segment commentary live |

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 DuPont ROE Decomposition
**Purpose:** Diagnose whether ROE comes from operating excellence, asset efficiency, or leverage.
```
ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
       (NI/Rev)     (Rev/Assets)    (Assets/Equity)
```
**Components:** margin quality (pricing power), asset turnover (capital intensity), leverage (risk).
**Worked example:** Indian IT services major — ROE 28% = 18% margin × 1.1 turnover × 1.4 leverage → margin-driven, low-risk. An AI-SaaS challenger showing ROE 28% from 6% margin × 0.8 turnover × 5.8 leverage is a leverage trap.
**Trigger:** any time reported ROE changes > 300 bps YoY.

### 3.2 5-Source Moat Matrix
**Purpose:** Separate buzzword "AI moat" from structural moat.
```
Moat Source        Example (IT/AI/Product)           Durability (1-5)
Network Effect     LinkedIn, GitHub                  5
Switching Cost     ServiceNow, SAP                   4
Brand              Accenture, McKinsey               3
Scale              AWS, Azure infra                  4
IP / Process       ASML, NVIDIA CUDA stack           5
```
**Worked example:** A pre-IPO AI-SaaS selling GPT wrappers scores 0/5 on network, 1/5 on switching cost (data portability), 1/5 on brand — effectively no moat; passing.
**Trigger:** before paying > 10x sales multiple.

### 3.3 Quality-Growth-Value 2x2 (ROIC vs Growth)
**Purpose:** Place a stock on the compounding map.
```
              Low Growth (<10%)     High Growth (>20%)
High ROIC     BUY: compounder       CORE HOLD: quality growth
(>15%)        (dividend + buyback)  (pay up within reason)
Low ROIC      AVOID: value trap     WATCH: unproven; demand
(<10%)                              path to ROIC>WACC
```
**Worked example:** Microsoft sits top-right; an unprofitable AI-SaaS sits bottom-right until ROIC > WACC is visible within 3 years.
**Trigger:** annual portfolio review.

### 3.4 SaaS Unit Economics Anchor
**Purpose:** Connect operating metrics to equity valuation.
```
Lever              Healthy       Red-Flag
Rule of 40         > 40          < 25
NRR                > 115%        < 100%
LTV / CAC          > 3           < 1.5
Burn Multiple      < 1           > 2
Magic Number       > 0.75        < 0.4
```
**Worked example:** Consulting-tech carve-out shows Rule-of-40 = 48, NRR 122%, Burn Multiple 0.6 → justifies 12x forward sales. A peer at Rule-of-40 = 22, Burn 3.8 cannot justify the same multiple.
**Trigger:** every earnings release.

## 4. Formulas

1. **EPS = Net Income / Diluted Shares Outstanding**
   - Threshold: diluted-to-basic gap > 10% = heavy dilution risk.
   - Example (AI-SaaS listed peer): NI ₹120 Cr / 10 Cr diluted shares = EPS ₹12. Basic EPS ₹14 → 14% gap → dilution flag.

2. **P/E = Price / EPS ; Forward P/E = Price / Next-12-mo EPS**
   - Threshold: > sector median + 1 SD demands a growth story.
   - Example: Price ₹600, fwd EPS ₹15 → Forward P/E = 40. Sector median 25 → premium 60%.

3. **PEG = P/E / Earnings Growth %**
   - Threshold: < 1 attractive, 1-2 fair, > 2 stretched.
   - Example: P/E 40 / growth 35 = PEG 1.14 → fair for AI-SaaS.

4. **ROE (DuPont) = (NI/Revenue) × (Revenue/Assets) × (Assets/Equity)**
   - Threshold: sustainable ROE > 15%; watch leverage > 3x.
   - Example (Consulting firm): 15% × 1.3 × 1.6 = 31% ROE. Margin-led, safe.

5. **ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital ; compare vs WACC**
   - Threshold: ROIC − WACC > 500 bps = value creation.
   - Example: NOPAT ₹900 Cr / IC ₹5,000 Cr = 18%. WACC 11% → spread 700 bps → compounder.

6. **Rule of 40 = Revenue Growth % + FCF Margin %**
   - Threshold: > 40 healthy; > 60 elite.
   - Example (AI-SaaS): 45% growth + 5% FCF margin = 50 → clears bar.

7. **FCF Yield = FCF / Market Cap**
   - Threshold: > 5% attractive, < 2% priced for perfection.
   - Example: FCF ₹1,500 Cr / MCap ₹60,000 Cr = 2.5% → priced-in.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Read the 10-K / annual report before any broker note | Don't start from a pitch deck or a tweet thread |
| Decompose ROE via DuPont before celebrating it | Don't confuse leverage-driven ROE with quality |
| Compare multiples to sector peers and own history | Don't benchmark a SaaS firm against an IT services firm |
| Anchor SaaS stocks to Rule of 40, NRR, Burn Multiple | Don't value loss-making SaaS on P/E alone |
| Read MD&A for tone and segment-level caveats | Don't skip to the numbers — language leaks risk |
| Check insider buying/selling and capital allocation history | Don't ignore founder dilution via SBC grants |
| Stress-test growth assumptions with PEG and expectations-investing reverse DCF | Don't extrapolate 3-yr growth to 10 years |
| Track red flags: rising DSO, capitalised R&D, aggressive revenue recognition | Don't trust headline EPS when cash flow diverges |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Employer Stock Concentration (AI Product Lead)
Anika holds 35% of net worth in her employer's listed AI-SaaS RSUs. She runs DuPont: ROE 22% = 12% margin × 0.9 turnover × 2.0 leverage — margin-led. Moat check: strong switching cost (deep workflow integration) + IP (proprietary eval pipeline), no network effect. Rule-of-40 = 42 (38% growth + 4% FCF margin). Decision: trim from 35% to 15% of portfolio; diversify, but thesis remains intact.

### Scenario 2 — Pre-IPO AI-SaaS Competitor (Consulting Partner)
Rohan evaluates a competitor filing for listing. Peer multiples: competitor asks 18x sales vs sector median 9x. Unit economics: NRR 98%, Burn Multiple 2.4, Magic Number 0.35. MD&A red flags: "adjusted" revenue excludes refunds; capitalised R&D grew 60% YoY while headcount flat. Decision: skip the anchor IPO allocation; revisit at 8x sales post-lockup.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE: "Growth at Any Price"
Sameer buys a hyped GenAI stock at ₹10 L allocation, 20x forward sales, Rule-of-40 = 22, Burn Multiple rising from 2.1 to 3.8. MD&A mentions "strategic investment cycle"; DSO jumps from 60 to 95 days. 18 months later: multiple compresses to 6x, stock down 60%. Quantified loss ≈ ₹6 L on the ₹10 L position. Lesson: Rule-of-40 + Burn Multiple would have flagged it in 20 minutes.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Pull** latest 10-K / annual report and last 4 quarterly filings into a single folder.
2. **Compute** a DuPont decomposition, ROIC-vs-WACC spread, and FCF yield in a spreadsheet.
3. **Benchmark** P/E, EV/Sales, PEG, FCF yield against 5 sector peers and 5-year own history.
4. **Score** moat on the 5-source matrix (0-5 each) with one piece of evidence per source.
5. **Stress-test** SaaS names on Rule-of-40, NRR, Burn Multiple, Magic Number.
6. **Read** MD&A line-by-line; list every hedged phrase, accounting change, and segment miss.
7. **Write** a 1-page thesis: catalyst, risk, mispricing, exit trigger, and position size.
8. **Review** quarterly; kill the thesis if any of the 3 primary KPIs (ROIC, Rule-of-40, NRR) breach threshold for 2 consecutive quarters.

## 9. Quick-Recall Card
- Equity analysis = is the business a compounder, and is the price sane?
- Decompose ROE (DuPont); demand ROIC > WACC by > 500 bps.
- Anchor SaaS names to Rule-of-40, NRR, Burn Multiple — not P/E.
- Read MD&A; score moat on the 5-source matrix; track insider ownership.
- Kill thesis on KPI breach for two consecutive quarters.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: "Does this company earn ROIC above its cost of capital through a durable moat, and am I paying a price that leaves a margin of safety if growth disappoints by 25%?"
