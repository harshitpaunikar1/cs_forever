# Scenario Planning

## Overview

Scenario planning builds multiple plausible pictures of the future rather than relying on a single forecast. Each scenario describes a different combination of key uncertainties — like economic growth, regulation, or technology shifts — and traces their consequences for the business. The goal is not to predict which scenario will happen, but to prepare the organization to respond no matter which one does.

---

## Why It Matters

Single-point forecasts give a false sense of certainty. The real world delivers surprises — pandemics, trade wars, sudden technology disruptions — that no model can predict precisely. Scenario planning forces leadership to think through "what if" situations in advance, build flexible strategies, and avoid being paralyzed when conditions change. Companies that practice it consistently recover faster from shocks.

## Key Principles

- Identify the two or three biggest uncertainties that would change your strategy, not every possible variable
- Build three to four distinct scenarios that are internally consistent and genuinely different from each other
- For each scenario, map out the specific business implications: revenue, cost, supply chain, talent
- Use scenarios to stress-test existing plans rather than to replace traditional forecasting

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Scenario** | A coherent narrative describing one plausible future state of the world |
| **Key Uncertainty** | A major external factor whose outcome is unknown and would significantly affect the business |
| **Wind Tunneling** | Testing an existing strategy or plan against multiple scenarios to identify vulnerabilities |
| **Signpost** | An early indicator that one particular scenario is beginning to unfold |

## Use Case

An energy company develops three scenarios — rapid green transition, gradual shift, and fossil fuel rebound — and evaluates its five-year capital investment plan under each to ensure it remains profitable in at least two of the three futures.

## Scenario

> A European airline built four post-pandemic recovery scenarios ranging from "full recovery by 2023" to "permanently lower business travel." The most pessimistic scenario revealed that three underperforming routes would drain cash for years. Management pre-emptively cut those routes and redeployed aircraft to leisure destinations. When business travel remained flat, the airline was already positioned for the new demand mix and outperformed peers by 12% on operating margin.

## Examples

- A pharmaceutical firm models scenarios around patent expiration, generic competition, and regulatory approval timelines to decide how much to invest in a new drug
- A city government creates flood, drought, and stable-climate scenarios to prioritize infrastructure spending over the next 20 years

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Scenario Planning Core Frame
**Purpose:** Turn uncertainty into a small set of actionable futures.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Uncertainty Drivers
        |
        v
  [Key uncertainty 1]    [Key uncertainty 2]
        \                 /
         \               /
          v             v
        Scenario A   Scenario B   Scenario C
              \        |        /
               \       |       /
                v      v      v
           Strategy stress-test
                |
                v
          Triggered actions
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: Key uncertainty 1, the first driver that could reshape revenue, cost, or growth.
Component 2: Key uncertainty 2, the second driver that creates a different strategic branch.
Component 3: Scenario set, the small number of distinct futures built from those drivers.
Component 4: Stress-test, the exercise that shows which plan survives across futures.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product team planning an AI copilot uses model quality and enterprise regulation as the two uncertainties. One scenario assumes fast model improvement and low regulation; another assumes slow model gains and strict compliance; a third assumes mixed progress. The decision is whether to build a broad platform, a regulated vertical version, or a wait-and-see roadmap.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When leadership says "the forecast is too brittle" or "we need a plan that works even if the market shifts."

### Wind Tunneling
**Purpose:** Test one plan against several futures to find weak points.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Plan -> Scenario 1 -> pass/fail
     -> Scenario 2 -> pass/fail
     -> Scenario 3 -> pass/fail
     -> Adjust plan until failures are acceptable
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: The plan or initiative being tested.
Component 2: The scenarios acting as pressure tests.
Component 3: The failure points that show where the plan is brittle.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A consulting team stress-tests a cloud migration plan against budget cuts, vendor delays, and security incidents. If the migration only works in the best case, the team redesigns it into smaller waves with rollback options.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When a strategy looks good on slides but may fail in execution.

## 4. Formulas
No explicit formula appears in the source. A practical planning proxy is:

Formula: Scenario resilience score = number of scenarios in which the plan remains viable / total scenarios tested [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Variables:
Viable = the plan still meets minimum business goals.
Total scenarios tested = the number of distinct futures in the set.
Why this formula exists: It answers whether a strategy is robust or only works in one future.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 0.5 -> fragile plan -> redesign the strategy
Value 0.5-0.8 -> partially robust -> add contingency triggers
Value > 0.8 -> resilient plan -> proceed with monitoring
Worked example with numbers: An IT vendor tests a launch plan across 4 scenarios and the plan works in 3. Resilience score = 3/4 = 0.75 [verified from model knowledge, not source]. That is good but not bulletproof, so the launch should include a fallback pricing and staffing plan.

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Build one "most likely" future and call it planning | Build 3-4 distinct plausible scenarios |
| Treat every variable as equally important | Focus on the 2-3 key uncertainties that change strategy |
| Let scenarios become generic stories | Make them internally consistent and decision-relevant |
| Replace forecasting entirely | Use scenarios to stress-test the forecast and plan |
| Wait for a crisis to decide triggers | Define signposts and responses in advance |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)
Scenario 1: AI product launch under regulation uncertainty
Situation: A product leader is launching an AI assistant for customer support. One scenario assumes open regulatory conditions and rapid adoption; another assumes stricter controls and slower enterprise approval.
Applicable framework/metric: Scenario planning + signposts.
Analysis: If approval cycle time exceeds 60 days, shift to a narrower B2B pilot. If it stays under 30 days, expand the rollout. If it sits between 30 and 60 days, keep the launch limited to low-risk use cases.
Decision rule: "If metric > 60 days, do A. If between 30 and 60 days, do B. If below 30 days, do C."
Action: Prepare three launch packages, one legal review checklist, and a trigger dashboard for approvals and customer objections.

Scenario 2: Cloud migration under demand volatility
Situation: An IT consulting team is planning a migration of analytics workloads to the cloud. Demand could spike, stay flat, or contract if the client cuts budget.
Applicable framework/metric: Wind tunneling.
Analysis: Test the migration plan against three futures: 20% demand growth, flat demand, and 15% demand decline. If the plan fails under the decline case, split the migration into smaller waves.
Decision rule: "If projected utilization stays above 70%, proceed with the full wave. If it is 50%-70%, phase it. If it drops below 50%, freeze the next wave."
Action: Build a phased roadmap, rollback plan, and budget gate for each release wave.

Scenario 3: Consulting revenue planning with two key uncertainties
Situation: A consulting firm wants to hire two new managers but faces uncertainty on client spend and deal conversion.
Applicable framework/metric: Key uncertainty mapping.
Analysis: If monthly pipeline conversion falls below 18%, freeze hiring. If conversion is 18%-25%, backfill only critical roles. If it rises above 25%, approve the hire and add delivery capacity.
Decision rule: "If metric > 25%, do A. If between 18% and 25%, do B. If below 18%, do C."
Action: Track conversion weekly, keep a hiring trigger table, and prewrite staffing scenarios for each revenue path.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Identify the two or three uncertainties that could materially change the plan.
2. Write three to four scenarios that are plausible, distinct, and internally consistent.
3. Map the impact of each scenario on revenue, cost, talent, and delivery capacity.
4. Test the current plan against every scenario and note where it breaks.
5. Define signposts, thresholds, and owner actions for each scenario.
6. Build a one-page decision memo showing what changes now versus later.
7. Review the scenario set on a fixed cadence and update triggers when assumptions shift.
