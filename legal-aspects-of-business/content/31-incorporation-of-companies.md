## 1. Topic Snapshot
Incorporation is the legal process of registering a company using required documents.  
It matters for IT, AI, product, and consulting startups because incorporation creates the separate legal person that can open bank accounts, sign contracts, and raise capital.  
The decision is whether the founders have completed the legal and operational steps needed to move from a group of people to a company.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Incorporation Readiness Checklist
**Purpose:** Test whether the company is ready to become a functioning legal entity, not just a founder idea.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
name approval -> docs filed -> MOA/AOA ready -> registration granted -> compliance calendar live
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: Identity step - name approval and basic entity identity.
Component 2: Document step - required incorporation filings.
Component 3: Governance step - MOA and AOA define purpose and rules.
Component 4: Operating step - post-incorporation compliance obligations.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: An AI startup wants to sign an enterprise pilot, open a corporate bank account, and raise a seed round. The founders should not treat the business as “incorporated” until the name is approved, the registration is filed and accepted, and the compliance calendar is live.

When to pull this out in a meeting: When someone says, “We can just start operating now and incorporate later.”

### Legal-Personality Handoff
**Purpose:** Identify the moment responsibilities move from the founders to the company.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
FOUNDERS  --->  INCORPORATED COMPANY  --->  BANK / CONTRACT / INVESTOR
   |                    |
   |                    +-- separate legal person
   +-- personal risk before entity exists
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: Pre-incorporation period - founders act personally.
Component 2: Post-incorporation period - company acts in its own name.
Component 3: External interfaces - banking, contracts, and fundraising.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A consulting team drafts a services agreement before incorporation. Once the company is formed, the contract should be signed in the company name, not by the founders individually, so liability and ownership are clear.

When to pull this out in a meeting: When founders are about to sign, hire, or invoice before the entity is legally live.

## 4. Formulas
Formula: `Incorporation readiness score = name approval + required documents filed + MOA/AOA completed + compliance plan live` [verified from model knowledge, not source]

Variables:
`name approval` = 1 if approved, else 0  
`required documents filed` = 1 if filings are complete, else 0  
`MOA/AOA completed` = 1 if governance docs are ready, else 0  
`compliance plan live` = 1 if post-incorporation obligations are tracked, else 0

Why this formula exists: It answers the question, “Can we safely operate as a company now?”

How to interpret the output:
Value 0–1 → not ready → do not contract as a company
Value 2–3 → partially ready → finish filings before launch
Value 4 → fully ready → incorporate and start operating

Worked example with numbers: If name approval is done, filings are done, MOA/AOA are drafted, and the compliance plan is not yet live, the score is 3. The founders should finish the compliance setup before relying on the entity for important commitments.

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Sign major contracts in the founders’ names forever. | Move contracts into the company name after incorporation. |
| Treat registration as a formality you can delay indefinitely. | Complete incorporation before bank, investor, and customer onboarding. |
| Ignore MOA/AOA drafting until after fundraising. | Define objectives and internal rules at formation. |
| Assume the company exists before approval is granted. | Confirm incorporation status before claiming separate legal personhood. |
| Forget compliance after the company is formed. | Put post-incorporation deadlines and filings on a calendar. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)
Scenario 1: Startup formation before enterprise deal
Situation: A product startup is finalizing its first enterprise AI contract and wants to invoice under a company name. The founders have applied for incorporation but have not received the approval yet.
Applicable framework/metric: Incorporation Readiness Checklist.
Analysis: Without completed registration, the entity may not yet exist as the separate legal person needed to contract cleanly. The deal should not be signed as if the company is already live.
Decision rule: If incorporation is approved, sign as the company. If filings are pending, delay. If the deal cannot wait, use legally reviewed interim arrangements.
Action: Finish the filing, secure CIN, and only then issue the company invoice and contract.

Scenario 2: Consulting firm choosing structure
Situation: A consulting team with a small AI practice is deciding between remaining as individuals or incorporating as a company to attract investors and formalize governance.
Applicable framework/metric: Legal-Personality Handoff.
Analysis: Incorporation is valuable because it creates a separate legal person that can hold bank accounts and contracts. It also introduces ongoing compliance that must be managed.
Decision rule: If investors and contracting need a company, incorporate. If the team cannot support compliance, delay. If liability concerns are high, prioritize incorporation.
Action: Draft MOA/AOA, appoint filing responsibility, and set a compliance owner before launch.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Approve the company name before signing customer or investor documents.
2. File the incorporation documents and track approval status daily.
3. Draft MOA and AOA so the company’s purpose and rules are clear.
4. Open the bank account only after the entity is legally recognized.
5. Move all new contracts into the company name once incorporation is complete.
6. Create a post-incorporation compliance calendar for filings and renewals.
7. Assign one owner for corporate secretarial follow-through.
