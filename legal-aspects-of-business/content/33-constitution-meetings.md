# 33. Constitution of Company & Meetings

## Overview

A company’s constitution (MOA/AOA) sets its purpose and internal rules. Meetings are how shareholders and directors make decisions.

---

## Why It Matters

Proper procedure avoids invalid decisions and protects stakeholder rights.


## Key Principles

- MOA/AOA must be followed
- Board meetings for management decisions
- Shareholder meetings for major approvals
- Proper notice, quorum, and minutes


## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Quorum** | Minimum members needed |
| **Resolution** | Formal decision |
| **Minutes** | Official meeting record |


## Use Case

Approving new shares or appointing directors via resolutions.


## Scenario

> Company passes a resolution to alter articles to allow issue of preference shares.


## Examples

- AGM to approve accounts and dividends.
- Board meeting to approve a major contract.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Meeting Validity Matrix
**Purpose:** Decide whether a board or shareholder decision is procedurally safe.
[verified from model knowledge, not source] This matrix is a control overlay for the source's notice, quorum, and minutes requirements.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
                      Notice and minutes
                   Weak                         Strong
Quorum
Missing            Invalid / high challenge risk  High challenge risk; fix process first
Present            Fixable but incomplete          Validity likely; keep records clean
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: Notice means the meeting was properly called and participants had time to prepare.
Component 2: Quorum means enough members were present to transact business.
Component 3: Minutes mean the decision was recorded accurately and stored.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: [verified from model knowledge, not source] A product team wants board approval for a major AI vendor contract. If the directors were not properly notified, the meeting has quorum issues, or the minutes are missing, the approval can be challenged later. If notice, quorum, and minutes are all clean, the contract approval is much easier to defend.
When to pull this out in a meeting: Use it when someone asks whether a resolution was "basically fine" even though the process was rushed.

## 4. Formulas

[verified from model knowledge, not source] Formula: `Meeting Validity Score = notice + quorum + minutes + authority`
Variables:
notice = 0/1 for proper notice and delivery proof
quorum = 0/1 for required attendance
minutes = 0/1 for recorded and approved minutes
authority = 0/1 for whether the meeting type can legally approve the item
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the decision is procedurally valid or at risk of challenge.
How to interpret the output:
0 or 1 -> invalid / challenge risk -> do not rely on it
2 -> weak process -> repair before using
3 -> mostly valid -> document and review
4 -> strong validity -> proceed and archive
Worked example with numbers: [verified from model knowledge, not source] A board meeting has proper notice, quorum, and minutes, but the item actually needs shareholder approval. The score is 3, so the process is incomplete and the team should re-run it through the correct body.

## 5. Do vs Don't

[verified from model knowledge, not source] These controls turn constitutional rules into working governance.

| Don't | Do |
|---------|-------|
| Treat the MOA/AOA as paperwork only | Use them as the actual operating rulebook |
| Call a meeting without proper notice | Send notice and keep delivery proof |
| Let a tiny group make major decisions | Check quorum before voting |
| Skip minutes because "everyone knows what happened" | Record, approve, and archive the decision |
| Use a board meeting for a shareholder-only approval | Route decisions to the correct body |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

[verified from model knowledge, not source] The scenarios below translate procedure into decision quality.

Scenario 1: Approving a major AI vendor contract
Situation: A company wants to approve a long-term AI tooling contract in a board meeting. The team is short on time and has only half the directors in the room.
Applicable framework/metric: Meeting Validity Score.
Analysis: notice = 1, quorum = 0, minutes = 1, authority = 1, so the score is 3.
Decision rule: If the score is 4, proceed. If it is 3, repair the process. If it is 0 to 2, do not rely on the decision.
Action: Re-call the meeting or get the right quorum before signing.

Scenario 2: Issuing preference shares
Situation: A product company wants to issue preference shares to an investor, but the articles do not clearly permit the new rights.
Applicable framework/metric: Authority and constitution fit.
Analysis: If the AOA does not authorize the issue structure, procedural validity is weak.
Decision rule: If the constitution fits, proceed. If it needs amendment, amend first. If the decision was already taken without authority, re-run it properly.
Action: Update the articles and then pass the relevant resolution.

Scenario 3: AGM for accounts and dividends
Situation: A consulting company wants to approve accounts and declare dividends at the AGM, but the minutes from the prior meeting were never signed.
Applicable framework/metric: Meeting Validity Score.
Analysis: notice = 1, quorum = 1, minutes = 0, authority = 1, so the score is 3.
Decision rule: If the score is 4, proceed. If it is 3, finalize the missing records. If it is below 3, stop and fix the process.
Action: Approve the minutes, close the record gap, and file the AGM outputs properly.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

[verified from model knowledge, not source] This playbook makes the constitution and meeting rules usable in a live business.

1. Keep current versions of the MOA and AOA in one governance folder.
2. Build a notice template with date, time, agenda, and attendance details.
3. Check quorum before every board or shareholder meeting starts.
4. Use a resolution tracker to map each decision to the right meeting body.
5. Draft minutes within one business day and approve them quickly.
6. Tie major approvals like new shares or director appointments to the right constitutional clause.
7. Audit meeting records quarterly for missing notices, quorum failures, or unsigned minutes.
