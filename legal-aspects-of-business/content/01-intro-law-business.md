# 1. Introduction to Law and Business

## Overview

Law is a set of rules made by the government to keep things fair and safe. Business law explains how businesses should deal with customers, workers, other companies, and the public.

---

## Why It Matters

Business decisions have legal consequences. Knowing the basics helps avoid fines, disputes, and business failure.


## Key Principles

- Law sets rights and duties
- Businesses must follow rules (compliance)
- Courts and authorities enforce law
- Written records reduce confusion


## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Law** | Rules enforced by the state |
| **Compliance** | Following legal requirements |
| **Liability** | Legal responsibility for harm/loss |
| **Regulation** | Rules made for specific industries |


## Use Case

A company checks legal requirements before launching a product.


## Scenario

> A startup launches a food item without proper labeling and faces a complaint. Legal checks could have prevented it.


## Examples

- Company registers under law to operate legally.
- Business issues invoices and pays taxes as required.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Legal-Ready Launch Stack
**Purpose:** Decide whether a business can launch now or needs more legal work first.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
1. Registration
   -> Is the business legally allowed to operate?
2. Compliance
   -> Are required rules, taxes, and disclosures satisfied?
3. Records
   -> Can you prove what you did?
4. Enforcement watch
   -> Are there complaints, regulators, or court risks?
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Registration: the entity or product is properly set up to exist in the market.
Compliance: the rules are being followed before launch.
Records: invoices, approvals, and logs can defend the decision later.
Enforcement watch: complaints or inspections are monitored after launch.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A SaaS company wants to launch a new AI workflow product. The stack tells the team to confirm entity registration, review disclosure rules, archive approval records, and set a complaint-response process before go-live.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When a launch is being rushed and legal readiness is unclear.

## 4. Formulas

Formula: Compliance coverage ratio = documented requirements / applicable requirements [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Variables:
documented requirements = rules the team has already mapped and evidence-backed
applicable requirements = total rules that apply to the business activity
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the team has enough legal coverage to launch responsibly.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 0.70 -> weak coverage -> delay launch
Value 0.70-0.90 -> workable coverage -> fix gaps before launch
Value > 0.90 -> strong coverage -> launch with monitoring
Worked example with numbers: A product team maps 18 of 20 required items for a new market launch. Coverage = 0.90. That is close to strong, but the remaining two items should be closed before launch day.

Formula: Incident risk score = exposure x control gap x enforcement intensity [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Variables:
exposure = how much the business touches customers, workers, or the public
control gap = how many checks are missing
enforcement intensity = how likely fines, lawsuits, or regulator action are
Why this formula exists: It helps decide which launch or contract needs the most legal attention first.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 0.25 -> low risk -> monitor
Value 0.25-0.50 -> medium risk -> tighten controls
Value > 0.50 -> high risk -> pause and fix
Worked example with numbers: A consulting-led marketplace has exposure 0.8, control gap 0.6, and enforcement intensity 0.7. The score is 0.336. That is medium risk, so the team should add documentation and legal review before scaling.

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Launch a product before checking legal requirements. | Build a launch checklist that includes compliance and records. |
| Treat legal work as only a lawyer's problem. | Make legal readiness part of product, finance, and operations. |
| Rely on memory or Slack messages for key decisions. | Keep written records, approvals, and invoices. |
| Ignore complaints until they become public. | Set a response path for customer, regulator, and court issues. |
| Assume registration and taxes can wait until after growth. | Get entity setup and reporting right before the business scales. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

Scenario 1: AI startup launching a paid feature
Situation: An AI startup wants to ship a paid workflow assistant and start invoicing enterprise customers next week. The product is ready, but the company has not finished its registration and tax checklist.
Applicable framework/metric: Compliance coverage ratio.
Analysis: The team has 16 of 20 required items documented, so coverage is 0.80. That is workable but not launch-safe until the missing four items are closed.
Decision rule: If coverage is below 0.70, delay launch. If 0.70-0.90, close gaps and launch only when the highest-risk items are done. If above 0.90, proceed and monitor.
Action: Finish registration, check invoicing requirements, archive approvals, and launch only after the checklist is complete.

Scenario 2: Product manager handling a labeling complaint
Situation: A consumer product team ships a new device with a packaging label that a customer says is misleading. The complaint is small, but the company wants to avoid a regulator escalation.
Applicable framework/metric: Incident risk score.
Analysis: Exposure is 0.7 because the product goes to the public, the control gap is 0.4 because the label review was incomplete, and enforcement intensity is 0.5 because the category is moderately regulated. The score is 0.14.
Decision rule: If the score is below 0.25, monitor and fix quickly. If 0.25-0.50, tighten controls. If above 0.50, pause launches until the legal review is complete.
Action: Reissue the label, log the correction, and add a final sign-off step before the next release.

Scenario 3: Consulting team advising a new market entry
Situation: A consulting team is helping a fintech client enter a new country. The client wants speed, but the legal team needs proof that the entity, invoices, and local filings are all correct.
Applicable framework/metric: Legal-ready launch stack.
Analysis: The entity is registered, but the team has no written records for one critical service flow and no complaint-handling process. That means the launch stack is incomplete.
Decision rule: If registration, compliance, records, and enforcement watch are all ready, launch. If any layer is missing, fix that layer before scaling.
Action: Add a record-keeping policy, build a complaint workflow, and delay the market launch until sign-off is complete.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Build a launch checklist that covers registration, compliance, records, and complaint handling.
2. Assign a legal owner for each product or service release.
3. Store approvals, invoices, contracts, and policies in one searchable folder.
4. Add a pre-launch review for labeling, taxes, and consumer disclosures.
5. Create a regulator and customer complaint workflow with response deadlines.
6. Review the checklist after every launch and update it when rules change.
7. Escalate high-risk issues to counsel before revenue starts flowing.
