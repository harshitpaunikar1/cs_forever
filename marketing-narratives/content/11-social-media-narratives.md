# Social Media Narratives

## Overview

Social media narratives are ongoing, episodic stories a brand tells across platforms like Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, and YouTube. They are built for feeds: short, visual, responsive to comments, and designed to be shared.

---

## Why It Matters

Social is where most people now discover brands, and algorithms reward content that keeps viewers engaged. A strong serialized narrative outperforms random posts on reach, follower growth, and direct sales. It also builds a community that markets for you.

## Key Principles

- Treat your account as a show with recurring characters, segments, and running jokes.
- Post on a predictable rhythm so followers know when to return.
- Respond to comments in-character to turn viewers into a community.
- Adapt the same story to each platform's native format (Reel, Short, carousel, thread).
- Use analytics to double down on episodes that drive saves and shares.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Content Series** | A repeating themed format (e.g., "Monday Motivation") viewers can anticipate. |
| **Creator Persona** | A consistent voice or character that represents the brand on social. |
| **Community Management** | Ongoing replies, DMs, and engagement with followers. |
| **Native Format** | Platform-specific content types like Reels, Stories, or TikToks. |
| **Viral Loop** | A content design where each share creates more shares. |

## Use Case

A local bookstore builds a weekly Instagram "Staff Picks" series where three employees each recommend one book with a 30-second personal story, growing following from 800 to 32,000 in a year.

## Scenario

> A B2B accounting software brand had a LinkedIn page that posted product updates to silence. They launched a weekly narrative series: "Friday CFO Confessions" — short videos of real finance leaders sharing one mistake they made. Followers grew 14x in nine months, and inbound demo requests from LinkedIn rose from 6 to 210 per month.

## Examples

- Wendy's Twitter persona tells an ongoing narrative of cheeky rivalry that draws millions of organic impressions weekly.
- Zomato's social narrative blends meme-native humour with hyperlocal stories about Indian food culture.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Serialized Story Engine
**Purpose:** Turn an account into a repeatable show instead of a random posting stream.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Recurring character -> recurring segment -> recurring rhythm -> audience habit
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Recurring character: the creator persona or recognizable voice.
Recurring segment: the repeated content format.
Recurring rhythm: the posting cadence.
Audience habit: the reason followers come back.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product team runs "Build Log Friday," where the PM, designer, and engineer each explain one shipping lesson in a short video. Followers know what to expect, and the series becomes a reliable source of saves and repeat views.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When social posts are isolated and there is no repeatable format.

### Native Format Fit
**Purpose:** Adapt the same story to the platform's language.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Story idea -> Reel / Short / carousel / thread -> platform behavior -> distribution
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Story idea: the core message.
Native format: the packaging that fits the platform.
Platform behavior: how people consume and share there.
Distribution: the resulting reach and engagement.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A consulting firm turns one case study into a LinkedIn carousel, a YouTube short, and a thread. The same narrative is repackaged so each platform can reward it differently.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When a creative asset is being copied between platforms without adaptation.

### Community Flywheel
**Purpose:** Use comments and DMs to turn viewers into participants.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
content -> comments -> response -> loyalty -> more sharing
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Content: the original narrative episode.
Comments: the community reaction.
Response: how the brand participates.
Loyalty and sharing: the compounding effect.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A SaaS founder replies in-character to technical questions under a launch video, then turns the best question into the next post. The comment thread becomes input for the next story beat.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When engagement exists but is not being converted into community momentum.

## 4. Formulas

Formula: Engagement rate = interactions / impressions x 100
Variables:
Interactions = likes, comments, shares, saves, or other defined actions.
Impressions = total views or exposures.
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the content is actually compelling.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 1 -> weak resonance -> change the hook or format.
Value 1-3 -> acceptable -> iterate.
Value > 3 -> strong signal -> scale the series.
Worked example with numbers: A LinkedIn video gets 24,000 impressions and 720 interactions. Engagement rate = 3.0%, which is a strong sign the narrative is landing.

Formula: Follower growth rate = ((new followers - starting followers) / starting followers) x 100
Variables:
New followers = followers at the end of the period.
Starting followers = followers at the beginning of the period.
Why this formula exists: It shows whether the narrative is compounding audience.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 5 -> slow growth -> refine content series.
Value 5-20 -> healthy growth -> maintain cadence.
Value > 20 -> breakout growth -> invest in distribution.
Worked example with numbers: An AI product account grows from 4,000 to 5,200 followers in a quarter. Follower growth rate = 30%, which supports expanding the series.

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Post random updates with no recurring format. | Run a content series people can anticipate. |
| Copy the same post across every platform. | Repackage the story in the native format for each platform. |
| Ignore comments until the campaign ends. | Use community management to extend the story in real time. |
| Measure success only by likes. | Track saves, shares, follower growth, and direct sales. |
| Treat the brand as faceless. | Use a creator persona that makes the narrative recognizable. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)
Scenario 1: Product-led social series
Situation: A B2B analytics product launches a weekly "data mistake of the week" series on LinkedIn and YouTube Shorts. The team wants to know whether the series is building a durable audience or just one-off reach.
Applicable framework/metric: Serialized Story Engine and follower growth rate.
Analysis: Views are stable, saves rise from 180 to 290 per month, and followers grow from 6,000 to 7,800 over a quarter. That is 30% follower growth, which indicates the series is compounding.
Decision rule: If follower growth > 20%, expand the series. If between 5% and 20%, tune the hook. If below 5%, change the format.
Action: Keep the weekly cadence, add a recurring host, and build a content backlog.

Scenario 2: AI launch distribution
Situation: An AI copilot launch gets strong reach on X, but the product team is unsure whether the content is being shared because it is useful or just because the team posts often.
Applicable framework/metric: Native Format Fit and engagement rate.
Analysis: A carousel version gets 48,000 impressions and 2,160 interactions for a 4.5% engagement rate, while the copied thread version only gets 1.2%. The native format clearly performs better.
Decision rule: If engagement rate > 3%, scale the format. If between 1% and 3%, test a stronger hook. If below 1%, stop reposting the same asset.
Action: Rebuild the launch content as a native carousel and a short demo clip instead of a copy-pasted thread.

Scenario 3: Consulting community loop
Situation: A consulting firm's LinkedIn posts get comments but not many referrals or inbound requests. The team wants to turn engagement into an actual flywheel instead of treating comments as noise.
Applicable framework/metric: Community Flywheel and share rate.
Analysis: One post gets 900 impressions, 45 comments, 60 shares, and 12 demo inquiries. Share rate is 6.7%, which suggests the narrative is useful enough to travel.
Decision rule: If share rate > 5%, build a follow-up episode. If between 2% and 5%, sharpen the story. If below 2%, rewrite the hook.
Action: Reply to the best comments in-character, turn the most common objection into the next episode, and link it to a demo CTA.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Choose one content series that can run for at least eight episodes.
2. Assign a creator persona so the brand sounds consistent.
3. Map each episode to the native format on each platform.
4. Set a posting rhythm that followers can recognize.
5. Build a community management workflow for comments and DMs.
6. Track reach, saves, shares, engagement rate, and follower growth weekly.
7. Turn the best-performing episode into the next one so the narrative compounds.
