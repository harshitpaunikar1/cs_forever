
# Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC)

## Overview

IMC means using all communication tools (ads, social media, PR, sales) in a consistent message.

## Why It Matters

If messages differ across platforms, customers get confused. IMC builds a strong, clear brand image.

## Key Principles

- Identify target audience
- Set communication objectives
- Choose message and channels
- Measure results and improve

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **IMC** | consistent communication across channels |
| **Reach** | how many people see your message |
| **Frequency** | how often they see it |
| **Conversion** | turning viewers into buyers |

## Use Case

A new phone launch uses the same theme across TV, YouTube, influencers, and stores.

## Scenario

> A brand says “premium” in ads but offers cheap-looking packaging—IMC fixes the mismatch.

## Examples

- Festival campaign: same offer on posters, Instagram, and in-store banners.
- College event sponsorship + social media + email follow-ups.

# Mass Communications (Advertising, Promotions, Events, PR)

## Overview

Mass communication is when a brand talks to many people at once using ads, promotions, events, and PR.

## Why It Matters

It builds awareness quickly and can boost short-term sales (promotions) and long-term image (PR).

## Key Principles

- Ads create awareness and brand meaning
- Promotions push quick purchase (discounts, coupons)
- Events create experience and engagement
- PR builds credibility

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Advertising** | paid mass message |
| **Sales promotion** | short-term incentive to buy |
| **Public relations (PR)** | managing brand reputation and media |
| **Event marketing** | brand presence in experiences |

## Use Case

A beverage brand sponsors a music festival and runs a limited-time offer.

## Scenario

> A company faces negative rumors. PR responds with facts, quality proof, and transparent communication.

## Examples

- Buy-1-get-1 deal for one weekend (promotion).
- Press release + interviews after launching a CSR initiative (PR).

# Personal Communications (Direct, Digital, WOM, Personal Selling)

## Overview

Personal communication is more direct: emails, WhatsApp messages, sales calls, influencer chatter, and word-of-mouth.

## Why It Matters

Personal communication can be more convincing and targeted than mass advertising—especially for costly or complex products.

## Key Principles

- Use direct marketing for personalized offers
- Encourage word-of-mouth through good experiences
- Personal selling follows steps (approach → present → handle objections → close)

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Direct marketing** | email/SMS/catalog targeting |
| **WOM** | word-of-mouth recommendations |
| **Lead** | potential customer |
| **Objection handling** | answering customer concerns |

## Use Case

A laptop seller calls leads and explains specs based on their needs.

## Scenario

> A happy customer posts a review; friends trust it more than ads and buy.

## Examples

- Email to past buyers: “20% off on accessories.”
- Sales rep visits a business client with a demo and proposal.

# New Product Development (NPD) + Adoption Process

## Overview

NPD is how companies create and launch new products—from idea to market. Adoption explains how customers start using new products.

## Why It Matters

Most new products fail without testing and planning. A proper process reduces risk and increases chances of success.

## Key Principles

- Generate many ideas, screen the best
- Test concept with customers
- Develop product and test in small market
- Launch with clear targeting and promotion

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Idea screening** | selecting best ideas |
| **Concept testing** | checking customer reaction to idea |
| **Market testing** | trial launch in limited area |
| **Adoption** | customer starts using new product |

## Use Case

A snack brand tests flavors with small groups, then launches the top winner.

## Scenario

> A company launches smart home devices. Early adopters buy first; later, mass market joins after reviews and price drops.

## Examples

- Shampoo brand tests packaging designs and chooses the most preferred.
- App company runs beta version before full release.

# Global Marketing (Entry, Adaptation, Global Strategy)

## Overview

Global marketing is selling in other countries by choosing where to enter, how to enter, and how much to adapt.

## Why It Matters

Different countries have different laws, culture, income, and competition. Without adaptation, global expansion can fail.

## Key Principles

- Select markets carefully (size, growth, risk)
- Choose entry mode (export, licensing, joint venture, direct investment)
- Adapt product, price, and promotion when needed
- Manage country-of-origin effects

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Exporting** | selling abroad from home country |
| **Licensing** | allowing a local firm to use your brand/tech |
| **Joint venture** | partnering with local firm |
| **Standardization vs adaptation** | same marketing vs customized |

## Use Case

A food brand adapts taste/spice level for different countries.

## Scenario

> A fashion brand enters a new country via local partner first, then opens its own stores after learning the market.

## Examples

- Fast-food chain changes menu to match local preferences.
- Electronics brand keeps product same but changes warranty and pricing.

# Managing a Holistic Marketing Organization + Control + CSR

## Overview

This is about organizing marketing inside the company, measuring performance, and doing marketing responsibly.

## Why It Matters

Without control, marketing becomes wasteful. Responsible marketing builds long-term trust and avoids legal/reputation problems.

## Key Principles

- Marketing must coordinate with other departments
- Use metrics and dashboards to track results
- Control plans: annual plan control, profitability control, strategic control
- CSR and ethical marketing improve trust

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Marketing control** | checking results and correcting actions |
| **Marketing metrics** | numbers like sales, conversion, retention |
| **Dashboard** | visual report of key metrics |
| **CSR** | corporate social responsibility |

## Use Case

A company tracks campaign ROI weekly and stops poor-performing ads quickly.

## Scenario

> A brand is accused of misleading claims. It changes communication, improves transparency, and trains teams for ethical marketing.

## Examples

- Brand runs eco-friendly packaging and shares proof (CSR + trust).
- Company measures customer retention monthly and improves service when it drops.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Framework 1: IMC Consistency Audit
**Purpose:** Ensure all communications convey same message.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Touchpoint          │ Message consistency?   │ Example
 ─────────────────── ┼────────────────────── ─┼─────────────────
 TV / video ads       │ Core theme             │ "Private-first AI"
 Social posts         │ Same theme adapted    │ Developer humour + privacy hook
 Website              │ Theme reinforced      │ Privacy-first hero copy
 Sales deck           │ Same POD               │ Privacy in deck
 Customer emails       │ Consistent language    │ Updates on privacy features
 Customer service     │ Staff trained           │ Privacy-focused support
 Packaging / docs     │ Same tone              │ Privacy policy prominent
 Events                │ Theme delivered        │ Privacy-focused booth

 Audit: score each touchpoint 1-5 on message consistency.
 Target: all ≥ 4.
```

Components:
- 8+ touchpoints
- Consistency audit
- Internal marketing (staff) often weakest

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** AI SaaS IMC audit: ads (5), social (4), website (5), sales deck (3 - mixed messaging), email (4), support (2 - staff untrained on positioning). Gaps: sales deck, support. Fix: sales enablement + support training on positioning.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Campaign launches; annual marketing reviews.

---

### Framework 2: Media Mix Decision (Earned/Owned/Paid)
**Purpose:** Balance 3 media types.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Type           │ Definition                   │ Strength               │ Caveat
 ────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼────────────────────── ─┼─────────────────
 PAID           │ Ads you buy                  │ Controlled reach + speed│ Declining effectiveness
                │ (Google, Meta, TV, sponsor) │ Immediate scale         │ Expensive
 OWNED          │ Assets you control           │ Persistent + compounding│ Takes time
                │ (website, blog, email)       │ Relationship-building  │ Requires investment
 EARNED         │ Third-party coverage        │ Most trusted + credible│ Less controllable
                │ (PR, reviews, WOM, press)  │                         │

 Best campaigns combine all 3:
   Paid → drive traffic
   Owned → convert + nurture
   Earned → amplify trust
```

Components:
- 3 media types; use all
- Each has role in funnel

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** AI product launch:
- Paid: LinkedIn ads to drive trials ($100k)
- Owned: blog + email nurture ($30k content)
- Earned: developer advocacy + PR ($50k team) → TechCrunch coverage
- Budget: 50% Paid / 30% Owned / 20% Earned

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Media mix allocation; campaign planning.

---

### Framework 3: Marketing Control Framework (Kotler's 4)
**Purpose:** Different types of control for different issues.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Type               │ What it measures                 │ Frequency
 ──────────────── ─┼───────────────────────────────── ─┼───────────
 ANNUAL PLAN       │ Did we hit annual targets?       │ Monthly
                   │ (sales, share, profit)           │
 PROFITABILITY     │ Which products/markets/channels  │ Quarterly
                   │ are profitable?                   │
 EFFICIENCY        │ Are resources used well?         │ Quarterly
                   │ (S&M ROI, sales productivity)    │
 STRATEGIC         │ Is our marketing strategy still  │ Annual
                   │ right? (marketing audit)         │

 All 4 needed; missing one creates blind spots.
```

Components:
- 4 types; different horizons
- Strategic is often missed (firefighting on annual plan)

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** SaaS marketing controls:
- Annual: ARR on target → Green
- Profitability: enterprise segment 40% margin; SMB 8% → reallocate
- Efficiency: CAC rising 20% YoY → investigate
- Strategic: competitor pivoting to open-source; we need positioning update

Without strategic control, the competitor shift is missed.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** CMO reviews; annual planning.

## 4. Formulas

### Formula 1: Reach × Frequency = GRP
**Formula:** `GRP = Reach (%) × Frequency`

**Worked example:** Campaign reaches 50% of target audience 3 times average → GRP = 150.
Industry benchmark: 200 GRP for new launch; 100 for maintenance.

**Data source:** Nielsen, digital-ad platforms.

---

### Formula 2: Marketing ROI
**Formula:** `Marketing ROI = (Revenue attributable to marketing − Marketing cost) / Marketing cost`

**Worked example:** Campaign spend $500k; attributable revenue $2M (via attribution model). ROI = (2000 − 500)/500 = **3.0** → $3 revenue per $1 spent.

**Data source:** Attribution system + finance.

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: AI Product Launch with IMC
**Situation:** New AI product launching across channels.

**Applicable framework/metric:** IMC Consistency + Media Mix.

**Analysis:**
- Core theme: "private-first AI"
- Paid: search + LinkedIn $200k
- Owned: content hub + webinars $80k
- Earned: TechCrunch pitch + developer community $50k
- IMC audit: all touchpoints reinforce theme

**Decision rule:** No launch without IMC check + balanced media mix.

**Action (Monday morning):** IMC audit template; publish media mix budget allocation.

---

### Scenario 2: Consulting Advising on Marketing Org Structure
**Situation:** Enterprise client: marketing reports revenue but can't explain ROI per activity.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Kotler Control Framework.

**Analysis:**
- Annual Plan: tracked (revenue)
- Profitability: not tracked by segment (misallocated $5M/year)
- Efficiency: no CAC by channel
- Strategic: no annual marketing audit

**Decision rule:** Mature marketing org needs all 4 controls.

**Action:** Install dashboards + annual audit; reorganise reporting.

---

### Scenario 3 (Anti-example): IMC Failure at Global Launch
**Situation:** Tech company launches globally; same ads in every country. Fails in some markets.

**Analysis (what goes wrong):**
- Standardisation without adaptation
- Cultural context missed
- WOM turns negative

**Cost of this mistake:** $10M failed launch in 3 countries.

**Decision rule:** Localise creative; keep core theme.

**Action:** Re-launch with adapted creative per market; test first.
