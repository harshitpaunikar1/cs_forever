
# Digital & Online Media (Social, Search, Ads)

## Overview

Digital marketing uses online tools like social media, search ads, influencers, email, and websites to reach customers. It helps target specific people and measure results quickly.

## Why It Matters

Customers spend a lot of time online, and digital allows low-cost testing, precise targeting, and fast learning. It’s also easier to track performance than many offline methods.

## Key Principles

- Choose platforms where your customers are active.
- Use content that educates, entertains, or solves problems.
- Track metrics and improve continuously.
- Balance paid ads with organic growth.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **SEO** | Improving website visibility in search results. |
| **SEM/Paid search** | Paying for search ads (e.g., Google Ads). |
| **CTR** | Click-through rate (clicks / impressions). |
| **Conversion** | Desired action (purchase, signup). |
| **Retargeting** | Showing ads to people who visited before. |

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Framework 1: Channel-Audience Fit Matrix
**Purpose:** Decide which channels deserve budget based on where the audience is and how strong the intent is.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
                    High intent
            Search / landing pages / retargeting
High audience     -------------------------------
presence          Social / email / communities
            Awareness / discovery / broad reach
                    Low intent
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: audience presence - where customers already spend time.
Component 2: intent - how close they are to acting now.
Component 3: content fit - whether the message matches the channel.
Component 4: spend priority - fund the quadrant that matches the buying stage.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A B2B AI workflow tool finds that engineers are active on LinkedIn and search, while executives respond better to email and case studies. The matrix says LinkedIn for discovery, search for high-intent demand capture, and email for nurture.

When to pull this out in a meeting: When someone wants to fund every channel equally.

### Framework 2: Test-Learn-Scale Loop
**Purpose:** Run digital marketing as a sequence of small tests before committing real budget.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Hypothesis -> Small test -> Metric readout -> Learning -> Scale / stop
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: hypothesis - what you expect to happen.
Component 2: test size - the cheapest valid experiment.
Component 3: metric readout - CTR, conversion, CAC, or ROAS.
Component 4: decision - double down, revise, or kill.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product team tests two landing pages for an AI note-taking app. One uses feature-led copy, the other uses outcome-led copy. The winning page gets the paid search budget.

When to pull this out in a meeting: When a launch is being approved without a measurable pilot.

### Framework 3: Search-Social-Retargeting Funnel
**Purpose:** Connect discovery, capture, and recovery into one flow instead of treating channels separately.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Social discovery -> Search capture -> Retargeting recovery -> Conversion
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: social discovery - create interest and reach.
Component 2: search capture - meet high-intent queries.
Component 3: retargeting recovery - bring back undecided visitors.
Component 4: conversion - turn traffic into a business result.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A cybersecurity consulting firm runs thought-leadership reels, search ads for "cloud security audit", and retargeting ads with a free assessment offer. The funnel shows how awareness becomes booked calls.

When to pull this out in a meeting: When traffic is happening, but conversion is weak.

### Framework 4: Paid vs Organic Portfolio
**Purpose:** Balance immediate traffic with compounding traffic.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
            More control
     Paid search / paid social
  --------------------------------
  SEO / community / email / content
            More compounding
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: paid channels - faster scale, higher marginal cost.
Component 2: organic channels - slower scale, lower marginal cost.
Component 3: content assets - posts, guides, videos, landing pages.
Component 4: efficiency - mix that meets both quarterly and long-term goals.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A SaaS PM funds paid search for launch demand but also invests in SEO pages and help content so the acquisition cost falls over time.

When to pull this out in a meeting: When the team is overspending on ads and ignoring durable traffic.

## 4. Formulas
No explicit numeric formulas appear in the source. The formulas below are decision heuristics synthesized from the source themes and marked [verified from model knowledge, not source].

### Formula 1: CTR
Formula: `CTR = clicks / impressions`
Variables:
clicks = number of ad clicks
impressions = number of times the ad was shown
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the creative and targeting earned attention.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 1% -> weak relevance -> revise creative or targeting
Value 1%-3% -> acceptable -> test variants
Value > 3% -> strong response -> scale carefully
Worked example with numbers: 240 clicks from 12,000 impressions = 2.0% CTR. Decision: keep testing, but do not scale until conversion quality is proven.

### Formula 2: Conversion Rate
Formula: `Conversion Rate = conversions / clicks`
Variables:
conversions = signups, purchases, donations, or bookings
clicks = ad or link clicks
Why this formula exists: It answers whether traffic turns into business value.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 2% -> weak landing experience -> fix offer or page
Value 2%-5% -> workable -> optimize message match
Value > 5% -> strong intent or offer fit -> scale traffic
Worked example with numbers: 18 donations from 300 clicks = 6% conversion rate. Decision: the donation page is working; expand reach.

### Formula 3: CAC
Formula: `CAC = marketing spend / new customers acquired`
Variables:
marketing spend = total campaign cost
new customers acquired = converted customers in the period
Why this formula exists: It answers what each customer costs to acquire.
How to interpret the output:
Value > unit margin or > LTV / 3 -> dangerous -> reduce spend or improve funnel
Value roughly within target range -> monitor -> keep optimizing
Value clearly below target -> attractive -> scale channel
Worked example with numbers: $6,000 spend and 120 customers = $50 CAC. Decision: if one customer is worth more than $150 in gross margin, the channel may be attractive.

### Formula 4: ROAS
Formula: `ROAS = attributed revenue / ad spend`
Variables:
attributed revenue = revenue linked to the campaign
ad spend = media cost
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the campaign produces enough revenue for the money spent.
How to interpret the output:
Value < 1.0 -> losing money on media
Value 1.0-3.0 -> watch closely and improve funnel
Value > 3.0 -> healthy on direct revenue, subject to margin
Worked example with numbers: $24,000 attributed revenue on $6,000 spend = 4.0x ROAS. Decision: scale only if margins and incrementality still hold.

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Launch on every channel just because it exists | Pick the channels where customers are active and intent is visible |
| Judge success by impressions alone | Track CTR, conversion, CAC, and ROAS together |
| Scale spend before testing message-market fit | Run low-cost tests first and learn from the cheapest valid experiment |
| Treat retargeting like a dump-all bucket | Segment visitors and retarget by behavior and recency |
| Overweight paid ads and ignore organic growth | Build paid for speed and organic for compounding |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)
### Scenario 1: SaaS Launch With Search Plus Retargeting
Situation: An AI workflow product launches with Google search ads and retargeting for trial signups. Search delivers 20,000 impressions, 500 clicks, and 40 trials, while retargeting adds 10 more trials from 80 clicks.
Applicable framework/metric: Search-Social-Retargeting Funnel and CAC.
Analysis: Search CTR is 2.5%. Trial conversion from search clicks is 8%. If search spend is $2,500, CAC is $62.50 per trial before retargeting effects. Retargeting lifts total trials to 50, which improves blended CAC.
Decision rule: If trial CAC is below the expected first-year margin, keep scaling. If CAC rises while conversion falls, tighten targeting or landing pages.
Action: Move budget toward high-intent search terms, keep retargeting only for recent visitors, and test one landing page variant.

### Scenario 2: Nonprofit Donation Drive
Situation: A nonprofit uses Instagram reels, email, and retargeting to push donations during a campaign. The campaign gets 100,000 impressions, 2,000 clicks, 220 donations, and $5,500 spend.
Applicable framework/metric: Test-Learn-Scale Loop and Conversion Rate.
Analysis: CTR is 2.0%. Click-to-donation conversion is 11%. Cost per donation is $25. If average donation is $60 and repeat giving is stable, the campaign can fund future outreach.
Decision rule: If cost per donation is below target and donation quality is stable, scale. If the cost rises or repeat-giver quality drops, rework creative and audience selection.
Action: Test a storytelling version of the reel, tighten email segmentation, and exclude people who already donated from generic retargeting.

### Scenario 3: Local Service With SEO and Paid Search
Situation: A dental clinic runs SEO pages for "best dentist near me" and Google Ads for high-intent search queries. Organic traffic brings 12,000 visits and 360 bookings. Paid search brings 1,500 visits, 135 bookings, and costs $1,800.
Applicable framework/metric: Paid vs Organic Portfolio and ROAS.
Analysis: Organic booking rate is 3.0%. Paid booking rate is 9.0%. Paid CAC is about $13.33 per booking. If each booking has at least $50 of contribution margin, paid search is profitable and should stay on high-intent terms.
Decision rule: If paid CAC stays below margin per booking, keep the ads. If organic traffic keeps growing, shift some spend into SEO and content rather than adding more paid budget.
Action: Expand local SEO pages, keep paid ads on the strongest keywords, and review calls-to-booking quality weekly.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Map the channel mix into a simple audience-by-intent grid in a spreadsheet or Miro board.
2. Build a tracking sheet for impressions, clicks, CTR, conversions, CAC, and ROAS for every campaign.
3. Create two to three message variants in a creative test backlog before scaling any spend.
4. Set retargeting rules by recency, behavior, and audience exclusion in the ad platform.
5. Publish one landing page per major offer and record the page-to-conversion rate.
6. Review paid-versus-organic spend monthly in a budget dashboard for the PM or marketing lead.
7. Write a weekly learning memo that captures test results, decisions, and next experiments.
