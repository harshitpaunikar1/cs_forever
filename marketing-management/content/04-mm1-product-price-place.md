
# Competitive Dynamics + Product Life Cycle (PLC)

## Overview

Competitive dynamics is how firms compete. PLC explains how products move from launch to growth to maturity to decline.

## Why It Matters

Strategy must change with competition and stage. What works during launch may fail in maturity.

## Key Principles

- Leader protects share; challenger attacks; follower copies; nicher specializes
- PLC stages need different marketing (intro vs maturity)
- React smartly to price wars and competitor moves

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Market leader** | top player |
| **Challenger** | tries to overtake leader |
| **Nicher** | serves a small specialized segment |
| **PLC** | introduction, growth, maturity, decline |

## Use Case

A soft drink brand uses heavy promotion in growth stage and loyalty deals in maturity.

## Scenario

> A product becomes common, competition increases, so the brand adds new variants and improves distribution.

## Examples

- New app: free trials (intro) → referrals (growth) → bundles (maturity).
- Old MP3 player market declines as streaming rises (decline).

# Product Strategy (Levels, Line, Mix, Differentiation, Packaging)

## Overview

Product strategy means deciding what product to offer, how many versions, and what features/quality/packaging it should have.

## Why It Matters

Products that match customer needs sell better. A clear product line helps serve different budgets and preferences.

## Key Principles

- Think in product levels: core benefit → actual product → extra services
- Manage product line length and product mix
- Differentiate with features, design, quality, support
- Packaging and labeling influence buying

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Core benefit** | main value (clean clothes, safe travel) |
| **Product line** | related products (shampoos of one brand) |
| **Product mix** | all product lines a company sells |
| **Warranty/guarantee** | promise about product performance |

## Use Case

A phone brand offers basic, mid, and premium models (product line strategy).

## Scenario

> Customers want both budget and premium options, so the brand introduces variants with different features and prices.

## Examples

- Biscuits: small pack for trial, family pack for heavy users.
- Detergent: standard + “stain remover” premium version.

# Services Marketing (Service Nature, Quality, Expectations, SST)

## Overview

Services are activities (like haircut, banking). Service marketing focuses on delivering good experiences.

## Why It Matters

Bad service makes customers leave quickly. Service quality builds loyalty and reputation.

## Key Principles

- Services are intangible and depend on people/process
- Manage expectations (promise only what you can deliver)
- Improve service quality with training and systems
- Use SST (self-service tech) like kiosks, apps

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Service quality** | how good the service feels to customer |
| **Customer expectations** | what they think will happen |
| **SST** | self-service technologies (self-checkout, ordering apps) |

## Use Case

A restaurant reduces waiting by using QR ordering and fast payment.

## Scenario

> A customer expects quick service. The bank adds token systems + mobile support to reduce frustration.

## Examples

- Airline improves service via better check-in and complaint handling.
- Hospital uses appointment app to reduce waiting and confusion.

# Pricing Strategy (Objectives, Demand, Costs, Competition, Methods)

## Overview

Pricing decides how much to charge so customers buy and the company earns profit.

## Why It Matters

Price affects demand strongly. Too high reduces buyers; too low reduces profit and brand value.

## Key Principles

- Set pricing objective (profit, market share, survival)
- Understand demand sensitivity
- Include costs and competitor pricing
- Use smart tactics: discounts, bundles, segmented pricing

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Price elasticity** | how demand changes when price changes |
| **Penetration pricing** | low price to gain market quickly |
| **Skimming** | high price first, then lower later |
| **Discounts/allowances** | price reductions for conditions |

## Use Case

A new OTT platform uses low intro price to gain subscribers (penetration).

## Scenario

> Competitor cuts price. Company decides whether to match, improve value, or focus on premium segment.

## Examples

- Combo meals priced lower than buying items separately (bundle).
- Student discount for movie tickets (segmented pricing).

# Marketing Channels (Design, Management, Conflict, Omni-channel)

## Overview

Channels are the ways a product reaches customers: online, retailers, distributors, etc.

## Why It Matters

Even great products fail if customers can’t find them easily. Good channels increase reach and convenience.

## Key Principles

- Choose channels based on customer buying habits
- Decide channel levels (direct vs intermediaries)
- Manage channel members with incentives and support
- Handle channel conflicts (online vs offline price fights)

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Channel** | path product takes to customer |
| **Intermediary** | middle party (dealer, retailer) |
| **Omni-channel** | connected online + offline experience |
| **Channel conflict** | clash between channel partners |

## Use Case

A brand sells online and in stores with consistent offers to avoid conflict.

## Scenario

> Retailers complain about lower online prices. Brand creates separate online-exclusive bundles and keeps base price consistent.

## Examples

- Cosmetics: sells via stores + website + marketplaces.
- Electronics: authorized dealers + service centers + online store.

# Retailing, Wholesaling, and Logistics

## Overview

Retail = selling to final customers. Wholesale = selling in bulk to retailers. Logistics = moving and storing products efficiently.

## Why It Matters

Good logistics reduces cost and improves delivery speed. Retail decisions affect customer experience and sales.

## Key Principles

- Choose store format and location wisely
- Manage inventory to avoid stockouts
- Efficient logistics improves service and reduces waste
- Wholesalers help reach more retail points

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Retail format** | supermarket, specialty store, online, etc. |
| **Private label** | store’s own brand |
| **Supply chain** | flow from producer to customer |
| **Inventory** | stock of products |

## Use Case

A grocery chain uses warehouses and route planning to deliver fresh items daily.

## Scenario

> A store frequently runs out of popular items. Better forecasting + supplier coordination fixes the problem.

## Examples

- D-mart style bulk buying lowers cost and passes savings to customers.
- E-commerce uses fulfillment centers to deliver fast.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Framework 1: 5 Product Levels (Levitt / Kotler)
**Purpose:** Identify differentiation opportunities at each level.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Level 5: POTENTIAL — all possible future augmentations
      (innovation opportunities; delight factor)
 Level 4: AUGMENTED — additional services/support
      (warranty, installation, 24/7 support, training)
 Level 3: EXPECTED — what customers take for granted
      (basic functionality + quality expected by category)
 Level 2: BASIC — minimum functional product
      (meets core need; table stakes)
 Level 1: CORE BENEFIT — fundamental customer need
      (what they're really buying — e.g., "communication" for phone)

 Each level is an opportunity to differentiate.
 Mature markets compete increasingly on higher levels.
```

Components:
- 5 levels
- Competition climbs the levels as markets mature

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** AI SaaS:
- Core: productivity gain
- Basic: AI model working
- Expected: integrations + SSO + API
- Augmented: training + dedicated success manager + compliance docs
- Potential: future AI-agent capabilities; co-development

Map competitors against the 5 levels; find gaps for differentiation.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Product-strategy workshops; new-feature prioritisation.

---

### Framework 2: SERVQUAL 5 Dimensions
**Purpose:** Diagnose service quality systematically.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Dimension         │ What it means                          │ Example (AI tool)
 ────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────
 Reliability        │ Delivers promised service dependably  │ 99.9% uptime
 Assurance          │ Knowledge + courtesy of staff          │ Expert support
 Tangibles          │ Physical facilities, equipment, docs  │ Clean UI, polished docs
 Empathy            │ Caring individual attention            │ Customer success
 Responsiveness     │ Willingness to help + prompt service   │ < 1hr response SLA

 Measured via customer surveys comparing:
   Expectation - Perceived Performance → Gap
 Negative gap (expected > perceived) = service failure zone.
```

Components:
- 5 dimensions; scored by customers
- Gap analysis → improvement priority

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** AI-platform SERVQUAL: Reliability strong (99.9%); Responsiveness weak (24hr SLA; customers expect 1hr). Gap identified → invest in support capacity; re-measure.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** CX reviews; support-team performance.

---

### Framework 3: Channel Design + Conflict Resolution
**Purpose:** Design coherent channel architecture; prevent/resolve conflict.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Channel Levels:
   0-level: Producer → Customer (direct-to-consumer)
   1-level: Producer → Retailer → Customer
   2-level: Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Customer
   3-level: Producer → Wholesaler → Jobber → Retailer → Customer

 Modern hybrid:
   Own D2C site + Amazon marketplace + physical stores + resellers
   (channel mix)

 CHANNEL CONFLICT TYPES:
   - Horizontal: 2 retailers same level (territory / price)
   - Vertical: producer vs. retailer (direct sales cannibalise)
   - Multi-channel: online vs offline pricing

 Resolution:
   - Channel roles clearly defined (who serves which segment)
   - Differentiated product / pricing by channel
   - Revenue-share or exclusivity deals
   - Omni-channel integration (consistent customer experience)
```

Components:
- Level choice
- Modern channels are hybrid
- Conflict resolution requires architecture

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** AI platform sold direct + via resellers. Resellers complain of direct-sales cannibalisation. Solution: direct for enterprise accounts above $50k; resellers get exclusive mid-market rights; clear territory rules; co-marketing MDF.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Channel conflict escalations; D2C strategy debates.

## 4. Formulas

### Formula 1: Price Elasticity (reminder)
See `strategic-pricing/06-price-elasticity-modeling.md` for depth. Key: `%ΔQ / %ΔP`.

---

### Formula 2: SERVQUAL Gap
**Formula:** `Gap = Expectations − Perceived Performance`, scaled 1–7 per dimension.

Negative Gap → deficiency. Prioritise largest negative.

**Worked example:** Reliability: Expected 6.2, Perceived 5.8, Gap −0.4 (minor). Responsiveness: Expected 5.5, Perceived 3.8, Gap −1.7 (severe). Responsiveness tops fix list.

**Data source:** Customer survey.

---

### Formula 3: Channel Cost + Value
**Formula:** `Channel Contribution = (Revenue × Channel Share) − (Commission + Support Cost)`

**Worked example:** Direct $1M revenue, 0% commission, $100k support = $900k contribution. Reseller $500k rev, 20% commission, $30k support = $370k contribution. Direct more profitable but reseller reaches different segments.

**Data source:** Sales by channel + cost accounting.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Compete only on core benefit in mature market | Climb to augmented / potential levels |
| Use single pricing strategy | Segment + dynamic where fit |
| Ignore channel conflict | Design rules + territory; use omni-channel |
| Skip service dimensions on non-product businesses | SERVQUAL applies |
| Treat packaging as afterthought | In CPG, packaging is marketing |
| Run online + offline with different prices unmanaged | Coordinate; differentiate by channel-specific value |
| Kept rigid product line as market shifts | Product-line pruning in maturity / decline |
| Ignore last-mile cost | It's often the biggest cost line in digital commerce |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: AI Tool Differentiating in Mature Market
**Situation:** AI market crowded; product at parity on core + basic levels.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Product Levels.

**Analysis:**
- Competitors mostly at Level 3 (Expected)
- Move to Level 4 (Augmented): dedicated CSM, training, compliance docs
- Start Level 5 (Potential): co-development partnerships

**Decision rule:** In mature market, differentiate at higher product levels.

**Action (Monday morning):** Build CSM programme; offer enterprise training + certification; annual roadmap co-design.

---

### Scenario 2: Consulting Advising on Channel Conflict
**Situation:** Client sells via resellers + own e-commerce. Resellers angry about direct prices.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Channel Design + Conflict Resolution.

**Analysis:**
- Current: same price both channels; direct marketing aggressive
- Fix: territorial rules + differentiated SKUs + MDF for resellers

**Decision rule:** Modern B2B should have clear channel architecture.

**Action:** 2-week workshop; redesign channel policy; roll out with 30-day notice.

---

### Scenario 3 (Anti-example): Packaging as Afterthought
**Situation:** FMCG client launches premium product in basic packaging; shelf presence weak.

**Analysis (what goes wrong):**
- In CPG, package is marketing
- Basic packaging signals commodity; consumers pass over
- 40% sales shortfall vs plan

**Cost of this mistake:** $3M launch impact; 6 months lost.

**Decision rule:** CPG packaging investment = 10-20% of product cost; matches positioning.

**Action:** Re-design packaging; re-launch Q2.
