
# Mass Communication (Advertising, promotions, PR, events)

## Overview

Mass communication talks to many people at once using TV, radio, print, events, PR, and promotions.

## Why It Matters

It builds awareness fast and shapes public image. Promotions can boost short-term sales.

## Key Principles

- Set clear objective (inform, persuade, remind)
- Choose media type and timing carefully
- Promotions should not damage brand value
- PR builds credibility more than ads

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Advertising** | Paid mass media communication |
| **Sales promotion** | Short-term incentives |
| **PR** | Public relations (earned media) |
| **Sponsorship** | Supporting events for visibility |

## Use Case

A car company launches with TV ads + auto expo event + PR reviews.

## Scenario

> A brand sponsors a marathon and promotes “fitness lifestyle,” gaining trust and visibility.

## Examples

- “Buy 1 Get 1” for clearing inventory.
- PR press release after winning an award.

# Personal Communication (Direct, interactive, WOM, personal selling)

## Overview

Personal communication includes direct messages (email/SMS), interactive marketing (web/app), word-of-mouth, and personal selling.

## Why It Matters

It can be more persuasive than mass ads because it feels personal. Word-of-mouth can grow a brand quickly.

## Key Principles

- Personalize communication using data
- Encourage reviews and referrals
- Train sales teams for trust-building
- Make it easy to share and recommend

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Direct marketing** | Email/SMS/catalog/telecalling |
| **Interactive marketing** | Website/app engagement |
| **WOM** | Word-of-mouth recommendations |
| **Viral marketing** | Content spreading rapidly |

## Use Case

A D2C brand runs referral codes and email reminders for repeat purchase.

## Scenario

> A customer loves a new café, posts it on Instagram, and friends visit—sales grow without paid ads.

## Examples

- Referral bonus in apps (₹100 credit).
- Sales reps selling water purifiers through demos.

# New Market Offerings / NPD

## Overview

This covers how companies create new offerings—ideas, testing, development, market testing, launch, and customer adoption.

## Why It Matters

New offerings drive growth. But failures are costly, so structured NPD improves chances of success.

## Key Principles

- Generate many ideas, screen quickly
- Test concept with customers early
- Do business analysis (cost, profit potential)
- Pilot launch before full rollout
- Track adoption and improve

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Concept testing** | Testing idea with customers |
| **Market testing** | Testing in a small market |
| **Commercialization** | Full launch |
| **Adoption process** | Awareness → interest → trial → repeat |

## Use Case

A snack brand launches a “baked chips” product first in metro cities, then expands.

## Scenario

> A company tests 2 new flavors in one city. The winner gets national launch.

## Examples

- New chocolate “healthy dark” aimed at health-conscious buyers.
- New app feature rolled out to 10% users first.

# Global Marketing

## Overview

Global marketing is selling in other countries. Companies decide whether to go abroad, which countries to enter, and how much to change the product and marketing.

## Why It Matters

Global markets bring growth, but also risk (culture differences, laws, competition). Correct entry strategy reduces failures.

## Key Principles

- Choose market based on demand and feasibility
- Pick entry mode (export, licensing, JV, investment)
- Decide standardization vs adaptation
- Manage country-of-origin perceptions

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Export** | Selling abroad from home country |
| **Licensing** | Allowing another company to use your brand/tech |
| **Joint venture** | Partnering with local firm |
| **Adaptation** | Changing product/marketing to fit local needs |

## Use Case

An Indian snack brand adapts spice levels for Southeast Asian markets.

## Scenario

> A company enters UAE through a local partner (JV) to understand regulations and distribution.

## Examples

- McDonald’s adapting menu by country.
- Smartphone brands changing features/prices for emerging markets.

# Holistic Marketing Organization (Internal marketing, CSR, control)

## Overview

Holistic marketing means the whole company works together for the customer—internal teams, society responsibility, long-term planning, and measurement.

## Why It Matters

Marketing is not only the marketing department’s job. Long-term success needs alignment, ethics, and continuous improvement.

## Key Principles

- Align departments (sales, service, operations)
- Build a customer-driven culture
- Practice responsible marketing (CSR, cause marketing)
- Control and improve through metrics and audits

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Internal marketing** | Employees aligned to brand and customer |
| **CSR** | Corporate social responsibility |
| **Marketing control** | Checking performance vs plan |
| **Strategic control** | Long-term evaluation of strategy |

## Use Case

A company runs cause-related campaigns and improves customer trust while tracking performance with dashboards.

## Scenario

> A brand faces complaints about packaging waste. It switches to recyclable packs and communicates the change, improving brand image and loyalty.

## Examples

- TOMS-style cause branding (buy one, give one concept).
- A firm using monthly dashboards to track leads, conversions, and churn.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Awareness-to-Action Ladder
**Purpose:** Move the audience from seeing the message to acting on it.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Awareness -> Interest -> Trial -> Repeat
```
Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Awareness: does the audience know the offer exists?
Interest: does the audience care?
Trial: do they try it once?
Repeat: do they come back?
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: An AI copilot launch may use PR for awareness, a demo webinar for interest, a free pilot for trial, and onboarding nudges for repeat use.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When a launch has awareness but weak conversion.

### Persuasion Loop
**Purpose:** Decide whether the mix should be mass, personal, or referral-led.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Mass message -> personal follow-up -> WOM/referrals -> conversion
```
Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Mass message: TV, print, events, sponsorship.
Personal follow-up: email, SMS, direct marketing, sales outreach.
WOM/referrals: social proof and trust transfer.
Conversion: the decision and repeat behavior.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A consulting firm can use a thought-leadership article for reach, targeted follow-up emails for meetings, and client references to close enterprise work.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When the campaign needs both awareness and trust.

### NPD Funnel
**Purpose:** Cut product-launch risk before scaling the spend.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Ideas -> concept test -> market test -> commercialization -> adoption
```
Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Concept testing: will customers understand the idea?
Market testing: does it work in a small market?
Commercialization: full launch.
Adoption process: awareness to repeat behavior.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A mobile fintech feature is concept-tested with users, market-tested in one city, then commercialized only after pilot adoption and support metrics look healthy.
When to pull this out in a meeting: Before a product launch or promotional spend spike.

### Global Entry Matrix
**Purpose:** Choose the right market-entry mode and the right level of adaptation.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Export < Licensing < JV < Direct investment
            |         |
      more control?   more local fit?
```
Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Export: lowest commitment.
Licensing: low investment, less control.
Joint venture: shared control, local knowledge.
Adaptation vs standardization: how much the offer changes by country.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A SaaS company entering a regulated market may choose a joint venture for distribution and compliance while keeping the core product standardized.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When the team is deciding how to enter a new country.

### Holistic Control Loop
**Purpose:** Make the whole firm responsible for the brand and the customer.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Internal marketing -> culture -> customer experience -> control
            |             |              |
          CSR         alignment        metrics
```
Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Internal marketing: employees understand and deliver the promise.
CSR: the firm behaves responsibly.
Control: metrics and audits keep the strategy honest.
Trust: the brand earns credibility over time.
IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: An AI company with privacy concerns must align product, legal, support, and marketing so the message matches the actual controls.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When brand promise and operational reality start to drift.

## 4. Formulas
The source is mostly qualitative, so the formulas below are model-knowledge heuristics that make the decision logic explicit. `[verified from model knowledge, not source]`

### Formula 1: Communication Impact Score `[verified from model knowledge, not source]`
Formula: `Impact = Reach * Relevance * Credibility`
Variables:
`Reach` = size of the audience touched
`Relevance` = fit between message and need
`Credibility` = trust in the source and channel
Why this formula exists: It helps compare campaign options quickly.
How to interpret the output:
Low reach but high credibility -> useful for niche or enterprise sales
High reach but low credibility -> awareness only, weak conversion
High reach + relevance + credibility -> strongest launch candidate
Worked example with numbers: A PR story reaches 1.0M people, but relevance and credibility are 0.5 and 0.8 on a normalized scale, so impact is 400k "effective reach" units. Decision: add a targeted personal follow-up.

### Formula 2: NPD Readiness Score `[verified from model knowledge, not source]`
Formula: `Readiness = (Concept_Test + Market_Test + Ops_Readiness + Support_Readiness) / 4`
Variables:
`Concept_Test` = customer understanding
`Market_Test` = small-market performance
`Ops_Readiness` = ability to deliver at scale
`Support_Readiness` = service and complaint handling readiness
Why this formula exists: It keeps commercialization from outrunning the organization.
How to interpret the output:
Below 2.5 -> keep testing
2.5-3.5 -> pilot and refine
Above 3.5 -> launch broadly
Worked example with numbers: Scores of 4, 3, 4, 4 give readiness 3.75. Decision: launch with close monitoring and a rollback plan.

### Formula 3: Global Entry Fit `[verified from model knowledge, not source]`
Formula: `Fit = Demand * Feasibility * Local_Adaptation / Risk`
Variables:
`Demand` = market attractiveness
`Feasibility` = operational and regulatory fit
`Local_Adaptation` = how well the offer can be localized
`Risk` = regulatory, channel, and execution risk
Why this formula exists: It turns country-entry discussions into a decision screen.
How to interpret the output:
Low fit -> export only or wait
Mid fit -> partner or JV
High fit -> invest directly
Worked example with numbers: Demand 4, feasibility 3, adaptation 4, risk 2 gives fit 24. Decision: JV or direct entry depending on control needs.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| Don't | Do |
|-------|----|
| Run a promotion without a clear objective. | Pick inform, persuade, or remind as the objective first. |
| Use mass advertising when trust is the real problem. | Add PR, referrals, and personal follow-up. |
| Launch nationally before testing the offer. | Concept-test and market-test before commercialization. |
| Assume global markets want the same message. | Decide where to standardize and where to adapt. |
| Treat CSR as a side project. | Build it into culture, control, and brand trust. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: AI Product Launch Needs Awareness and Trial
**Situation:** A product team is launching an AI meeting assistant. Early traffic is strong, but trial and repeat usage are weak.
**Applicable framework/metric:** Awareness-to-Action Ladder + Communication Impact Score.
**Analysis:** Reach is high, but relevance is only moderate because the message is too generic. Trial conversion is 7 percent and repeat is 3 percent.
**Decision rule:** If awareness is high but trial is low, sharpen the message and add a product demo; if trial is low but repeat is high, improve onboarding; if both are low, revisit positioning.
**Action:** Pair a mass-awareness campaign with a targeted demo and a referral-based trial offer.

### Scenario 2: Consulting Firm Using WOM to Win Enterprise Deals
**Situation:** A consulting firm wants to sell an analytics transformation to a bank. Paid ads are not credible enough for this market.
**Applicable framework/metric:** Persuasion Loop.
**Analysis:** The firm has strong reference clients and client testimonials, which makes WOM and personal selling more powerful than mass promotion.
**Decision rule:** If trust is the bottleneck, use references and direct outreach; if scale is the bottleneck, add broad PR and thought leadership.
**Action:** Run a case-study event, follow with partner-led outreach, and ask for referral intros from current clients.

### Scenario 3: Global Entry Through a Local Partner
**Situation:** A SaaS company wants to enter a regulated overseas market. Direct export is simple, but local distribution and legal requirements are unclear.
**Applicable framework/metric:** Global Entry Matrix + Global Entry Fit.
**Analysis:** Demand 4, feasibility 3, adaptation 4, risk 2 gives fit 24. A JV scores better than direct export because local knowledge matters.
**Decision rule:** If regulation and distribution are high-friction, prefer a JV or licensing structure; if the market is simple and control matters more, export or direct invest.
**Action:** Form a local partnership, standardize the core product, and localize messaging and compliance.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. Write one objective for each campaign: inform, persuade, or remind.
2. Build a channel map that separates mass, personal, and referral channels.
3. Run concept tests before market tests and only commercialize after a pilot clears adoption targets.
4. Decide where the global message is standardized and where the offer must be adapted.
5. Create a communications dashboard that tracks reach, trial, repeat, and trust.
6. Add CSR and internal-marketing checks so the external promise matches internal delivery.
7. Use references, testimonials, and PR together when credibility matters more than raw reach.
