
# Core Marketing Basics (Needs–Wants–Value–Markets)

## Overview

Marketing is understanding people’s needs, creating something useful, and helping people choose it.

## Why It Matters

When companies understand real needs, they build products people actually want. That leads to better sales and stronger customer trust.

## Key Principles

- People buy benefits, not just products
- Marketing is about value creation, not only advertising
- Focus on customer satisfaction and long-term relationships

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Need** | Basic requirement (food, safety, comfort) |
| **Want** | Need shaped by culture and taste (pizza vs rice) |
| **Demand** | Want + ability to pay |
| **Value** | Benefits minus costs (money, time, effort) |
| **Market** | Set of buyers with similar needs |

## Use Case

A water purifier brand highlights “safe water for family health” (benefit), not just “filters.”

## Scenario

> A family worries about water quality → searches solutions → chooses a purifier that promises safe water + low maintenance.

## Examples

- Smartphone buyers want “good photos” → companies market camera quality.
- Students need “easy travel” → scooter brands market mileage + comfort.

# Marketing Orientations + Holistic Marketing

## Overview

A company can think in different ways: “Make more” (production) “Make better product” (product) “Push sales hard” (selling) “Understand customer” (marketing)Holistic marketing means everyone in the company supports customer value.

## Why It Matters

Wrong orientation can cause failure (great product, but nobody wants it). Holistic marketing builds consistent customer experience across departments.

## Key Principles

- Customer-first thinking usually wins long-term
- Marketing works best when sales, service, operations work together
- Measure not only sales, but also customer satisfaction and impact

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Production concept** | Customers prefer availability/low cost |
| **Product concept** | Customers prefer best quality/features |
| **Selling concept** | Customers need persuasion to buy |
| **Marketing concept** | Start with customer needs |
| **Holistic marketing** | Relationship + integrated + internal + performance marketing |

## Use Case

A bank trains staff, improves app, simplifies processes, and runs customer-friendly communication—together.

## Scenario

> A customer complains about slow service. Holistic marketing fixes front desk, backend process, and communication—so the experience improves end-to-end.

## Examples

- Fast-food chain improves packaging + delivery timing + complaint handling.
- E-commerce app aligns ads, product pages, delivery, and returns policy.

# Strategy & Planning (Mission, SBU, SWOT, Marketing Plan)

## Overview

Marketing strategy is deciding where to compete and how to win. Planning turns strategy into a clear action plan.

## Why It Matters

Without planning, teams do random promotions. Strategy helps use time and budget on the most useful activities.

## Key Principles

- Start with mission and goals
- Use SWOT to understand situation
- Choose target market and positioning
- Create a marketing plan with actions + budget + control

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Mission** | Why the business exists |
| **SBU** | Strategic Business Unit (a major business division) |
| **SWOT** | Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats |
| **Marketing plan** | Written plan for objectives + actions + budget |

## Use Case

A clothing brand plans a winter launch: segment → target → positioning → product range → promotion calendar.

## Scenario

> A shoe company sees growth in running culture (opportunity), has good design (strength), but weak distribution (weakness). It partners with online platforms and launches a running line.

## Examples

- Gym sets goal: “500 new members in 3 months” → plan includes offers + referrals + local ads.
- Restaurant uses SWOT → adds lunch combos to increase weekday sales.

# Marketing Information + Intelligence + Demand Forecasting

## Overview

This is about collecting information (sales, market trends, competitors) and using it to estimate future demand.

## Why It Matters

Forecasting helps avoid overstock or shortage. Good information improves pricing, production, and promotion timing.

## Key Principles

- Use internal records + market intelligence
- Track trends in economy, tech, lifestyle
- Forecast demand using past sales + market data
- Update forecasts regularly

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **MIS** | System to collect/store/share marketing data continuously |
| **Marketing intelligence** | Ongoing info about market changes |
| **Forecasting** | Estimating future demand |
| **Demand measurement** | Calculating market size and sales potential |

## Use Case

A dairy brand forecasts summer demand for ice cream and increases production early.

## Scenario

> A store sees rising demand for air coolers in May every year, so it orders stock in March to avoid missing sales.

## Examples

- Cinema forecasts holiday crowd and increases show timings.
- Umbrella company predicts monsoon demand based on weather + last-year sales.

# Marketing Research Process

## Overview

Marketing research is a step-by-step way to solve a marketing problem using data.

## Why It Matters

It reduces guesswork. It helps decide what customers prefer, what price works, and which ad is effective.

## Key Principles

- Define the problem clearly
- Choose method (survey/interview/observation/experiment)
- Collect and analyze data carefully
- Present findings and make decisions

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Research objective** | What you want to learn |
| **Questionnaire** | Structured survey questions |
| **Sampling** | Choosing a small group to represent many |
| **Dashboard** | Visual tracking of metrics |

## Use Case

A company tests two ad versions and chooses the one that increases sign-ups more.

## Scenario

> A café’s sales drop. Research shows customers want faster service. Café adds self-order and improves speed.

## Examples

- A brand runs focus groups for new shampoo fragrance.
- A food app surveys users to improve delivery experience.

# Customer Value, Satisfaction, Loyalty, CRM, CLV

## Overview

This topic is about keeping customers happy so they buy again and stay loyal.

## Why It Matters

Getting new customers is often harder than keeping old ones. Loyal customers bring repeat sales and positive word-of-mouth.

## Key Principles

- Deliver value better than competitors
- Measure satisfaction (feedback, ratings, NPS)
- Use CRM to manage relationships
- Focus on long-term customer lifetime value (CLV)

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Customer perceived value** | Customer’s view of benefit vs cost |
| **Satisfaction** | How well product meets expectations |
| **Loyalty** | Repeated purchase + preference |
| **CRM** | System/process to manage customer relationships |
| **CLV** | Total profit from a customer over time |

## Use Case

A telecom company offers special plans to long-term customers to reduce churn.

## Scenario

> A subscriber wants to cancel. CRM shows they are valuable, so the company offers a better plan + faster support to retain them.

## Examples

- Coffee shop gives points and free drinks to loyal customers.
- Online store gives easy returns to increase trust and repeat purchases.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Framework 1: Marketing Orientations Evolution
**Purpose:** Diagnose your org's orientation; plan evolution.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Orientation    │ Era       │ Focus                   │ Anti-pattern
 ─────────────┼───────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────
 Production     │ Pre-1920  │ Make more, cheaper      │ "If we build it, they'll buy"
 Product        │ 1920-50   │ Best product wins       │ Feature obsession
 Selling        │ 1950-70   │ Aggressive push          │ Customer distrust
 Marketing      │ 1970-90   │ Customer needs first     │ Moderate; sustainable
 Holistic       │ 1990+     │ Relationship + internal │ Integrated experience
                │           │ + integrated + performance│
 Societal       │ 2010+     │ + social + environmental │ ESG + marketing align
```

Components:
- 6 orientations; most modern orgs mix holistic + societal
- "Product concept" trap = classic tech-company failure mode

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** AI startup pushes technical specs in every meeting; customers leave. Diagnosis: Product concept. Shift to Marketing concept: start with customer job. Pipeline doubles.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Marketing-org assessments; culture debates.

---

### Framework 2: Holistic Marketing 4-in-1
**Purpose:** Ensure marketing crosses all four dimensions.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Dimension              │ What it means
 ─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────
 RELATIONSHIP           │ Build long-term relationships with
                        │ customers, partners, employees
 INTEGRATED             │ Coherent marketing mix across
                        │ channels, messages, touchpoints
 INTERNAL               │ All employees buy into + deliver
                        │ customer promise
 PERFORMANCE            │ ROI-measured; finance + brand metrics
```

Components:
- 4 dimensions; gaps = inconsistent customer experience
- Internal often the weakest

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** SaaS platform with great external marketing; staff in support not trained on positioning; customer experience inconsistent. Fix: internal training + performance metrics tied to customer outcomes.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Marketing-org reviews; CX debates.

### Framework 3: Customer Value → Loyalty Cascade
**Purpose:** Trace value creation → loyalty → CLV growth.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Customer Perceived Value
         │
         ▼
 Satisfaction (expectations met/exceeded)
         │
         ▼
 Loyalty (behavioural + attitudinal)
         │
         ▼
 Retention + Expansion (repeat purchase; cross-sell)
         │
         ▼
 CLV (long-term profit)
         │
         ▼
 Lower CAC (referrals from loyal)
         │
         ▼
 Virtuous cycle

 Break points:
  - Value gap → satisfaction gap
  - Satisfaction without loyalty (competitor offers more)
  - Loyalty without expansion (missed upsell)
```

Components:
- 5-step cascade
- Each link has levers + measurement

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** SaaS company measures: CPV via willingness-to-pay surveys; NPS rising to 52; retention 95%; expansion NRR 118%; CLV $20k. Every link tracked.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Customer-experience program design; board metric reviews.

## 4. Formulas

### Formula 1: Customer Perceived Value
**Formula:** `CPV = Total Benefits (functional + emotional + social) − Total Costs (monetary + time + energy + psychic)`

**Why this formula exists:** Captures why customers choose you — non-monetary factors matter.

**Worked example:** Customer comparing 2 AI tools:
- Option A: $20/mo; saves 3 hrs/week; basic quality → Low CPV despite low price
- Option B: $50/mo; saves 5 hrs/week; integrates with stack → Higher CPV

Customer picks B despite higher price.

**Data source:** Conjoint studies; customer interviews.

---

### Formula 2: Customer Satisfaction (CSAT/NPS)
See `business-analytics/09-customer-analytics.md`. Key: NPS = %Promoters − %Detractors.

Benchmark: NPS 30-50 good; 50+ great.

---

### Formula 3: CLV
See `business-analytics/09-customer-analytics.md`. Key SaaS formula: `CLV = (ARPU × GM%) / Churn`.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Fall into Product-Concept trap | Start with customer need / JTBD |
| Do external marketing without internal alignment | Internal marketing: everyone knows the promise |
| Measure only sales | Measure CLV, NPS, retention, expansion |
| Treat forecasting as one-shot | Rolling + scenario-based forecasts |
| Confuse satisfaction with loyalty | Measure both; satisfied customers can still leave |
| Rely only on MR projects | Build MIS for continuous intelligence |
| Ignore Kano delight attributes | Delight attributes differentiate; basics are table stakes |
| Orient around "best product" | Orient around best customer outcome |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: SaaS Moving from Product Concept to Marketing Concept
**Situation:** AI startup failing despite superior tech. Competitors with weaker tech winning.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Marketing Orientations + CPV.

**Analysis:**
- Product orientation evident: pitch focuses on specs
- Customers care about outcome (time saved, ease of use)
- Shift messaging: emphasise outcomes; customer case studies

**Decision rule:** Pitch customer outcomes, not product features.

**Action (Monday morning):** Retrain sales + marketing; rewrite website around customer outcomes.

---

### Scenario 2: Consulting Advising on Loyalty Program
**Situation:** Retailer's repeat-purchase rate stagnant; CAC rising.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Value→Loyalty Cascade + CLV.

**Analysis:**
- NPS 38 (good); retention weak (65%)
- Gap: satisfaction → loyalty
- Launch tiered loyalty program tied to CLV

**Decision rule:** Build loyalty where satisfaction exists but retention weak.

**Action:** Tier program launch; measure retention lift in 6 months.

---

### Scenario 3 (Anti-example): Best Product, No Customers
**Situation:** Tech team builds "technically superior" product; 0 customer interviews done pre-launch.

**Analysis (what goes wrong):**
- Product Concept trap
- Missed actual customer need
- Product fails; $5M engineering wasted

**Cost of this mistake:** $5M + 18 months + team morale.

**Decision rule:** Customer interviews mandatory pre-build.

**Action:** Kill / pivot; rebuild with Marketing Concept discipline.
