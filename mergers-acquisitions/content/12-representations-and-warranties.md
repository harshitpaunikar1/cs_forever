# Representations and Warranties

## Overview

Representations (reps) are statements of fact the seller makes about the business, and warranties are promises that those statements are true. If a rep or warranty is later found to be false, the buyer can claim compensation under the sale agreement.

---

## Why It Matters

Reps and warranties move risk from the buyer to the seller for anything that was not clearly found in diligence. They shape how much of the price is really at risk after closing and are one of the most heavily negotiated parts of any deal.

## Key Principles

- Demand wide reps on title, financials, litigation, tax, and compliance
- Limit liability with caps, baskets, and time limits
- Use specific indemnities for known risks found in diligence
- Consider W&I insurance to shift risk to an insurer
- Keep a disclosure schedule that carves out specific exceptions

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Representation** | Statement of fact about the business at a point in time |
| **Warranty** | Promise that a statement is true, backed by damages |
| **Indemnity** | Pound-for-pound promise to repay specific losses |
| **Cap** | Maximum seller liability, often a percentage of price |
| **W&I Insurance** | Policy covering breach of warranties |

## Use Case

A buyer negotiates a 12-month survival period on general warranties, a 7-year period on tax warranties, a basket of Rs 1 crore before claims can start, and a cap of 20% of the purchase price.

## Scenario

> After closing, the buyer found the target had under-paid GST for two years. Thanks to a specific tax indemnity and W&I insurance, the buyer recovered Rs 18 crore in 6 months without a court battle.

## Examples

- A seller caps general warranty claims at 15% of price and 18 months survival.
- A buyer takes a W&I policy to cover unknown breaches up to $50M for 7 years.

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Representations are statements of fact the seller makes about the target (title, financials, IP, tax, compliance, data), and warranties are promises those statements are true—breach creates a contractual damages claim under the SPA. For an IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader, generic legal reps are insufficient: you must engineer tech/IP/data/AI-specific reps (training-data provenance, open-source licence hygiene, model ownership, customer-data processing, cyber breach history) BEFORE the SPA is signed, because post-close discovery without a matching rep = no recovery. The core decision: which risks stay with the seller (via reps + indemnity + W&I), which are priced into the deal, and what cap/basket/survival structure aligns risk with who controls it.

## 2. Jargon & Terminology

| Term | Full Form | Plain-English | Why It Exists | How Measured | Where You Hear It |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Representation | Statement of Fact | "This is true today" | Establishes baseline truth | Binary (true/false at signing & close) | SPA Art. 3 / 4 |
| Warranty | Contractual Promise | "We'll pay if that fact was wrong" | Converts reps into damages claim | Damages quantum | SPA + indemnity clause |
| Indemnity (General) | Loss-shifting clause | Dollar-for-dollar reimbursement for rep breach | Avoids common-law damages proof burdens | $ loss recovered | Indemnification article |
| Indemnity (Specific) | Named-risk indemnity | Carve-out for a known issue (pending tax case) | Handles risks found in diligence | Uncapped or separate cap | Schedule of specific indemnities |
| Disclosure Schedule | Seller Disclosure Letter | List of exceptions to reps | Seller's escape valve—carves out known breaches | # of items / $ exposure | Exhibit to SPA |
| Cap (General) | Max Liability—Business Reps | Ceiling on breach recovery | Keeps seller's tail liability finite | % of Enterprise Value | Term sheet / SPA §8 |
| Cap (Fundamental) | Max Liability—Core Reps | Higher/uncapped for title, authority, capitalisation | Core reps can't be "risked" | Up to 100% of EV | SPA |
| Cap (Tax) | Max Liability—Tax Reps | Special treatment, often statute of limitations | Tax is quantifiable and regulated | Often 100% of EV or uncapped | Tax indemnity schedule |
| Mini-Basket / De Minimis | Per-claim threshold | Claims below $X ignored | Avoids nuisance claims | $ per claim | SPA indemnity |
| Basket (Tipping) | Aggregate threshold—tipping | Once crossed, recover from dollar ONE | Forces materiality but full recovery on breach | Aggregate $ threshold | Negotiated by buyer |
| Basket (Deductible) | Aggregate threshold—deductible | Once crossed, recover only EXCESS | Seller-friendly; keeps skin in game | Aggregate $ threshold | Seller redline |
| Survival Period | Claim Window | Time to bring breach claim | Risk has a sunset | Months / years | SPA §8 |
| Fundamental Reps | Core Reps | Title, authority, cap-table, taxes | These are "deal-killers" if false | Usually 6 yrs → indefinite | Legal opinion relies on these |
| Business Reps | Operational Reps | Financials, IP, employees, contracts, litigation | Bread-and-butter diligence coverage | 12–24 mo survival | Bulk of SPA Art. 4 |
| W&I Insurance (RWI) | Reps & Warranties Insurance | Third-party policy covering rep breaches | Clean seller exit + buyer recovery certainty | Premium 2.5–4.5% of limit | Broker term sheet (Aon/Marsh) |
| Retention | RWI Deductible | Insurer's "basket" below which no payout | Aligns insurer with diligence | 0.75–1.0% of EV | RWI policy |
| Anti-Sandbagging | Knowledge-of-Buyer clause | Buyer can't claim on reps it knew were false pre-close | Seller-friendly | Binary clause | Negotiated |
| Pro-Sandbagging | Buyer-knowledge irrelevant | Buyer CAN claim even if it knew | Buyer-friendly default (Delaware) | Binary clause | Negotiated |
| Knowledge Qualifier | "To seller's knowledge" | Rep only covers what specified officers knew | Reduces seller liability | List of named individuals | SPA preamble |
| Materiality Qualifier | "In all material respects" | Rep breach only if material | Reduces seller liability | Materiality scrape removes it for damages | SPA |
| Materiality Scrape | Double-scrape provision | Ignores materiality qualifier for breach AND damages calc | Buyer-protective | Binary clause | Buyer redline |
| Subject-To vs Carve-Out | Disclosure mechanism | Subject-To = broad carve; Carve-Out = specific item | Precision of exceptions | Schedule structure | Disclosure letter |
| Escrow | Held-back purchase price | % of price held by escrow agent | Self-help recovery for breaches | 5–15% of EV, 12–18 mo | Escrow agreement |
| Holdback | Seller deferred consideration | Buyer withholds portion of price | Same economic effect as escrow, buyer-controlled | $ or % | SPA |
| Specific Performance | Equitable Remedy | Court orders performance, not damages | Some breaches not compensable in $ | Injunction | SPA remedies clause |
| Cap on Indemnity Liability | Aggregate cap | All indemnity claims combined ≤ $X | Seller's total downside | % of EV | SPA §8 |
| Loss Definition (Direct) | Compensable damages | Out-of-pocket losses, diminution in value | Core recovery | $ actual loss | Definitions section |
| Loss Definition (Consequential) | Indirect / Lost Profits | Downstream revenue loss, multiple-of-EBITDA damages | Highly negotiated—usually excluded or carved | $ multiplied value | Buyer redline critical |
| IP Reps | Intellectual Property Reps | Ownership, no infringement, assignment, open-source hygiene | Tech-deal core value driver | # of registrations / FTO opinion | SPA Art. 4 |
| Cyber Reps | Cybersecurity Reps | No material breaches, SOC 2, incident log | Post-GDPR / CCPA mandatory | Incident count / $ fines | SPA Art. 4 |
| Data-Protection Reps | Privacy Reps | GDPR/DPDP/CCPA compliance, DPA with vendors, consent records | Regulatory fines = multi-% of revenue | DPIA completeness | SPA + data schedule |
| AI Model / Training Data Reps | AI-Specific Reps (2023+) | Training-data provenance, model ownership, bias testing, copyright cleanliness | NYT v. OpenAI-era risk | Dataset audit log | SPA Art. 4 (modern addition) |

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Reps & Warranties Architecture
**Purpose:** Show which reps get which treatment—uncapped core vs capped operational vs special tax.

```
+------------------------------------------------------------+
|                   REPS & WARRANTIES STACK                  |
+------------------------------------------------------------+
| FUNDAMENTAL REPS  | Title, Authority, Cap-Table, No-Conflict|
|   Cap: 100% EV    | Survival: 6 yrs → Indefinite           |
|   Basket: N/A     | Anti-sandbag: rarely permitted          |
+------------------------------------------------------------+
| TAX REPS          | Historical returns, no audits, GST/VAT  |
|   Cap: 100% EV    | Survival: Statute of Limitations (7 yr)|
|   Basket: separate| Often separate tax indemnity             |
+------------------------------------------------------------+
| BUSINESS REPS     | Financials, IP, Contracts, Employees,  |
|   Cap: 10–25% EV  |   Litigation, Compliance, Cyber, AI    |
|   Basket: 0.5–1%  | Survival: 12–24 months                  |
|   RWI-coverable   | Bulk of Art. 4                          |
+------------------------------------------------------------+
| SPECIFIC INDEMS   | Named risks from DD (pending tax,       |
|   Cap: bespoke    |   specific litigation, data breach)     |
|   Survival: bespoke| Outside RWI typically                  |
+------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Components:** rep category → cap tier → survival → basket applicability → RWI coverage.
**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** $500M AI-SaaS acquisition — Fundamentals at 100% cap ($500M) / 6 yrs; Tax at 100% cap / 7 yrs; Business reps at 15% cap ($75M) / 18 mo with 0.75% tipping basket ($3.75M); Specific indemnity for pending EU AI Act audit uncapped for 3 yrs.
**Trigger:** at LOI stage, anytime a buyer signs a term sheet.

### 3.2 Cap / Basket / Survival Decision Matrix

**Purpose:** Right-size the risk envelope by deal archetype.

```
Deal Archetype      | Cap (Business) | Basket    | Survival (Gen) | RWI?
--------------------+----------------+-----------+----------------+------
VC Primary Round    |  N/A (no SPA)  |  N/A      |  N/A           | No
VC Secondary        |  100% of tranche| 1%       |  12 mo         | Rare
Growth/Minority PE  |  15–25%        |  0.75–1%  |  18 mo         | Sometimes
PE LBO (Control)    |  10–15%        |  0.5%     |  12–18 mo      | Yes (standard)
Strategic Acquisition|  10–20%       |  0.5–1%   |  18–24 mo      | Yes
Distressed / Carveout|  20–30%       |  1–2%     |  24 mo         | Hard to place
AI-SaaS (IP-heavy)  |  15–25%        |  0.75%    |  24 mo + IP 36 | Yes, w/ AI endorsmt
IT Services         |  15–20%        |  0.5%     |  18 mo         | Yes
```

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** A $250M AI-copilot acquisition (PE-backed strategic) lands at 17.5% cap ($43.75M), 0.75% tipping basket ($1.875M), 24-month general survival, 36-month IP/AI-specific survival, tax = SOL.
**Trigger:** term-sheet negotiation; rehearse before exclusivity.

### 3.3 W&I Insurance vs Escrow Trade-off

**Purpose:** Choose risk-transfer mechanism that aligns speed-to-close, seller clean-exit, and buyer recovery certainty.

```
Dimension             | Escrow (12–15% / 18 mo) | RWI (2.5–4.5% premium)
----------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------
Upfront Cost          | Zero (opportunity cost) | 2.5–4.5% of limit + retention
Seller Clean Exit     | No (capital trapped)    | Yes (proceeds fully released)
Buyer Counterparty    | Seller (insolvency risk)| A-rated insurer
Claim Speed           | 30–60 days              | 90–180 days (investigation)
Coverage Scope        | Negotiated SPA          | SPA reps + typically broader
Unknown Breaches      | Covered by SPA reps     | Covered (core RWI benefit)
Known Issues          | Can be indemnified      | Excluded (specific indem needed)
Psychological Signal  | Seller commitment       | Diligence quality signal
Typical Deal Size     | Any                     | $25M+ EV
```

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** $500M AI-SaaS target — escrow alone = $75M capital trapped for 18 mo (seller hates); instead, $50M RWI limit at 3.25% ($1.625M premium) + $3.75M retention + $10M holdback on specific AI-training-data indemnity. Seller gets 97% of proceeds at close.
**Trigger:** once buyer's diligence memo is drafted (~2 weeks before signing).

### 3.4 AI/Tech-Specific Reps Checklist (modern addition)

```
[ ] Ownership of all models, weights, fine-tunes, checkpoints
[ ] Training-data provenance log (licences, consents, scraped sources)
[ ] Open-source software BOM + licence compliance (GPL/AGPL exposure)
[ ] No copyright-infringement claims pending/threatened re training data
[ ] Customer-data use within ToS + applicable DPA
[ ] No customer data used to train base models without consent
[ ] Bias / fairness testing documentation
[ ] Model cards + evals maintained
[ ] No regulated-use deployments without compliance (EU AI Act)
[ ] Cyber: no material incident in 36 mo; SOC 2 Type II current
[ ] GDPR/DPDP/CCPA registrations, DPIAs, Art. 30 records
[ ] Sub-processor list accurate; DPAs flow-down
[ ] Export-control classification (EAR/OFAC) for model weights
[ ] Third-party API dependencies (OpenAI, Anthropic) terms current
```

**Trigger:** mandatory for any deal where the target's revenue depends on ML models, customer data, or GenAI features.

## 4. Formulas

### 4.1 Basket — Tipping vs Deductible
**Tipping basket:** once aggregate losses > threshold T, buyer recovers from dollar ZERO.
```
Recoverable = Total_Loss           if Total_Loss ≥ T
Recoverable = 0                    if Total_Loss <  T
```
**Deductible basket:** buyer recovers only the excess over T.
```
Recoverable = max(0, Total_Loss − T)
```
**Example ($500M AI-SaaS, T = $3.75M = 0.75% of EV):**
- Claim of $3M → Tipping: $0; Deductible: $0.
- Claim of $5M → Tipping: $5M; Deductible: $1.25M.
- Difference in buyer recovery at $5M loss = $3.75M.

### 4.2 Cap Scaling by Rep Tier
```
Cap_Fundamental  ≈ 1.00 × EV            (title, authority, cap-table)
Cap_Tax          ≈ 1.00 × EV  or        (separate, SOL-bound)
                   special indemnity, no cap
Cap_Business     ∈ [0.10 , 0.25] × EV   (bulk of Art. 4)
Cap_IP_AI        ∈ [0.15 , 0.30] × EV   (tech-heavy targets)
```
**Example ($500M EV):** Fundamental $500M, Tax $500M, Business $75M (15%), IP/AI $100M (20%).

### 4.3 RWI Premium & Retention
```
Premium  = Rate × Policy_Limit,   Rate ∈ [2.5%, 4.5%]
Retention= r × EV,                r ∈ [0.75%, 1.0%], drops to 0.5% after 12 mo
Minimum Premium floor ~ $150–200K regardless of limit
```
**Example ($500M EV, $50M limit, rate 3.25%):**
- Premium = 0.0325 × $50M = $1.625M
- Retention = 0.0075 × $500M = $3.75M (drops to $2.5M at month 13)
- Break-even vs escrow: at 15% escrow ($75M), seller's 18-mo opportunity cost at 8% WACC ≈ $8.4M → RWI ($1.625M) is ~5× cheaper economically.

### 4.4 Survival Periods
```
General Business Reps:  12–24 months  (AI-SaaS: lean 24)
Fundamental Reps:       6 years → indefinite
Tax Reps:               Statute of Limitations (India: 7 yr; US: 3 yr + 3 yr safety)
IP / Cyber Reps:        24–36 months (tech deals)
AI-specific Reps:       36 months (litigation latency)
Specific Indemnities:   Bespoke (e.g., pending audit resolution + 2 yr)
```
**Example:** $500M AI-SaaS — General 24 mo, Fundamental 6 yr, Tax 7 yr, IP/AI 36 mo, specific EU-AI-Act indemnity 5 yr.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| # | DO | DON'T |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engineer explicit AI/ML/data reps beyond standard IP reps | Rely on generic "owns intellectual property" boilerplate |
| 2 | Negotiate materiality scrape for damages calculation | Accept double-materiality (qualifier for breach AND quantum) |
| 3 | Demand pro-sandbagging (or silent) so diligence findings don't waive claims | Sign anti-sandbagging letting seller off for anything buyer "knew" |
| 4 | Use tipping basket with low threshold (0.5–0.75%) | Accept deductible basket at 1%+ that erases small-to-mid breaches |
| 5 | Carve specific indemnities for known diligence issues with separate cap | Roll known risks into general cap and burn the envelope early |
| 6 | Bind RWI before signing; run insurer's underwriting calls in parallel to DD | Treat RWI as afterthought—insurer exclusions can gut reps on signing day |
| 7 | Define "Loss" to include reasonable investigation costs and exclude punitive, but NOT exclude diminution-in-value | Let seller exclude all consequential / lost-profit / multiple-based damages |
| 8 | Tie knowledge qualifier to specified named officers (CEO, COO, CFO, CTO, CISO, DPO) with "after reasonable inquiry" | Accept vague "seller's knowledge" with no named individuals or inquiry duty |
| 9 | Keep the disclosure schedule a living document updated until signing; require bring-down at close | Accept stale schedules without close-date bring-down certificate |
| 10 | For AI targets, require training-data provenance rep + open-source BOM rep + copyright-infringement rep | Assume IP rep covers "our model was trained on books we didn't license" |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — AI-SaaS Acquisition (Bespoke AI/ML Reps)
**Setup:** $280M acquisition of a B2B AI-copilot company. 70% of enterprise value is the ML model and training corpus.
**Play:** Buyer engineers a dedicated Article 4.18 "AI & Machine Learning Reps" covering: (a) ownership of all weights, checkpoints, and fine-tunes; (b) training-data licence/consent log; (c) no scraped copyrighted content post-2022; (d) no customer data used for base-model training; (e) bias testing completed per NIST AI RMF; (f) open-source BOM with no AGPL contamination; (g) third-party API (OpenAI/Anthropic) ToS compliance.
**Structure:** Cap for AI reps = 25% of EV ($70M), survival 36 months, 0.5% tipping basket. RWI policy $50M with AI-endorsement at 3.75% premium ($1.875M).
**Tools:** DealRoom VDR, Kira for contract review, Luminance for IP clause extraction, Aon/Marsh for RWI.
**Outcome basis:** 18 months post-close, a class-action copyright suit hits the industry; buyer triggers training-data rep + RWI; insurer covers defense + settlement up to policy limit.

### Scenario 2 — IT-Services Firm (Contracts + Employees + Tax)
**Setup:** ₹650 cr acquisition of a 2,000-person India-based IT-services firm serving BFSI clients.
**Play:** Core reps needed — (a) top-25 customer contracts assignable without consent or anti-assignment waivers pre-closing; (b) non-solicit + non-compete of top 200 engineers enforceable under Indian law; (c) no misclassification of contractors; (d) historical GST/TDS/corporate-tax positions accurate; (e) no transfer-pricing exposure on US parent invoicing; (f) SOC 2 / ISO 27001 current, no material cyber incidents.
**Structure:** Fundamental cap 100%, Business cap 18% (~₹117 cr), Tax indemnity separate uncapped, 18-month general survival, 7-year tax survival, deductible basket 0.75% (₹4.87 cr). Specific indemnity carved for a pending ₹22 cr GST dispute.
**Tools:** Ironclad CLM for customer-contract registry audit, DealRoom for employee-contract review, local tax counsel + Big-4 tax DD.
**Outcome basis:** Post-close GST dispute resolves at ₹18 cr; recovered via specific indemnity without touching general cap.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE: Knowledge-Qualified Reps Gut Recovery
**Setup:** ₹400 cr acquisition of a mid-market IT product company. Buyer's in-house counsel, pressured by seller's banker to close, accepts "to seller's knowledge" qualifier on ~80% of business reps with no named-officer list and no "after reasonable inquiry" duty.
**What goes wrong:** 14 months post-close, tax authorities raise a ₹42 cr demand for under-reported GST on cross-border SaaS delivery over 2 fiscal years. Buyer files claim. Seller invokes knowledge qualifier: the CFO (who left pre-close) "didn't know" of the exposure, and no one else signed reps. Claim fails.
**Quantified loss:** ₹42 cr tax + ₹8 cr penalty + ₹3 cr litigation = ₹53 cr unrecovered. Additionally, RWI policy had excluded tax-knowledge reps because of the broad knowledge qualifier—insurer denies.
**Root cause:** (a) knowledge qualifier without named-officer list; (b) no "after reasonable inquiry" duty; (c) no materiality scrape; (d) no separate tax indemnity carved as uncapped; (e) RWI underwriter exclusion not negotiated; (f) no specific indemnity for cross-border tax despite DD flag.
**Lesson:** On tax and regulatory reps, REFUSE knowledge qualifiers or bind them to named officers with reasonable-inquiry duty, and ALWAYS carve tax as a separate indemnity.

**Tools stack across scenarios:** DealRoom VDR (data room), Ironclad CLM (contract lifecycle + auto-extraction of assignment clauses), RWI brokers — Aon, Marsh, Lockton (policy placement), Kira & Luminance (AI-driven rep + contract review).

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Map** rep categories to the target's value drivers—produce a "Reps Coverage Matrix" before LOI drafting (IP%, data%, customers%, people%, tax exposure%).
2. **Draft** a first-cut SPA Article 4 with explicit AI/ML/data/cyber reps embedded—do NOT wait for outside counsel template.
3. **Engage** RWI brokers (Aon/Marsh/Lockton) at LOI stage; run insurer underwriting calls in parallel with DD so exclusions emerge early.
4. **Negotiate** the cap/basket/survival triplet on the term sheet, not in SPA markup; quantify with the 4.1–4.4 formulas in writing.
5. **Build** the disclosure schedule as a living artifact in DealRoom; require weekly reconciliation between DD findings and schedule items.
6. **Carve** specific indemnities for each red-flag item DD surfaces; separate cap, separate survival, outside RWI scope.
7. **Bind** pro-sandbagging (or silence) language and a full materiality scrape in the indemnity article before exclusivity expires.
8. **Rehearse** post-close claim mechanics—who files, who notifies insurer, what evidence package—so the first 30 days aren't improvised.

## 9. Quick-Recall Card

- Reps = statements of fact; Warranties = promises they're true; breach → indemnity claim under SPA.
- Three-tier architecture: Fundamental (~100% cap, indefinite) / Business (10–25% cap, 12–24 mo) / Tax (separate, SOL).
- Basket: Tipping = recover from $0 once crossed; Deductible = recover only the excess—fight for tipping.
- RWI premium ~2.5–4.5% of limit; retention ~0.75–1% of EV; lets seller exit clean and buyer recover from A-rated insurer.
- For AI/tech targets, engineer explicit training-data, open-source, model-ownership, cyber, and data-protection reps—generic IP reps WILL fail.
- Most costly mistakes: accepting anti-sandbagging + broad knowledge qualifier + deductible basket + no materiality scrape.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: **"Which tech/IP/data/AI risks in this target must I convert into explicit, sandbagging-proof, RWI-covered reps before signing—and what cap/basket/survival envelope makes the residual risk survivable?"**
