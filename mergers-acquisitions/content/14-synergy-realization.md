# Synergy Realization

## Overview

Synergy realization is the work of actually delivering the cost savings and revenue gains that were promised in the deal model. It turns forecast numbers into real P&L impact through specific actions, owners, and deadlines.

---

## Why It Matters

Most deals underperform because synergies stay on paper. Tracking and delivering synergies in real numbers protects the return on investment, justifies the premium paid, and builds investor trust for future deals.

## Key Principles

- Break synergies into concrete projects with owners and dates
- Separate cost synergies (quick, controllable) from revenue synergies (slower, riskier)
- Track synergies in the regular P&L, not a separate spreadsheet
- Book the cost to achieve (severance, IT merges) against the benefit
- Report progress openly to the steering committee and the board

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cost Synergy** | Savings from cutting duplicate costs |
| **Revenue Synergy** | Extra sales from cross-selling and reach |
| **Cost to Achieve (CTA)** | One-time spending needed to capture synergies |
| **Run-Rate Synergy** | Annualised benefit once fully captured |
| **Synergy Tracker** | Tool that monitors delivery of each synergy project |

## Use Case

A buyer commits to Rs 500 crore of annual cost synergies in three years. The CFO sets up a tracker of 80 projects, assigns each to a VP, reviews progress monthly, and reports year-on-year savings to analysts.

## Scenario

> A consumer goods buyer promised 5% margin uplift from a merger. By closing 3 of 11 plants, merging distributors, and standardising SKUs, it captured Rs 800 crore of cost synergies in 24 months and hit its guidance to investors.

## Examples

- A bank merger closes 150 overlapping branches to save Rs 300 crore annually.
- A tech deal cross-sells the target's analytics to 40% of the buyer's existing clients.

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Synergy realisation is the discipline of actually delivering the cost savings and revenue gains that were baked into the deal model — turning forecast line items into real, booked P&L impact through named projects, owners and deadlines. An IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader must track these inside the actual operating P&L (not a parallel "synergy spreadsheet") because most deals underperform precisely because synergies stay on paper — infra consolidations slip, cross-sell never lands, CTA balloons. The core decision this framework forces: for every rupee promised in the model, who owns it, when does it book, what does it cost to achieve, and what do we write off as value leakage?

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Synergy Classification Matrix — (Revenue × Cost × Financial × Tax) × (Probability × Timing)
**Purpose:** force the deal model to separate easy, controllable synergies from aspirational ones before committing external guidance.

```
                  | HIGH PROB (>70%)    | MED (40-70%)        | LOW (<40%)
  ----------------+---------------------+---------------------+-------------------
  Cost  (0-12 mo) | Vendor consolidation| Real estate closures| Org redesign wave 2
  Cost  (12-24 mo)| Infra consolidation | Procurement renegos | Shared services
  Rev   (0-12 mo) | Price harmonisation | Bundling            | Geo expansion
  Rev   (12-36 mo)| Existing cross-sell | New-logo cross-sell | New-product JV
  Fin / Tax       | Debt refi           | Tax structure       | Credit rating upgrade
```
**Components:** synergy class, probability band, timing band, owner VP, CTA estimate.
**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** an AI-SaaS roll-up buys a Databricks-heavy analytics firm. High-prob/fast cost = consolidating Snowflake + Databricks contracts. Low-prob/slow revenue = cross-selling target's vertical AI models to acquirer's BFSI logos (long procurement cycle).
**Trigger:** use at Day-0 to haircut the deal-model number before announcing to the Street.

### 3.2 Synergy Tracker Architecture (ASCII)
**Purpose:** hard-wire each synergy to a project, owner, baseline and P&L line so the CFO can audit it monthly.

```
  SYNERGY (₹cr)  ->  PROJECT          -> OWNER     -> BASELINE  -> MILESTONES     -> P&L LINE
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  S-01: Cloud    -> Merge AWS orgs    -> VP Infra  -> ₹42cr/yr  -> M+3 consolidate-> COGS/Hosting
  S-02: SaaS mix -> Kill dup Jira/GHE -> CIO       -> ₹8cr/yr   -> M+2 sunset     -> G&A/IT
  S-03: Xsell    -> BFSI account plan -> CRO       -> 0         -> M+6 1st booking-> Revenue-NewLogo
  S-04: HQ RE    -> Sublease floor 12 -> VP RE     -> ₹6cr/yr   -> M+9 sublease   -> G&A/RE
  S-05: Severance-> Wave-1 RIF (CTA)  -> CHRO      -> ₹24cr/yr  -> M+1 notify     -> Opex (savings)
```
**Components:** unique ID, project, owner, baseline ₹, milestones, P&L mapping, CTA, RAG.
**Example:** for a 3-yr ₹500 cr programme, an 80-row tracker with one VP per row and monthly reviews is industry-standard.
**Trigger:** stand up before Day-1; freeze baseline within 30 days of close.

### 3.3 Cost vs Revenue Synergy Haircut Guide
**Purpose:** force realism when plugging synergies into the deal model or investor guidance.

```
                     | Gross target | Typical capture | Suggested haircut
  Cost synergy       | 100          | 60-80%          | 20-40%
  Revenue synergy    | 100          | 20-40%          | 60-80%
  Financial synergy  | 100          | 70-90%          | 10-30%
  Tax synergy        | 100          | 60-80%          | 20-40%
```
**Components:** synergy class, gross, haircut %, net committed.
**Example:** a consumer-goods deal pitched ₹1,000 cr cost + ₹600 cr revenue synergies gross. Post-haircut: ₹700 cr cost (70%) + ₹180 cr revenue (30%) = ₹880 cr committed externally. Actual delivered ₹800 cr over 24 months — investor guidance held.
**Trigger:** any time leadership wants to commit a gross number externally.

### 3.4 CTA vs Benefit Payback Curve (ASCII)
**Purpose:** show steerco the "J-curve" — synergies cost money before they pay.

```
  ₹ cr
   +500 |                                          . . . . Cumulative savings
   +400 |                                 . . . .
   +300 |                        . . . .
   +200 |               . . . .
   +100 |      . . . .
      0 |._____________________________________________________________
   -100 |  .       _ _ _                                  Cumulative CTA
   -200 | . .  _ _
         M+0  M+3  M+6  M+9  M+12 M+15 M+18 M+21 M+24
                              ^ breakeven ~ M+14
```
**Components:** cumulative CTA (downward), cumulative savings (upward), crossover month.
**Example:** AI-SaaS deal: ₹90 cr CTA (severance + migration), ₹210 cr/yr run-rate savings — payback ~5 months after full run-rate hit.
**Trigger:** use when board challenges "why are we still spending?" in the first year.

## 4. Formulas

### 4.1 Run-Rate Synergy
`Run-Rate Synergy = Monthly realised benefit (steady state) × 12`
**Threshold:** run-rate should match committed by end of Year-2.
**Example:** AI-SaaS deal — infra consolidation saves ₹17.5 cr/month by M+18 → run-rate = ₹210 cr/yr.

### 4.2 Realised Synergy (period)
`Realised Synergy (period) = Actual cost saved OR incremental revenue attributable to deal, in that period`
**Threshold:** must tie back to specific P&L lines; unattributable = leakage.
**Example:** Q3 FY27 realised cost synergy = ₹48 cr (vendor + infra); realised revenue synergy = ₹11 cr (cross-sell bookings ACV-to-revenue).

### 4.3 Synergy NPV
`Synergy NPV = Σ [(Gross synergy_t × Realisation %_t) − CTA_t] / (1 + WACC)^t`
**Threshold:** Synergy NPV should ≥ control premium paid; else premium was unjustified.
**Example:** Gross ₹500 cr/yr, realisation 75%, CTA ₹150 cr front-loaded, WACC 11%, 5-year horizon → NPV ≈ ₹1,180 cr. If premium paid was ₹900 cr → deal justifies.

### 4.4 Synergy Capture Rate
`Capture Rate % = Realised synergy / Committed synergy`
**Threshold:** ≥ 90% at 24 months for cost; ≥ 50% at 24 months for revenue.
**Example:** Committed ₹500 cr by M+24; realised ₹460 cr → 92% capture. Revenue side committed ₹120 cr; realised ₹55 cr → 46% capture (within norm).

### 4.5 Value Leakage
`Value Leakage = Committed − Realised − Justified Deviations`
**Threshold:** leakage > 10% triggers steerco root-cause review.
**Example:** Committed ₹500 cr; Realised ₹420 cr; Justified deviations (FX, scope change) ₹30 cr → Leakage = ₹50 cr (10%) — borderline, deep dive required.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| # | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Book realised synergies into the actual operating P&L line (COGS, G&A, Revenue-NewLogo). | Don't run a side-tracker outside the audited P&L — it creates phantom savings. |
| 2 | Haircut revenue synergies to 20–40% capture before guiding externally. | Don't book revenue synergies at 100% pre-close to justify the price. |
| 3 | Disclose CTA as a separate, transparent line to the board. | Don't hide CTA inside BAU opex or capitalise aggressively to flatter EBITDA. |
| 4 | Freeze baselines within 30 days and lock goalposts. | Don't let workstreams re-baseline mid-year to hit their target. |
| 5 | Model dis-synergies explicitly — customer churn, regretted attrition, revenue dis-synergy from overlap. | Don't ignore dis-synergies; they are the silent killer of deal economics. |
| 6 | Track synergies for at least 36 months post-close with monthly steerco cadence. | Don't declare victory and shut the tracker after 12 months; long-tail slippage is common. |
| 7 | Attribute every tracker row to a named VP with comp tied to delivery. | Don't leave rows with "IMO" or "TBD" as owner — orphan synergies never land. |
| 8 | Reconcile top-down deal-model number to bottom-up workstream plans monthly. | Don't accept "we'll find it later" — unreconciled gaps become leakage. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — AI-SaaS integration (cost-led)
An analytics platform acquires a vertical AI startup. Infra audit finds 3 overlapping AWS orgs + duplicate Snowflake + Databricks contracts.
- **Actions:** consolidate into one AWS payer account; renegotiate Snowflake EDP; sunset Databricks for non-ML workloads; merge observability (Datadog vs New Relic → single).
- **Synergy:** ₹85 cr/yr cost synergy; CTA ₹20 cr (migration + 2 quarters of parallel run).
- **Tracking:** each project has a VP Infra owner in Jira; monthly run-rate rolled into COGS-Hosting in Adaptive Insights; dashboard in Looker to steerco.

### Scenario 2 — IT-services cross-sell (revenue-led)
A Tier-1 IT services firm acquires a boutique data-engineering consultancy. White-space mapping shows 40% of acquirer's BFSI accounts have no data-eng spend.
- **Actions:** Salesforce opportunity tagging "Deal-Synergy-Yes/No"; joint account plans for top 60 BFSI logos; incentive accelerator for rep bringing combined bookings.
- **Synergy:** target ₹220 cr incremental ACV over 24 months; haircut at 30% = ₹66 cr committed; actual 18-mo realised ₹48 cr (capture 73% of committed, better than haircut — good).
- **Tracking:** incremental bookings flagged in Salesforce, reconciled monthly to finance; cross-sell dashboard in Tableau.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE: over-promised revenue synergies
A SaaS roll-up committed 80% capture on ₹400 cr revenue synergy from cross-selling target's CPQ to acquirer's ERP base.
- **What went wrong:** CPQ–ERP integration slipped 9 months (API schema mismatch); sales reps not trained on combined pitch; no white-space map; no owner beyond "Head of Growth."
- **Quantified loss:** shortfall vs deal model = ₹240 cr over 24 months; CTA overrun ₹60 cr. Capture rate = 25% vs 80% promised.
- **Market reaction:** stock de-rated 14% on Q3 earnings when management lowered guidance → ~₹3,800 cr market-cap loss.
- **Root cause:** revenue synergies plugged at gross, no realisation haircut, no integration-playbook for CPQ merge, no named VP owner with comp on the line.

**Tools stack:** Anaplan / Adaptive Insights (synergy model), Salesforce (white-space + cross-sell), Snowflake / Databricks (cost telemetry), Jira / Asana (project owners + milestones), Looker / Tableau (steerco dashboards).

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Freeze** a Day-0 baseline P&L per BU and per cost line in Adaptive Insights (artifact: baseline pack signed by both CFOs).
2. **Decompose** the deal-model synergy into a tracker of 30–100 rows, each with ID, owner VP, baseline, CTA, milestones, P&L mapping (artifact: Synergy Tracker in Anaplan).
3. **Assign** a named VP/Director to each row with quarterly comp tied to delivery (artifact: RACI + comp-plan addendum).
4. **Stand up** a monthly Synergy Steerco chaired by the CFO + Integration Lead; require RAG status + run-rate + cumulative CTA per row (artifact: steerco deck template).
5. **Wire** realised synergies directly into the monthly management accounts — no parallel spreadsheets (artifact: mapping table Synergy-ID → GL account).
6. **Publish** a quarterly synergy scorecard to the board and (in aggregate) to analysts; report committed vs realised vs capture vs leakage (artifact: board scorecard PDF).
7. **Run** a 12-month post-close audit by Internal Audit on realisation and CTA classification; remediate leakage (artifact: audit memo).
8. **Extend** tracking to 36 months — revenue synergies and run-rate drift often surface late (artifact: 36-mo closeout report).

## 9. Quick-Recall Card

- Synergy realisation = turning the model's promises into real, booked P&L through projects, owners, deadlines.
- Cost synergies capture 60–80%; revenue synergies capture 20–40% — haircut before guiding.
- Always book CTA transparently; never capitalise it to flatter EBITDA.
- Track inside the actual P&L, not a side deck; 30–100 row tracker, one VP per row, monthly steerco.
- Value Leakage = Committed − Realised − Justified Deviations; leakage > 10% triggers root-cause.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: "For every rupee of synergy we promised, who owns it, when does it hit which P&L line, and what is our committed capture rate after a realistic haircut?"
