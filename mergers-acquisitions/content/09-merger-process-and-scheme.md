# Merger Process and Scheme of Arrangement

## Overview

A scheme of arrangement is a court-approved process used to legally combine two companies or restructure one. It gives a structured route to transfer shares, assets, and liabilities and is binding on all shareholders and creditors once approved.

---

## Why It Matters

A scheme is the standard way for Indian mergers, demergers, and amalgamations to get legal certainty. Missing a step can delay approval by months, expose directors to liability, and give dissenting shareholders grounds to block the deal.

## Key Principles

- Draft the scheme early with tax, legal, and accounting experts
- Get board approval and independent valuation reports
- File with NCLT and comply with notice and meeting rules
- Seek approvals from SEBI, stock exchanges, and CCI as needed
- Plan the appointed date, effective date, and record date carefully

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Scheme of Arrangement** | Court-approved reorganisation under the Companies Act |
| **NCLT** | National Company Law Tribunal that approves schemes |
| **Appointed Date** | Date from which the scheme is deemed effective |
| **Effective Date** | Date the scheme takes legal effect after all approvals |
| **Share Swap Ratio** | Number of acquirer shares given per target share |

## Use Case

Two listed Indian firms merging draft a scheme with a share-swap ratio of 3:5, get shareholder and creditor approval, and file with NCLT. Once approved, the target dissolves and all its assets move to the acquirer.

## Scenario

> A listed financial services group merged two subsidiaries via a scheme of arrangement. With careful planning of the appointed date, the group saved Rs 45 crore in tax and preserved accumulated losses for future set-off.

## Examples

- An Indian listed company demerges its FMCG division into a new listed entity via a scheme.
- Two group NBFCs amalgamate via an NCLT scheme to simplify regulatory compliance.

## 1. Topic Snapshot
A Scheme of Arrangement is the NCLT-approved, court-sanctioned process under Sections 230–234 of the Companies Act 2013 that binds every shareholder and creditor to a merger, demerger, or amalgamation once approved. An IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader must know this when restructuring group companies (e.g., collapsing a layered AI-SaaS holdco for IPO readiness), executing a tech-target carve-in, or simplifying NBFC/subsidiary stacks post-acquisition — because a missing step delays sanction 6–12 months and exposes directors to Section 232(8) liability. Decision: choose between a full scheme (Section 230), a Section 233 fast-track merger (small/group companies), or a cross-border Section 234 route, and lock Appointed/Effective/Record dates to preserve tax neutrality under Section 2(1B).

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Scheme Timeline
**Purpose:** Visualize the 9–12 month critical path so product/ops leaders can plan data-cutover, customer comms, and integration sprints.

```
Concept
   |
   v
Valuation Reports (2 Registered Valuers + Fairness Opinion)
   |
   v
Board Approval (Transferor + Transferee)
   |
   v
Stock Exchange / SEBI Observation Letter (for listed cos)
   |
   v
NCLT Application — First Motion (Form CAA-2)
   |
   v
Meetings (Equity Shareholders + Secured + Unsecured Creditors)
   |
   v
NCLT — Second Motion (Form CAA-5) + RD/OL/IT Dept observations
   |
   v
Sanction Order
   |
   v
INC-28 Filing --> Effective Date --> Record Date --> Share Issue
```

**Components:** Valuation, Board, SEBI, NCLT 1st, Meetings, NCLT 2nd, Sanction, INC-28.
**IT/AI/Product/Consulting example:** SaaS parent acquiring an AI-services subsidiary uses this timeline to schedule product roadmap freeze at Appointed Date and customer-contract novation at Effective Date.
**Trigger:** Any intra-group restructuring > ₹100 cr or involving a listed entity.

### 3.2 Scheme vs Section 233 Fast-Track Decision Matrix
**Purpose:** Pick the cheapest legal vehicle for intra-group cleanups.

```
                        | Full Scheme (Sec. 230–232) | Fast-Track (Sec. 233)
------------------------|----------------------------|-----------------------
Eligible parties        | All companies              | Small cos / holding-WOS
Approving authority     | NCLT                       | Central Govt (RD)
Time to close           | 8–12 months                | 3–5 months
Approx cost             | ₹50L – ₹5 cr               | ₹10–40 L
Creditor meeting        | Mandatory                  | Not always needed
Public notice           | Yes                        | RD-level only
Appeal risk             | Higher                     | Lower
```

**Components:** Eligibility, Authority, Time, Cost, Creditor, Notice, Appeal.
**IT/AI/Product/Consulting example:** Consulting firm's two wholly-owned IT subsidiaries merge via Section 233 to save ~9 months and ~₹3 cr vs a full scheme.
**Trigger:** Party eligibility check at scoping.

### 3.3 Stakeholder Approval Map
**Purpose:** Ensure no approval gate is missed before NCLT second motion.

```
                  Shareholders | Creditors | Regulators | Exchanges | SEBI | CCI
Full Scheme          75% val   |  75% val  |  RD/OL/IT  |  NOC      |  Obs |  If thresh.
Demerger (listed)    75% val   |  75% val  |  RD/OL/IT  |  NOC      |  Obs |  Usually N/A
Fast-Track (S.233)   90% val   |  9/10 val |  RD only   |  Intimate |  N/A |  N/A
Cross-border (S.234) 75% val   |  75% val  |  RBI + RD  |  NOC      |  Obs |  If thresh.
```

**Components:** 6 stakeholder rails × 4 scheme types.
**IT/AI/Product/Consulting example:** AI-SaaS holdco merging Indian subsidiary into parent confirms CCI not triggered (below ₹2,000 cr asset/₹6,000 cr turnover thresholds), avoiding a 150-day Phase-1 review.
**Trigger:** Kick-off of legal workstream.

## 4. Formulas

### 4.1 Share Swap Ratio
```
Share Swap Ratio = Value per Target Share / Value per Acquirer Share
```
**Threshold:** Must be backed by two registered valuer reports + fairness opinion for listed cos (SEBI LODR).
**Example:** SaaS parent (value ₹500/share) merges IT-services subsidiary (value ₹300/share). Swap ratio = 300/500 = **3:5** (3 acquirer shares for every 5 target shares). If target has 10,00,000 shares outstanding, acquirer issues 6,00,000 new shares.

### 4.2 Tax Impact — Section 2(1B) Amalgamation Neutrality
```
Tax-neutral if ALL three conditions hold:
(a) All property of transferor -> transferee;
(b) All liabilities of transferor -> transferee;
(c) Shareholders holding >= 3/4 (by value) of transferor shares become transferee shareholders.
```
**Threshold:** If any condition fails, capital gains tax + loss of Section 72A carry-forward.
**Example:** In scenario from source, careful Appointed Date planning preserved ₹45 cr in tax savings + carried-forward losses under Section 72A (7-year window for amalgamation of industrial undertakings).

### 4.3 Scheme Cost Budget
```
Scheme Cost = NCLT fees + Valuation (2) + Legal + Merchant Banker + Stamp Duty + Printing/Advertisement
```
**Threshold:** Stamp duty can be 0.1%–7% of consideration depending on state (Maharashtra ~0.7%, Karnataka ~3%).
**Example (SaaS parent + IT-services sub merger, ₹800 cr deal, Maharashtra):**
- NCLT filing + counsel: ₹1.2 cr
- Two valuer reports + fairness opinion: ₹60 L
- Merchant banker: ₹90 L
- Stamp duty (0.7%): ₹5.6 cr
- Advertisement + printing: ₹15 L
- **Total ~₹8.5 cr**, 10–11 month timeline.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Draft the scheme with tax + legal + accounting in the same room on day one. | Don't let legal finalize clauses without tax reviewing Section 2(1B) conditions. |
| Commission two IBBI-registered valuers + fairness opinion upfront. | Don't rely on a single internal DCF for the swap ratio — SEBI will reject. |
| Pre-consult stock exchanges and SEBI before filing first motion. | Don't file NCLT petition without NOCs from BSE/NSE; it will bounce. |
| Choose Appointed Date to preserve carried-forward losses (Section 72A). | Don't pick a random Appointed Date that splits a financial year mid-quarter without modeling tax impact. |
| Use Section 233 fast-track for WOS/small-co cleanups. | Don't default to full scheme when parties qualify for fast-track — wastes 6 months + crores. |
| Brief the Product/Engineering org on Effective Date for data migration cut-over. | Don't novate customer contracts before Effective Date — void under Indian Contract Act. |
| Run CCI threshold test early (asset/turnover). | Don't ignore CCI; gun-jumping penalty up to 1% of combined turnover. |
| File INC-28 within 30 days of sanction. | Don't delay INC-28; scheme doesn't take effect, and Effective Date slips. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — AI-SaaS Holdco Merges Indian Subsidiary into Parent for IPO Readiness
AI-SaaS group with a Singapore holdco and an Indian operating subsidiary wants a clean Indian listing structure. They execute a Section 234 cross-border inbound merger: Singapore parent's IP and contracts transfer to the Indian entity at Appointed Date 1-April-2026. RBI + NCLT approvals take 11 months. Post-merger, DRHP filed with a single Indian entity, eliminating minority-interest footnotes and improving comparable multiples by ~1.2x.
**Tools:** NCLT e-filing, MCA21, SEBI SCORES, VDR (Intralinks/Datasite), valuation software (Kroll/Aranca).

### Scenario 2 — Consulting Group Demerges Boutique AI Advisory into Listed Entity
Big-4-style consulting holdco demerges its boutique AI advisory arm into a new listed entity via Section 230 scheme. Swap ratio 1:1 to existing shareholders. Demerger preserves Section 2(19AA) tax neutrality: all assets/liabilities of undertaking transfer, 3/4 shareholders get shares. Boutique unit trades at a 3.5x revenue multiple vs parent's 1.8x — ₹1,200 cr market-cap unlock.
**Tools:** NCLT e-filing, SEBI LODR filings, merchant banker (Axis Capital), legal VDR.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE: Wrong Appointed Date Kills Tax Neutrality
A product company merging a loss-making AI-research subsidiary picks Appointed Date of 31-March-2025 to "close the year clean," but the scheme doesn't transfer 100% of liabilities (an ESOP trust liability is carved out). Section 2(1B)(ii) fails → amalgamation not tax-neutral. Capital gains of ₹180 cr triggered on asset transfer + loss of ₹90 cr Section 72A carry-forward. **Incremental tax hit: ~₹68 cr + lost shield worth ~₹23 cr NPV = ₹91 cr of value destroyed.** Could have been avoided with a 2-week cross-functional review.
**Tools:** NCLT e-filing, MCA21, IT Dept observation letter, tax-modeling in Excel/Alteryx.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Scope** the restructuring thesis — write a 2-page Scheme Rationale Memo (IPO readiness, regulatory simplification, synergy capture).
2. **Engage** two IBBI-registered valuers + merchant banker for fairness opinion; lock Appointed Date with tax counsel.
3. **Draft** the Scheme Document + Board Resolutions; circulate to audit committee and independent directors.
4. **File** NOC requests with BSE/NSE and SEBI observation letter application (if listed) via LODR Regulation 37.
5. **Petition** NCLT with First Motion (Form CAA-2) + Scheme + valuation reports; obtain meeting-convening order.
6. **Convene** equity shareholders' + secured + unsecured creditors' meetings; obtain 75% by value approvals; file Chairman's Report.
7. **Second Motion** — respond to RD/OL/IT/SEBI observations; attend NCLT hearing; obtain Sanction Order.
8. **Implement** — file INC-28 within 30 days → Effective Date → Record Date → share issuance → system cutovers (ERP, CRM, product SKUs, cloud tenancies) → customer/vendor novations.

## 9. Quick-Recall Card
- A Scheme of Arrangement (Sec. 230–232) is the NCLT-sanctioned master vehicle for Indian mergers, demergers, and amalgamations; Sec. 233 is the fast-track cousin; Sec. 234 handles cross-border.
- The deal turns on three dates — Appointed (tax/accounting), Effective (legal, post INC-28), and Record (share issuance).
- Swap ratio must sit on two IBBI-registered valuer reports + a fairness opinion for listed entities.
- Tax neutrality under Section 2(1B) requires all assets, all liabilities, and ≥75% shareholder continuity — miss one and capital gains + Section 72A carry-forward shield both die.
- Stakeholder gates: 75% shareholders + 75% creditors (by value), SEBI, exchanges, RD/OL/IT, CCI if thresholds hit.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: **"Which scheme route (Sec. 230 full / Sec. 233 fast-track / Sec. 234 cross-border) and which Appointed Date maximize post-restructuring value after tax, timeline, and integration-risk costs?"**
