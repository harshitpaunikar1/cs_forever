# Term Sheets and Important Provisions

## Overview

A term sheet is a short document that sets out the main commercial terms of a deal before lawyers draft the full agreement. Key provisions include price, structure, conditions, exclusivity, and what happens if things go wrong.

---

## Why It Matters

The term sheet sets the direction for every document that follows. Getting it wrong means weeks of re-negotiation, wasted legal fees, and sometimes a broken deal; getting it right saves time and aligns both sides early.

## Key Principles

- Keep the term sheet short and focused on deal-makers
- Fix price, structure, and conditions before moving to drafting
- Use exclusivity to protect the buyer's diligence investment
- Flag walk-away rights and break fees clearly
- State which terms are binding and which are indicative

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Term Sheet / LOI** | Non-binding summary of main deal terms |
| **Exclusivity** | Period where seller cannot talk to other buyers |
| **Conditions Precedent (CPs)** | Events that must happen before closing |
| **Break Fee** | Payment if one side walks away |
| **MAC Clause** | Allows buyer to exit if something very bad happens |

## Use Case

A tech buyer signs a 3-page term sheet fixing price at $100M, structure as a share purchase, a 60-day exclusivity period, and closing subject to CCI approval and key-employee retention.

## Scenario

> A global buyer signed a term sheet with a break fee of $10M for either side. After diligence it found a pending lawsuit, triggered the MAC clause, and walked away without paying the break fee, saving a far costlier mistake.

## Examples

- A VC term sheet fixes valuation, board seats, liquidation preference, and anti-dilution.
- An M&A term sheet lists price, CPs, exclusivity, and long-stop date on one page.

## 1. Topic Snapshot
A term sheet is a short (usually 2-5 page) document that locks the main commercial, legal, governance and protection terms of a deal before lawyers draft the full SPA/APA — its provisions (price, structure, exclusivity, CPs, MAC, break fee) effectively pre-decide 80% of deal outcomes. For an IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader, engineering the term sheet means translating messy strategic intent (talent retention, IP ownership, integration risk, regulatory posture) into binding and non-binding clauses *before* handing to counsel — because every ambiguity here becomes weeks of re-negotiation later. Decision: which handful of provisions do you treat as binding dealmakers (exclusivity, break fee, confidentiality) and which as indicative (headline price, structure), and how do you make that explicit on page one?

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Term Sheet Anatomy
**Purpose:** Map every clause in a term sheet to one of four buckets so nothing is missed.

```
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
|   COMMERCIAL       |     LEGAL          |   GOVERNANCE       |   PROTECTION       |
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
| Price (headline)   | Conditions Prec.   | Board seats        | Reps & Warranties  |
| Structure (share/  | Long-stop date     | Veto matters       | Indemnity cap      |
|   asset/merger)    | Break / Rev.Break  | Observer rights    | Basket / survival  |
| Price mechanism    | MAC clause         | Info rights        | RWI                |
|   (locked-box /    | Exclusivity        | Drag / Tag         | Escrow / holdback  |
|    completion acc.)| Confidentiality    | ROFR               | Specific perform.  |
| Working capital    | Binding vs non-    | Approval matrix    | Non-compete / NSE  |
| Earn-out           |   binding label    | Key-employee ret.  | IP assignment      |
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
```
**Components:** 4 columns × 6-8 rows. **Worked example:** AI-SaaS acquisition — Commercial = $500M, share purchase, locked-box; Legal = 60-day exclusivity, CCI + key-employee retention CPs, $15M reverse break fee; Governance = 2 buyer board seats in holdco; Protection = 15% escrow, $50M RWI cap, 24-month survival, 3-year non-compete on founders. **Trigger:** Use when drafting term sheet page 1 or auditing a draft for gaps.

### 3.2 Binding vs Non-Binding Map
**Purpose:** Explicitly label each clause so no party is surprised about enforceability.

```
BINDING (enforceable on signing)        | NON-BINDING (indicative only)
----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------
- Exclusivity / No-Shop                 | - Headline price
- Confidentiality / NDA                 | - Deal structure (share vs asset)
- Break fee / Reverse break fee         | - Price mechanism choice
- Governing law + jurisdiction          | - Working-capital target
- Costs allocation                      | - Earn-out metrics
- No-solicit of employees               | - R&W scope
- Public announcement control           | - Board composition
- Standstill (if any)                   | - Timing / long-stop date
```
**Worked example:** IT-services rollup term sheet — 45-day exclusivity (binding), confidentiality extending 3 years (binding), $2M break fee (binding); but $180M headline, locked-box mechanism, and 24-month earn-out are non-binding subject to diligence. **Trigger:** Before signing, confirm the clause-by-clause label — a single sentence per section ("This Section 9 is binding; all other sections are non-binding").

### 3.3 MAC Clause Design Matrix
**Purpose:** Calibrate MAC trigger so it reflects real deal-risk without giving buyer a free out.

```
                  | Quantitative trigger | Qualitative trigger    | Carve-outs
------------------+----------------------+------------------------+------------------
What              | >X% drop in EBITDA / | "material adverse"     | Industry-wide,
                  | revenue / ARR        | change to business,    | macro, war/pandemic,
                  |                      | financial condition    | changes in law,
                  |                      |                        | announcement itself
Typical threshold | 20-40% EBITDA drop   | Subjective; judge-made | Durationally
                  | sustained 2+ quarters|                        | significant test
Use for           | Financial risk       | Catch-all              | Fair allocation
IT/AI lens        | ARR churn cliff;     | Key-model regulatory   | OpenAI/EU-AI-Act
                  | usage collapse       | ban; data-breach       | industry-wide
                  |                      | headline               | carve-out
```
**Worked example:** AI-SaaS deal — MAC defined as >30% ARR reduction sustained two quarters OR loss of top-3 customers OR material regulatory action against the core model; carve-outs for general market downturn, EU AI Act industry effects, and buyer-caused disruption. **Trigger:** Use at term-sheet drafting and re-check pre-signing; litigation history (Akorn v. Fresenius, Cigna/Anthem) shows MAC wins are rare without numeric precision.

### 3.4 (Optional) CP Dependency Tree
**Purpose:** Sequence CPs so unsatisfiable ones are flagged early.

```
[Regulatory approvals] ----+
   CCI / HSR / FDI / EU    |
                           v
                    [Financing]  ---- committed debt / equity
                           |
                           v
              [Key-Employee Retention] -- signed retention letters
                           |
                           v
              [Third-party consents] -- customer change-of-control
                           |
                           v
                       [Closing]
```
**Worked example:** IT-services rollup with 8 target acquisitions — regulatory is parallel, but key-customer consent (change-of-control in MSAs) sits on critical path; long-stop set to slowest-reg-authority + 30 days. **Trigger:** Any deal with 3+ CPs — visualise dependencies so single-node failure is visible.

## 4. Formulas

### 4.1 Break Fee as % of Deal Value
- **Formula:** Break Fee = Deal Value × %, typical 1-4% (seller-side), 4-8% (reverse, buyer-side where regulatory risk dominates).
- **Thresholds:** <1% may be unenforceable as not genuine pre-estimate; >4% may face "penalty" challenge in some jurisdictions.
- **Example (AI-SaaS, $500M deal):** Seller break fee at 3% = $15M; reverse break fee at 6% (antitrust-heavy) = $30M. Scenario: buyer regulatory clearance fails, buyer pays $30M RBF; seller retains $30M to offset 9-month lost-alternative cost.

### 4.2 Indemnity Cap % of Purchase Price
- **Formula:** Cap = Price × %. Typical general cap 10-25%; fundamental reps (title, authority, cap table, IP) 50-100% or uncapped.
- **Thresholds:** Below 10% seller-friendly; above 25% buyer-friendly; RWI allows compressed cap (often 0.5-1% with RWI sitting above).
- **Example ($500M deal):** General cap 15% = $75M; fundamental cap 100% = $500M; with $50M RWI primary layer + 1% seller escrow cap = $5M + RWI.

### 4.3 Basket — Tipping vs Deductible
- **Tipping basket:** any claim below $X ignored; once aggregate breaches cross $X, buyer recovers from dollar one.
- **Deductible basket:** buyer only recovers the portion *above* $X.
- **Thresholds:** Baskets typically 0.5-1% of purchase price; mini-basket (per-claim) ~0.05%.
- **Example ($500M deal):** 0.75% tipping = $3.75M; once breaches total $3.8M, buyer claims full $3.8M. Deductible variant: only $0.05M recoverable. Mini-basket $25K per claim.

### 4.4 RWI Premium
- **Formula:** Premium = Limit × (2.5%-4.5%); retention typically 1% of enterprise value dropping to 0.5% after 12 months.
- **Thresholds:** Limit usually 10% of deal value.
- **Example ($500M deal):** Limit $50M; premium at 3% = $1.5M; retention 1% = $5M (buyer absorbs) before RWI attaches. For AI-SaaS: add separate IP/privacy sublimit, often with dedicated retention and higher premium (~5%).

## 5. Do vs Don't

| # | Do | Don't |
|---|----|------|
| 1 | Label every clause binding or non-binding explicitly | Leave enforceability ambiguous — courts may infer more binding than intended |
| 2 | Fix exclusivity (45-90 days) before diligence spend > $250K | Spend large diligence budget without a no-shop locked in |
| 3 | Include quantitative MAC trigger plus qualitative + carve-outs | Rely solely on qualitative "material adverse change" — rarely wins in court |
| 4 | Engineer key-employee retention as a hard CP in tech/AI deals | Assume offer letters or bonus pools will hold post-signing |
| 5 | Set long-stop date = slowest regulatory authority + buffer (30-60 days) | Pick a round number that ignores dependency tree |
| 6 | Pre-wire RWI broker before term sheet signing | Leave RWI to SPA stage — broker cycle (3-5 weeks) can slip closing |
| 7 | Quantify break + reverse break fee to reflect sunk-cost and regulatory-risk asymmetry | Copy-paste 2% flat break fee from prior deal without calibration |
| 8 | Include explicit IP assignment CP and open-source audit for tech/AI targets | Assume GitHub repo and "founder assignments" are complete |
| 9 | Keep term sheet < 5 pages focused on dealmakers | Turn term sheet into a mini-SPA; it just invites re-negotiation |
| 10 | Route term sheet through one senior sponsor per side | Negotiate parallel drafts with different VPs — positions will diverge |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — AI-SaaS Acquisition ($500M)
Strategic buyer (cloud hyperscaler) acquiring a vertical AI-SaaS company. Term sheet provisions engineered:
- **Price:** $500M EV, locked-box at signing balance sheet.
- **CPs:** CCI/HSR clearance; 8 of 10 "key employees" (founders + 6 AI research leads) sign 4-year retention with 50% in RSU cliff; open-source license audit passes; data-privacy rep (no GDPR violations in 24 months).
- **Binding:** 60-day exclusivity, $15M break fee, $30M reverse break fee (regulatory), confidentiality.
- **Non-compete:** 3 years, global, narrow to LLM-fine-tuning vertical.
- **IP assignment:** 100% of model weights, training data provenance, customer contracts with IP-indemnity pass-through.
- **Protection:** 15% escrow for 18 months; RWI $50M limit at 3% premium; IP sublimit $25M.
- **Tools:** DealRoom VDR for diligence, Ironclad CLM for term-sheet version control, Aon RWI quote, LawVu M&A review workspace.

### Scenario 2 — IT-Services Rollup (Mid-Market)
PE buyer rolling up 5 regional IT-services firms over 18 months. Term sheet template:
- **Price mechanism:** Locked-box + completion-adjustment for working capital outside +/- 10% collar.
- **CPs light** (single deal ~ $40-80M): customer change-of-control consents for top 10 accounts; CCI if applicable; no litigation CP.
- **MAC carve-outs tight:** general economic conditions, pandemic resurgence, change in IT-services outsourcing tax policy — all carve-outs except *disproportionate impact* clause.
- **RWI:** Single-policy across the roll-up, $40M aggregate limit, rolling escrow release every 6 months as each tranche's survival runs off (15% → 10% → 5% → 0).
- **Governance:** PE board 3 seats, founders 2, 1 independent; veto matters defined for capex > $2M.
- **Tools:** Firmex VDR (multi-room), Marsh RWI, DocuSign CLM for term sheet + LOIs, iManage for closing binder.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE: Skipped Exclusivity
A Series B-to-growth acquirer agreed a 3-page term sheet at $180M for a fintech target but, to "move fast," omitted exclusivity. Over the next 35 days, buyer spent ~$1.1M on Big-4 diligence + ~$400K in external legal. Seller's banker used the buyer's signed term sheet as a stalking-horse, ran a closed 3-bid auction, and sold to a competitor at $212M. Buyer walked away with **$1.5M in sunk costs, zero asset, and a 4-month opportunity loss**. Root cause: the "it's just a term sheet, exclusivity slows us down" view. Correct move: 45-day exclusivity with a 15-day automatic extension if diligence is materially on track — cost to seller ~ nil, value to buyer ~ full diligence investment protected.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Draft** a one-page term-sheet skeleton in Ironclad / DocuSign CLM using the 4-bucket anatomy (Commercial / Legal / Governance / Protection).
2. **Label** every clause as binding or non-binding and add an explicit "Section X is binding" line for exclusivity, confidentiality, break fees, costs, no-solicit.
3. **Size** break fee, reverse break fee, indemnity cap, basket, and RWI premium using Section 4 formulas against actual deal value; document assumptions in a one-page sizing memo.
4. **Engineer** CPs using the dependency tree; flag any unsatisfiable CP (e.g., "all customer consents") and rewrite as a threshold (e.g., "customers representing 80% of ARR").
5. **Calibrate** MAC clause with numeric trigger + qualitative + carve-outs; red-line against Akorn v. Fresenius and recent Delaware MAC cases for language precision.
6. **Pre-wire** RWI broker (Aon / Marsh / Lockton) with a 2-page underwriting pack before term sheet sign so policy timing aligns to closing.
7. **Route** through one senior sponsor per side plus single outside counsel; ban parallel drafts across functions.
8. **Capture** the signed term sheet in the VDR (DealRoom / Firmex) and issue a change-control memo so every subsequent SPA clause can be traced back to a term-sheet provision.

## 8. Content Quality Audit

**Covered well:** headline concept of term sheet as commercial summary; exclusivity as buyer-protection for diligence spend; use-case and walk-away MAC scenario; VC-style examples.

**Underplayed:**
- Binding vs non-binding nuance — source flags the principle but doesn't show how to label clause-by-clause.
- MAC carve-outs (industry-wide, macro, disproportionate-impact sub-test) need specific Delaware-jurisprudence grounding.
- RWI vs escrow trade-off economics (premium %, retention, interaction with fundamental reps) is absent.
- Reverse break fees (buyer-side regulatory risk) and specific performance remedies not covered.
- Standstill clauses (for strategic-review / public-company contexts) missing.
- IP-assignment rigour and open-source licensing audit critical for tech/AI deals but not discussed.
- Tipping-vs-deductible basket mechanics and mini-basket — often the single most commercially sensitive SPA negotiation.

**Supplement with (≥5 sources):**
1. Rosenbaum & Pearl, *Investment Banking: Valuation, LBOs, M&A and IPOs*, 3rd ed. (Wiley, 2020) — term-sheet mechanics + consideration structure.
2. Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, *Takeover Law and Practice* (annual update) — binding/non-binding provisions, break fees, MAC case law.
3. American Bar Association, *Model Stock Purchase Agreement with Commentary* (most recent edition) — clause-by-clause market reference.
4. SRS Acquiom, *M&A Deal Terms Study* (2024) — empirical benchmarks for caps, baskets, survival, escrow, RWI.
5. Harvard Business School case, *Negotiation of M&A Term Sheets* (and related Dealmaking in the Face of Ambiguity simulation) — negotiation dynamics.
6. Akorn v. Fresenius (Delaware Chancery, 2018) judgment — the defining MAC decision worth reading in full.

**Red flags:**
- Any term sheet silent on binding/non-binding labelling.
- MAC clause without numeric trigger AND carve-outs.
- Exclusivity > 120 days or < 30 days without justification.
- Key-employee retention as a "best-efforts" covenant, not a CP, in AI/tech deals.
- Break fee copy-pasted without sizing memo.
- RWI policy not in underwriting by the time term sheet is signed.
- Cross-border deal with a long-stop date shorter than the expected regulatory timeline.

## 9. Quick-Recall Card
- Term sheet fixes 80% of the deal in 2-5 pages; everything downstream is translation.
- 4 buckets: Commercial, Legal, Governance, Protection — nothing lives outside them.
- Label binding vs non-binding clause-by-clause or courts will do it for you.
- Exclusivity + break fee + MAC + CPs are the four "what-if-it-goes-wrong" levers; size them numerically, not by template.
- In tech/AI deals, key-employee retention + IP assignment + open-source audit are non-negotiable CPs — not covenants.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: **which five provisions in this term sheet will, if mis-drafted today, force us to re-negotiate price, walk from the deal, or lose the key people we are actually buying?**
