# Buyouts vs. Acquisitions

## Overview

A buyout usually means a private group (often using borrowed money) takes full control of a company, often taking it private. An acquisition is a broader term for any purchase of a company, usually by another operating company.

---

## Why It Matters

The structure decides who runs the firm next, how much debt it carries, and how fast changes can be made. Confusing the two can lead to the wrong advisors, the wrong financing, and the wrong expectations for management and staff.

## Key Principles

- Buyouts often use heavy debt; acquisitions use cash, stock, or mixed consideration
- Buyouts focus on financial returns in 3 to 7 years
- Acquisitions often focus on strategic fit and long-term synergies
- Management teams are usually kept (and incentivized) in buyouts
- Integration pressure is higher in strategic acquisitions

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Leveraged Buyout (LBO)** | Buying a firm mostly with borrowed money |
| **Management Buyout (MBO)** | Current managers buy the company they run |
| **Private Equity** | Investment firms that buy, improve, and resell companies |
| **Strategic Buyer** | An operating company buying for business fit |
| **Financial Buyer** | An investor buying for resale profit |

## Use Case

A founder wants to retire and sell the family-run business. A private equity firm does a buyout, keeps existing managers, improves operations for five years, and sells to a larger strategic buyer.

## Scenario

> A listed packaging firm had stable cash flow but a low share price. A PE fund took it private through a leveraged buyout, cut non-core units, automated two plants, and sold it four years later to a global rival at double the purchase price.

## Examples

- A group of senior managers buys out a regional logistics firm from its retiring owner.
- A large auto OEM acquires a battery maker to control electric-vehicle supply.

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Buyouts are financial-sponsor-led, debt-heavy control transactions (LBO/MBO/PE) aimed at 3–7 year equity returns via operational improvement, debt paydown and multiple expansion; acquisitions are the broader universe of corporate purchases — usually by an operating (strategic) buyer — where cash/stock/mixed consideration funds a long-term fit-and-synergy thesis.
An IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader must distinguish them because the buyer type dictates price logic, governance, integration speed, management retention, and the post-deal operating cadence — confusing the two leads to the wrong advisors, the wrong data room, and misaligned earn-out/retention terms.
Decision: given this asset/founder context, should we run a **strategic sale**, a **sponsor/PE buyout (LBO/MBO/BIMBO)**, or a **dual-track** process — and what price, governance, and people outcome does each path deliver?

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Buyout vs Acquisition Matrix
**Purpose:** One-glance contrast of paths a founder/board can run.

```
                 | BUYOUT (PE/LBO/MBO)     | STRATEGIC ACQUISITION
-----------------+-------------------------+----------------------------
Buyer Type       | Financial sponsor       | Operating company
Financing        | 50-70% debt + equity    | Cash / stock / mixed, low debt
Governance       | Sponsor board + mgmt    | Absorbed into acquirer org
Time Horizon     | 3-7 yrs, exit-driven    | Indefinite, strategic hold
Integration      | Light; run as standalone| Heavy; merge systems + teams
Mgmt Retention   | Usually kept + sweet eq | Often replaced post-synergy
Price Logic      | IRR / LBO math          | Synergy NPV + scarcity premium
People Outcome   | Rollover, earn-outs     | Severance, redundancy risk
```
**IT/AI worked example:** A Series-C AI infra firm weighs (a) KKR-style buyout keeping founders + $40M sweet-equity pool vs (b) sale to hyperscaler where product folds into platform and 40% of team exits in year 1.
**Trigger:** Whenever a sell-side mandate is being scoped.

### 3.2 PE Value-Creation Levers
**Purpose:** Decompose where sponsor IRR actually comes from.

```
   ENTRY EQUITY  ──►   EXIT EQUITY
     |
     ├── Lever 1: Operational Improvement  (EBITDA growth: price, mix, cost, ARR)
     ├── Lever 2: Debt Paydown             (cash sweep shrinks debt → equity grows)
     ├── Lever 3: Multiple Expansion       (exit x > entry x via scale / re-rating)
     └── Lever 4: Strategic Bolt-Ons       (buy small @ low x, sell blended @ high x)
```
**Consulting worked example:** PE buys a $50M EBITDA IT-services firm at 9x; over 5 yrs grows EBITDA to $90M (+ops), pays down $200M debt, exits at 11x → IRR bridge attributes 45% to ops, 25% to deleveraging, 20% to multiple expansion, 10% to bolt-ons.
**Trigger:** Building or reading any LBO return bridge.

## 4. Formulas

### 4.1 Equity IRR Approximation
**Formula:** `IRR ≈ (Exit Equity / Entry Equity)^(1/n) − 1`
**Threshold:** PE target 20-25% gross IRR; below 15% deal dies at IC.
**IT/AI example:** PE buys mature SaaS: Entry Equity $200M → Exit Equity $600M over 5 yrs → IRR ≈ (3)^(1/5) − 1 = 24.6%. Pass.

### 4.2 Operational Improvement Contribution to IRR
**Formula:** `Δ Equity_ops = (EBITDA_exit − EBITDA_entry) × Entry Multiple`
**Threshold:** For modern PE, ops should drive ≥50% of value creation (vs pre-2008 where leverage dominated).
**Example:** SaaS EBITDA $30M → $55M at 10x entry multiple → $250M ops-driven equity uplift.

### 4.3 Debt Paydown Contribution
**Formula:** `Δ Equity_debt = Σ (FCF × Cash Sweep %) over hold period`
**Threshold:** Healthy LBO pays down 40-60% of entry debt by exit.
**Example:** $300M entry debt, $45M/yr FCF × 80% sweep × 5 yrs = $180M paydown → equity grows 1:1 by that amount.

### 4.4 Multiple Expansion Contribution
**Formula:** `Δ Equity_mult = (Exit Multiple − Entry Multiple) × Exit EBITDA`
**Threshold:** Do not underwrite >1.0x expansion without a structural re-rating story (scale, category creation, platform premium).
**Example:** SaaS entry 10x, exit 12x, exit EBITDA $55M → +2 × 55 = $110M from multiple expansion alone.

**Full IRR Bridge (SaaS PE buyout, 5-yr hold):**
```
Entry Equity  : $200M
+ Ops (EBITDA growth)   : +$250M
+ Debt paydown          : +$180M
+ Multiple expansion    : +$110M
− Fees/interest drag    : −$140M
= Exit Equity           :  $600M  →  IRR 24.6%, MOIC 3.0x
```

## 5. Do vs Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Run a **dual-track** (strategic + sponsor) to discover real price | Assume a strategic will always pay more — PEs now win ~40% of mid-market |
| Model **rollover equity** as continued risk capital, not proceeds | Treat rollover as "cash in hand" — it's illiquid and can go to zero |
| Stress-test debt capacity at **Debt/EBITDA ≥ 5x** with -20% EBITDA shock | Underwrite an LBO on base case only; covenants break at -15% |
| Design **sweet equity + ratchet** with hurdle IRRs aligned to fund target | Hand out flat mgmt equity that pays out even at 8% IRR |
| Ask strategics for a **signed retention/IP plan** pre-LOI | Accept "culture will be preserved" as a verbal promise |
| Track **integration debt** (tech, org, data) separately from financial debt | Bury integration cost inside synergy numbers |
| Pick advisors by **buyer-type fit** (sponsor-native vs strategic-native bankers) | Use the same banker for LBO and cross-border strategic — skillsets differ |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Mature SaaS founder at the crossroads
$120M ARR vertical SaaS, 30% EBITDA, 15% growth. Strategic (large platform vendor) offers $900M all-cash, product folded in, founder exits in 12 months. PE offers $850M headline but with $150M founder rollover, $40M sweet-equity pool, 5-yr second bite potentially worth another $300M at 2x exit. **Lesson:** sticker price ≠ take-home; governance and second-bite economics often flip the ranking. **Tools:** CapIQ + PitchBook comps, Excel LBO model, VDR (Datasite/Intralinks).

### Scenario 2 — IT-services MBO
Regional IT-services firm, ₹500cr revenue, 12% EBITDA, senior VP + CFO want to buy. PE backs MBO at 7x EBITDA; debt capped at 3.5x (services = low tangible collateral). Mgmt gets 20% sweet equity with IRR ratchet. **Lesson:** MBO preserves culture and client continuity but services-firm debt capacity is structurally lower than product/SaaS — don't copy-paste LBO leverage from a manufacturing comp. **Tools:** LBO model with services-specific covenants, CultureAmp pulse for year-1 retention.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE: Founder sells AI startup to strategic expecting culture continuity
$60M ARR applied-AI startup sold to global tech major for $550M cash + 2-yr earn-out. Founders told "autonomous BU, no layoffs". Within 12 months: product roadmap absorbed into acquirer's platform, 3 of 5 senior researchers leave, 2 marquee enterprise customers churn citing "roadmap drift". **Quantified loss:** 8 key engineers at ~₹3cr loaded cost × replacement difficulty → ~₹25cr retention hit; 2 lost logos × ₹12cr ACV = ₹24cr revenue gone; earn-out missed → ~$80M forgone. Total destroyed value ≈ ₹450cr + $80M. **Lesson:** if culture/IP continuity matters more than headline price, a PE buyout with rollover is often the rational choice — a strategic's integration gravity is almost always stronger than any verbal promise. **Tools:** retention forecasting, key-person risk register, integration clean-room agreements.

## 7. Implementation Playbook (Founder/Board choosing buyout vs strategic)

1. **Draft** a one-page **seller objective stack** (price / liquidity / control / people / legacy) — artifact: ranked objectives memo.
2. **Segment** candidate buyers into strategic vs financial vs hybrid — artifact: buyer longlist in CapIQ/PitchBook.
3. **Build** a **dual-track LBO + strategic synergy model** side-by-side — artifact: Excel with IRR bridge and synergy NPV.
4. **Quantify** the **people & IP outcome** under each path (retention %, earn-out risk, rollover path) — artifact: people-risk register.
5. **Negotiate** structural terms (rollover, sweet equity, earn-out, reps caps) before price — artifact: term-sheet comparison grid.
6. **Stress-test** debt capacity and covenant headroom at -20% EBITDA shock — artifact: downside LBO case.
7. **Select** buyer and **sign** exclusivity only after validated financing proof and reference-checked PE operating partner — artifact: reference-call notes.
8. **Define** Day-1 / Day-100 operating plan including governance cadence, board comp, and integration (or standalone) charter — artifact: 100-day plan deck.

## 9. Quick-Recall Card
- Buyout = financial sponsor + heavy debt + 3-7 yr exit; Acquisition = strategic/operating buyer + cash/stock + indefinite hold.
- Price logic differs: IRR math vs synergy NPV; sticker price rarely tells the take-home story.
- Modern PE IRR is ~50% operations, ~25% deleveraging, ~25% multiple expansion + bolt-ons.
- Rollover + sweet equity + earn-out are where real founder upside is won or lost.
- Strategic integration gravity almost always overpowers verbal culture promises — structure, don't trust.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: **Given our asset profile, team, and liquidity needs, which buyer type (sponsor LBO/MBO vs strategic acquirer) maximises risk-adjusted take-home value for the founders and continuity for the product — and what three structural terms (rollover %, sweet equity hurdle, retention/IP protection) must be non-negotiable in the term sheet?**
