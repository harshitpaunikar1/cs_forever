# Company Law and SEBI Provisions

## Overview

M&A deals in India must follow the Companies Act 2013 (for mergers and share transfers) and SEBI regulations (for listed-company transactions). Together these rules cover board approvals, disclosures, minority protection, and the public offer process.

---

## Why It Matters

Non-compliance can stop a deal, trigger fines, or lead to criminal liability for directors. Strong legal planning keeps the deal on track, protects shareholders, and avoids last-minute surprises from regulators.

## Key Principles

- Check Companies Act sections on mergers (230–234) and related-party deals
- Follow SEBI Takeover Code (SAST) for listed targets
- Make open offer when crossing 25% voting rights in a listed firm
- Follow SEBI LODR for timely disclosures to stock exchanges
- Secure insider-trading and UPSI safeguards during diligence

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Companies Act 2013** | Main Indian law governing corporate actions |
| **SEBI SAST (Takeover Code)** | Rules for taking over listed companies |
| **Open Offer** | Mandatory offer to buy shares from public shareholders |
| **LODR** | Listing rules on disclosure obligations |
| **UPSI** | Unpublished price-sensitive information |

## Use Case

An investor group buys a 28% stake in a listed manufacturer. SAST requires an open offer for an additional 26% to public shareholders, which must be announced the same day the share purchase agreement is signed.

## Scenario

> A strategic buyer wanted to buy 60% of a listed company. By triggering the open offer at 26% and then signing a further agreement with promoters, it consolidated 60% within six months while meeting all SEBI timelines and escrow rules.

## Examples

- A scheme merger needs NCLT, stock exchange, and SEBI approvals before becoming effective.
- A 2% creeping-acquisition window lets promoters add shares without an open offer (up to the limit).

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Indian M&A runs on two statutory rails: the Companies Act 2013 (Sections 230-234 schemes, related-party rules, buybacks) and SEBI regulations (SAST, LODR, PIT) for any listed-company leg of the deal. For an IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader, compliance timing directly shapes signing-to-closing length, cash locked in escrow, and the minimum price at which the target can be acquired. The decision: pick a deal structure (SPA + open offer vs NCLT scheme vs delisting) that clears Companies Act + SEBI + CCI + FEMA gates without stalling the synergy clock.

       | No
       v
+------------------------------+
| Change in CONTROL (board /   |--Yes--> OPEN OFFER (control trigger)
|  veto / management)?         |
+------------------------------+
       | No
       v
+------------------------------+
| Indirect acquisition (parent)|--Yes--> OPEN OFFER (indirect)
|  passes material-sub tests?  |
+------------------------------+
       | No
       v
   NO OPEN OFFER (but LODR + PIT still apply)

**Components:** voting %, PAC aggregation, control test, creeping window, indirect tests.
**IT/AI lens:** AI-SaaS PE investor plans 28% primary into listed fintech → crosses 25% → mandatory offer for 26% more; model dilution + cash call in deal memo.
**Trigger:** every listed-target deal brief.

### 3.2 Compliance Stack
**Purpose:** map each deal action to the regulator that polices it.

```
Layer 1: Companies Act 2013   --> MCA / NCLT     (scheme, S.188, buyback)
Layer 2: SEBI SAST 2011       --> SEBI + Exchanges (open offer, disclosures)
Layer 3: SEBI LODR 2015       --> Stock Exchanges (Reg 30 events, 24h filings)
Layer 4: SEBI PIT 2015        --> SEBI           (UPSI, insider list, window)
Layer 5: CCI Act 2002         --> CCI            (combination notice)
Layer 6: FEMA / FDI Policy    --> RBI            (sectoral caps, FC-TRS/FC-GPR)
```
**Components:** statute -> regulator -> trigger event -> filing form.
**IT/AI lens:** 60% buy of listed IT-services firm fires all six layers; consulting PM maintains a single "compliance Kanban" per layer.
**Trigger:** kickoff of any cross-border listed-co acquisition.

### 3.3 Open Offer Timeline (SAST)
**Purpose:** sequence the 90-120 day public-offer calendar.

```
D-0    SPA signed  ---> Public Announcement (PA) same day to exchanges
D+5    Detailed Public Statement (DPS) in newspapers
D+5    Escrow funded (>=25% of offer consideration)
D+10   Draft Letter of Offer (DLoO) filed with SEBI
D+25   SEBI observations received (target)
D+37   Letter of Offer dispatched to shareholders
D+46   Tendering period OPENS (10 working days)
D+56   Tendering period CLOSES
D+66   Settlement of consideration + share transfer
D+75   Post-offer report to SEBI + 3/5/10% disclosures refreshed
```
**Components:** PA, DPS, escrow, DLoO, LoO, tendering, settlement.
**IT/AI lens:** Product leader syncs integration Day-1 planning with D+66 settlement; AI-due-diligence on customer data only after UPSI fencing.
**Trigger:** once board decides a listed-co takeover.

## 4. Formulas

1. **Open Offer Size**
   `Minimum Offer Size = 26% x Post-Issue Voting Equity`
   Example: listed IT-services firm has 100 cr shares post-SPA; offer for 26 cr shares.

2. **Minimum Offer Price (SAST Reg 8)**
   `Min Price = MAX( Negotiated/SPA price, 60-day VWAP, 2-week VWAP, highest in 26 weeks, highest in 60 trading days (frequently traded) )`
   Example: SPA = INR 820; 60-day VWAP = INR 790; 2-week VWAP = INR 865; 26-week high = INR 910 -> **Min Price = INR 910**.

3. **Escrow Minimum**
   `Escrow = 25% of first INR 500 cr of (offer size x price) + 10% of amount above INR 500 cr`
   Example: 26 cr shares x INR 910 = INR 23,660 cr offer -> Escrow = 0.25 x 500 + 0.10 x 23,160 = **INR 125 cr + INR 2,316 cr = INR 2,441 cr**.

4. **Creeping Acquisition Limit**
   `Allowed top-up per FY = 5% (aggregate) subject to 2% cap per transaction once holding is 25-75%`
   Example: promoter at 42% can add up to 5% in FY27 without an open offer, but no single slab > 2%.

5. **Delisting Floor Price (post-2021 RBB)**
   `Floor = MAX( SAST floor, 60-day VWAP, 26-week high )` and accepted only if 90% aggregate post-offer holding achieved.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| # | Do | Don't |
|---|----|-------|
| 1 | File PA on the same day the SPA is signed (SAST Reg 13(1)). | Don't delay the PA to "perfect" deal docs - each day risks Reg 30 breach. |
| 2 | Fund escrow **before** DPS with a scheduled bank / bank guarantee. | Don't assume promoter pledge cash is acceptable escrow - SEBI rejects it. |
| 3 | Maintain a Structured Digital Database (SDD) for every UPSI access. | Don't email sensitive models outside the VDR - PIT Reg 3(5) audit trail breaks. |
| 4 | Aggregate PAC holdings (including promoter group trusts, ESOP trusts). | Don't treat PAC test narrowly - SEBI probes look at "acting in concert" substance. |
| 5 | Check CCI Green Channel eligibility early for low-overlap AI/SaaS deals. | Don't file Form I when Green Channel suffices - you waste 30+ days. |
| 6 | Use highest-of-four price formula even when SPA price looks high. | Don't cap open-offer price at SPA; 26-week high may raise the floor. |
| 7 | Close trading window for designated persons from deal inception. | Don't let product leads with UPSI exercise ESOPs during blackout. |
| 8 | Keep Reg 30 LODR materiality policy and disclose within 30 min / 12 hrs / 24 hrs. | Don't selectively brief analysts - Reg 30(11) treats that as violation. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 - AI-SaaS Acquirer targets listed Indian IT-services firm
- **Setup:** US-HQ AI-SaaS company wants 55% of a listed mid-cap IT services firm (market cap INR 18,000 cr).
- **Regulatory path:** (a) NDA + SDD setup (PIT), (b) SPA negotiated - PA filed Day-0, DPS Day-5 (SAST), (c) Form II CCI filing given horizontal overlap in staffing, (d) LODR Reg 30 event filings for each milestone, (e) FEMA FC-TRS since non-resident is buyer.
- **Commercial outcome:** open-offer size 26% x post-issue equity; minimum price = INR 1,180 (26-week high, not SPA INR 1,050); escrow INR 545 cr; total deal outflow INR 14,700 cr; 110-day signing-to-closing.
- **Lens:** Product VP pre-agrees a 120-day integration freeze so that tech teams don't touch customer data until Day 66.

### Scenario 2 - PE Fund buys 28% of listed FinTech
- **Setup:** PE fund subscribes to 28% primary in listed FinTech (current market cap INR 6,500 cr). SPA price INR 640 / share.
- **SAST math:** 60-day VWAP INR 620; 2-week VWAP INR 655; 26-week high INR 700 -> **Min open-offer price = INR 700**; offer size = 26% of enlarged equity = ~ 3.9 cr shares; offer consideration = INR 2,730 cr; escrow = 25% of first INR 500 cr + 10% of INR 2,230 cr = **INR 348 cr**.
- **Timeline:** PA Day 0, DPS Day 5, DLoO Day 10, LoO dispatch Day 37, tendering Day 46-56, settlement Day 66.
- **Lens:** Consulting PM builds an escrow drawdown model tied to PE fund capital calls; an AI script monitors 3% / 5% disclosure thresholds daily.

### Scenario 3 - ANTI-EXAMPLE - UPSI breach during diligence
- **Setup:** Mid-size corporate buyer begins diligence on listed target; the M&A lead shares the target's unaudited revenue model with an ESOP-holding product director outside the SDD for "sanity-check".
- **What breaks:** Product director's spouse trades 8,000 shares the next week. SEBI informant complaint filed. PIT Reg 3(1) read with Reg 9A (CEO certification) breach.
- **Consequences (quantified):**
  - Deal delay: **9 months** (SEBI probe + Section 11 direction).
  - SEBI disgorgement + penalty: INR 8 cr.
  - Bridge-loan re-pricing (+125 bps on INR 1,200 cr debt) = INR 15 cr extra interest.
  - Share price drop 7% -> acquirer market-cap loss INR 420 cr.
  - Reputation: two sell-side analysts move to "Reduce"; target board demands fresh fairness opinion (INR 3.5 cr + 6 weeks).
- **Lens:** AI lead now codifies SDD access via IAM + chaperone bot; every VDR click is logged and auto-mailed to compliance.

**Tools:** MCA21 v3 (e-forms CAA-1/2/3), SEBI SCORES, BSE/NSE listing centre, legal VDR (iManage/Intralinks), CCI Green Channel portal, FEMA forms FC-GPR / FC-TRS on RBI FIRMS, SDD tools (Finalyca, InstaDD), SAST price-calculators from SEBI website.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Chart the trigger map** - run SAST flowchart + PAC test on Day -14 before term-sheet; artifact: "Trigger Memo" signed by external counsel.
2. **Open the SDD** - instantiate a Structured Digital Database, insider list, and trading-window-closure notice before NDA; artifact: SDD log + board circular.
3. **Draft Compliance Stack RACI** - one page mapping Companies Act, SAST, LODR, PIT, CCI, FEMA to owners; artifact: Compliance RACI in Confluence.
4. **Pre-file escrow + merchant banker** - appoint Cat-I merchant banker and open escrow at least 3 days before PA; artifact: escrow agreement + MB engagement letter.
5. **Same-day PA + Reg 30 disclosure** - concurrent filings to exchanges with SPA execution; artifact: PA PDF + LODR intimation.
6. **Run price-floor calculator** - lock min offer price as highest-of-four; artifact: price memo signed by MB.
7. **CCI pre-filing consultation** - decide Form I, Form II, or Green Channel by Day 10; artifact: CCI filing dossier.
8. **Post-close clean-up** - file post-offer report, open trading window, archive SDD for 8 years, NCLT compliance report for any scheme; artifact: closing memo + compliance certificate.

## 9. Quick-Recall Card
- Two rails: Companies Act (Ss. 230-234, 188, 67-68) and SEBI (SAST, LODR, PIT).
- Open offer fires at 25% / 5% creeping / change-in-control / indirect.
- Price = highest-of-four formula; escrow = 25% of first 500 cr + 10% beyond.
- Timeline: PA (D0) -> DPS (D5) -> DLoO (D10) -> LoO (D37) -> tender (D46-56) -> settlement (D66).
- UPSI = SDD + trading-window closure + chaperoning from NDA day.
- Parallel gates: CCI + FEMA + LODR continuous disclosure.

As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: **Which statutory trigger (SAST / Companies Act scheme / CCI / FEMA) sets the critical path of my deal, and have I pre-funded escrow, SDD, and disclosure workflows to hit that path without a single day of slippage?**
