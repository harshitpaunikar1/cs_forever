# Pricing Mechanisms

## Overview

A pricing mechanism is how the final amount paid at closing is calculated. Common methods include locked-box (price fixed on a past balance sheet), completion accounts (adjusted on the closing-date numbers), and earnouts (part of the price paid later based on performance).

---

## Why It Matters

Even after agreeing a price, small changes in working capital, cash, or debt can move the final payout by millions. The pricing mechanism decides who bears that risk between signing and closing and helps avoid disputes after the deal.

## Key Principles

- Choose locked-box for speed and certainty
- Choose completion accounts when the target's finances move a lot
- Use earnouts to bridge gaps in future expectations
- Clearly define cash, debt, and working capital to avoid fights
- Put dispute-resolution rules in the sale agreement

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Locked-Box** | Price fixed on a past balance sheet; no post-close adjustment |
| **Completion Accounts** | Price adjusted based on numbers at closing |
| **Earnout** | Extra payment tied to future financial performance |
| **Net Working Capital (NWC)** | Current assets minus current liabilities |
| **Leakage** | Value taken out of the target between lock date and closing |

## Use Case

A buyer and seller sign in March but cannot close until June due to approvals. They use a locked-box based on the December audited balance sheet, ban leakage, and agree interest on the price from December to closing.

## Scenario

> A seller insisted the business was worth Rs 500 crore; the buyer thought Rs 400 crore. They signed at Rs 400 crore plus a two-year earnout of up to Rs 150 crore based on hitting revenue targets. The target hit 80% of goals and the seller received an extra Rs 120 crore.

## Examples

- A PE seller prefers locked-box to avoid any surprises after closing.
- A strategic buyer uses a completion-accounts mechanism for a seasonal retail target.

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Pricing mechanisms are the contractual machinery that converts a headline Enterprise Value into the actual cash wired at closing — via locked-box, completion accounts, or earnouts — and they dictate who bears economic risk between signing and closing.
For an IT/AI/Product/Consulting leader, engineering the right mechanism matters because small shifts in working capital, deferred revenue, cash traps, or earnout metric definitions can swing final payout by tens of crores and decide whether a post-close integration is collaborative or litigious.
The decision: pick the mechanism (and its guardrails — NWC peg, leakage list, ticking fee, escrow, dispute resolution) that matches the target's financial volatility, the gap between sign and close, and the strategic purpose of the deal.

## 2. Jargon & Terminology

| Term | Full Form | Plain-English | Why It Exists | How Measured | Where You Hear It |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locked-Box | Locked-Box Pricing | Price fixed on a past audited balance sheet; no closing true-up | Speed + certainty; seller keeps upside from effective date | Equity price at reference date + ticking fee | PE exits, auction sales |
| Completion Accounts | Closing Accounts Mechanism | Price adjusted at closing using actual BS numbers | Buyer protection against swings | Draft accounts within 60–90 days of close | Strategic acquisitions, volatile targets |
| Earnout | Contingent Consideration | Deferred payment tied to post-close performance | Bridges valuation gap between buyer/seller | % of revenue/EBITDA over 1–3 yrs, capped | Founder exits, AI startups |
| NWC Peg | Net Working Capital Target | Benchmark level of operating WC the target must deliver | Stops seller stripping WC pre-close | Avg. of last 12 months NWC | Diligence, SPA schedule |
| CFDF | Cash-Free Debt-Free | Seller keeps cash, clears debt before closing | Standard EV-to-equity bridge | Cash and debt-like items at closing date | Every LOI / term sheet |
| Leakage | Permitted vs Non-Permitted | Value transfers out of target between ref date and close | Protects buyer under locked-box | Dividends, bonuses, related-party payments | SPA leakage schedule |
| Equity Bridge | EV→Equity Reconciliation | Formula converting EV to cash payable | Aligns negotiation on final wire | EV + Cash − Debt ± NWC adj. | Closing memo |
| Reference Date | Locked-Box Date | Date of the BS that "locks" the price | Anchor for no-leakage covenant | Usually last audited year-end | Locked-box SPAs |
| Ticking Fee | Interest on Price | Daily interest accrued from ref date to close | Compensates seller for time value | Price x rate x days/365 | Locked-box closings |
| True-Up | Post-Closing Adjustment | Net cash flow between buyer/seller after draft accounts | Corrects over/under-payment at close | Δ actual vs estimate | Completion-accounts deals |
| Escrow / Holdback | Withheld Consideration | Portion of price held by third party | Secures indemnity and true-up claims | 5–15% of price, 12–24 months | SPA escrow agreement |
| Indemnity Cap | Liability Ceiling | Max seller liability for breach of warranty | Allocates risk | % of purchase price | Rep & warranty schedule |
| Anti-Sandbagging | Knowledge Qualifier | Buyer loses claim if it knew of breach pre-close | Protects seller from known-risk claims | Clause language | Negotiation |
| Pro-Sandbagging | No-Knowledge Defense | Buyer can claim even if it knew pre-close | Protects buyer | Clause language | US deals often pro |
| RWI | Reps & Warranties Insurance | Insurance replacing escrow for warranty breach | Cleaner exit for seller | 2–4% of limit premium | PE exits, auction deals |
| Dispute Resolution | Independent Accountant | Neutral expert resolves true-up disputes | Avoids litigation | 15–30 day determination window | SPA completion schedule |
| MAC Clause | Material Adverse Change | Right to walk if target materially worsens | Risk allocation pre-closing | Qualitative + quantitative triggers | Signing-to-close risk |
| Drag-Along | Drag Rights | Majority can force minority to sell | Clean exit of cap table | Threshold (e.g., >50%) | Minority deals, VC exits |
| Tag-Along | Tag Rights | Minority can join majority's sale | Protects minority economics | Pro-rata participation | Shareholder agreements |

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Locked-Box vs Completion Accounts Decision Tree
**Purpose:** Pick the right mechanism up front; wrong choice costs millions in disputes.

```
                        START: Target P&L/BS
                                |
                  +-------------+-------------+
                  |                           |
          Stable / predictable           Volatile / seasonal
          cashflows & WC?                   (retail, projects)
                  |                           |
                 YES                          NO
                  |                           |
         Recent audited BS                Draft completion
         (<=6 mo old)?                    accounts (60-90d)
                  |                           |
                 YES                          |
                  |                           |
         Seller = PE/Auction?            Buyer = strategic?
                  |                           |
                 YES -> LOCKED-BOX       YES -> COMPLETION ACCOUNTS
                                         + NWC peg + dispute clause
```
**Components:** reference date, leakage list, ticking fee (LB); NWC peg, draft-review period, independent accountant (CA).
**Worked example (IT/AI/Product/Consulting):** AI-SaaS target with ARR-driven recurring revenue, audited Dec-2025 BS, PE seller, March→May close window → **Locked-Box** with 6% ticking fee and tight leakage list.
**Trigger:** Deal sign-to-close > 60 days OR seller is a financial sponsor in an auction.

### 3.2 NWC Peg Mechanics
**Purpose:** Prevent seller from draining WC pre-close and avoid punishing normal seasonality.

```
Month   NWC (INR cr)
Apr-25    42
May-25    38           Average (last 12) = 40 cr
...                    -> PEG = 40 cr
Mar-26    41
------------------------------------------
At close: Actual NWC = 35 cr -> Shortfall 5 cr
          Final Price = EV + Cash - Debt - 5 cr
```
**Components:** 12-month average, seasonality adjustment, collar (+/- 10% dead-band), exclusion list (deferred revenue, tax accruals).
**Worked example:** IT-services company with Q4 revenue spike — peg calculated on rolling 12M avg with seasonality overlay; deferred-revenue from prepaid consulting retainers excluded (treated as debt-like).
**Trigger:** Any deal where WC is >5% of EV or seasonality is material.

### 3.3 Earnout Design Matrix

| Dimension | Conservative | Market Standard | Aggressive | IT/AI/Product/Consulting Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Metric | Revenue | Gross Profit | Adjusted EBITDA | AI startup: ARR (most objective) |
| Duration | 12 months | 18–24 months | 36 months | Product co: 24 months |
| Cap | 10% of EV | 20–30% of EV | 40%+ | Consulting rollup: 25% |
| Acceleration | On change-of-control only | CoC + termination of founder without cause | Any integration action impeding metric | AI deal: CoC + platform migration |
| Governance | Seller runs business autonomously | Joint budget approval | Full buyer control | Ring-fenced P&L for earnout period |

**Purpose:** Close valuation gaps without buyer overpaying on speculation.
**Worked example:** Seller seeks ₹500 cr, buyer ₹400 cr → signed ₹400 cr + 2-year earnout up to ₹150 cr on revenue. 80% of target hit → seller receives ₹120 cr. Total paid ₹520 cr (mirrors the seller's number because performance proved it out).
**Trigger:** Valuation gap >15% OR founder-led business where retention of key people drives value.

### 3.4 Escrow / Indemnity Layer Diagram
```
| Layer                | Amount      | Duration  | Coverage                     |
|----------------------|-------------|-----------|------------------------------|
| True-Up Escrow       | 2% of price | 6 months  | Completion accounts shortfall|
| Indemnity Escrow     | 10% of price| 18 months | Reps & warranty breaches     |
| RWI Policy (optional)| 10% limit   | 6 yrs tax | Fundamental reps, tax        |
| Specific Indemnity   | Uncapped    | 7 yrs     | Known issues (litigation, IP)|
```

## 4. Formulas

### 4.1 Final Price (Completion Accounts)
`Final Price = Headline EV + Cash(closing) − Debt(closing) ± NWC Adjustment`
**Thresholds:** Dead-band of +/- 2% of EV; NWC adj. outside dead-band triggers true-up.
**Example (IT-services, INR cr):** EV 800 + Cash 45 − Debt 60 − NWC shortfall 12 = **Final Price 773 cr**.

### 4.2 Ticking Fee (Locked-Box Interest)
`Ticking Fee = Price × Rate × Days / 365`
**Thresholds:** 4–8% p.a. typical; higher if SOFR/MCLR based.
**Example:** AI-SaaS deal, price ₹600 cr, locked to Dec-31-2025 BS, closing May-15-2026 (135 days) at 6% → `600 × 0.06 × 135/365 = ₹13.3 cr` paid on top of ₹600 cr.

### 4.3 Earnout Present Value
`Earnout PV = Σ [Probability_i × Payout_i] / (1 + r)^t`
**Thresholds:** Discount rate = buyer WACC + 200–400 bps for execution risk.
**Example:** Year 1 payout ₹50 cr at 70% probability; Year 2 payout ₹100 cr at 55% probability; r = 14%:
`PV = (0.7 × 50)/1.14 + (0.55 × 100)/1.14² = 30.7 + 42.3 = ₹73.0 cr` (vs ₹150 cr headline).

### 4.4 Escrow Cost of Capital
`Escrow Cost = Escrow Amount × Seller Discount Rate × Duration(yrs)`
**Thresholds:** If >1% of price, push for RWI substitute.
**Example:** ₹80 cr escrow × 12% seller opportunity × 1.5 yrs = **₹14.4 cr** hidden cost of cash parked.

### 4.5 NWC True-Up
`True-Up = NWC(actual at close) − NWC(peg)`  (positive = seller gets more; negative = buyer gets rebate)

## 5. Do vs Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Define Cash, Debt, Debt-Like, NWC line-by-line in SPA schedules | Leave "debt-like items" to generic catch-all language |
| Build the NWC peg from a rolling 12-month average with seasonality overlay | Use a single month-end snapshot as the peg |
| Use ARR or revenue (not adjusted EBITDA) for earnout metrics in AI/SaaS deals | Use "Adjusted EBITDA" earnouts when buyer controls post-close cost allocation |
| Include an accelerated-earnout trigger on change-of-control and founder termination without cause | Let buyer decide unilaterally to discontinue the earnout-relevant product line |
| Name the independent accountant (Big-4 or boutique) and timeline in the SPA | Leave dispute resolution to "to be mutually agreed" |
| Stress-test the ticking fee and earnout PV in the base Excel deal model | Quote headline EV without computing effective price after ticking + escrow drag |
| Replace escrow with RWI when seller is PE and continuing liability is non-starter | Agree to >20% escrow without quantifying seller's cost of capital |
| Pre-list "Permitted Leakage" (agreed bonuses, deal fees, ordinary-course dividends) | Use a silent leakage clause — every flow becomes disputed |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — AI-SaaS Acquisition (Locked-Box, Clean Close)
PE-backed AI-SaaS target, audited Dec-2025 BS, signed March 2026, close June 2026. Structure:
- Locked-box on Dec-31-2025 equity value ₹600 cr.
- Ticking fee 6% from Jan-1 → closing (~₹17 cr).
- Permitted leakage: agreed management bonus ₹4 cr; ordinary dividends ₹2 cr/qtr.
- Non-permitted leakage: related-party payments to founder HoldCo → banned.
- RWI policy at 10% limit replaces traditional 10% escrow.
**Outcome:** Close executed on-time; seller keeps operating cash and Dec→June profits; buyer gets clean balance-sheet certainty. **Tools:** DealRoom VDR, CapIQ for peg benchmarks, Excel bridge model.

### Scenario 2 — IT-Services with Seasonal Revenue (Completion Accounts + NWC Peg)
Target delivers 40% of annual revenue in Q4 (retail-client project releases). Signed Jan, close April.
- Completion accounts mechanism — draft within 75 days of close.
- NWC peg at 12-month rolling average (₹58 cr) with +/-₹3 cr collar.
- Deferred revenue treated as debt-like; unbilled receivables included in NWC.
- Independent accountant: Grant Thornton; 30-day determination window.
**Outcome:** Draft accounts show NWC of ₹51 cr (shortfall ₹4 cr after collar → rebate ₹4 cr to buyer); resolved in 18 days without dispute. **Tools:** Diligent Accountants for true-up, CapIQ for peg benchmarking.

### Scenario 3 — ANTI-EXAMPLE: Loose Earnout Definition (₹X cr Loss)
Consulting boutique acquired for ₹300 cr upfront + ₹120 cr earnout on "Adjusted EBITDA" over 3 years. No governance protection. Post-close buyer:
- Reallocates ₹15 cr/yr of shared corporate overheads to target.
- Forces consolidation of target's delivery team into buyer's practice (metric denominator shifted).
- Expands target's sales team ("investment") reducing EBITDA.
**Result:** Earnout metric falls 60% below plan; founder-seller receives ₹18 cr vs expected ₹100 cr → **earnout loss ₹82 cr**, legal fees ₹6 cr, 14-month litigation; settlement at ₹45 cr plus NDA. **Root cause:** undefined Adjusted EBITDA, no ring-fenced P&L, no acceleration clause, no dispute-resolution short-circuit. **Lesson:** For IT/Consulting deals, either use revenue-based earnouts OR define the EBITDA adjustment schedule line-by-line in an SPA exhibit. **Tools needed but missing:** Excel pricing-mechanic model with scenario toggles, independent-accountant clause.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Map** target's financial volatility (Excel 12-month NWC/cash/debt chart) — output: volatility score that drives LB vs CA choice.
2. **Draft** the Equity Bridge in a one-page memo (EV, Cash, Debt, Debt-Like, NWC Peg) — output: aligned bridge used in term sheet.
3. **Benchmark** NWC peg using CapIQ comparables and last-12-month rolling average — output: defensible peg with seasonality overlay.
4. **Build** the pricing-mechanic model in Excel with toggles for LB vs CA, ticking fee rate, earnout probability tree — output: side-by-side effective-price comparison.
5. **Negotiate** leakage schedule (permitted list) and earnout governance protections (ring-fence, acceleration, metric definition) — output: SPA schedules.
6. **Select** escrow vs RWI based on seller profile and cost-of-capital math — output: risk-transfer stack.
7. **Name** the independent accountant and determination timeline in the SPA — output: dispute-resolution clause that short-circuits litigation.
8. **Stress-test** closing mechanics with a dry-run completion-accounts process 30 days before close — output: zero-surprise closing memo.

## 9. Quick-Recall Card
- Pricing mechanism = machinery turning EV into cash wired at close.
- Three options: Locked-Box (speed/certainty), Completion Accounts (volatile targets), Earnout (bridge valuation gaps).
- Always: define Cash/Debt/NWC line-by-line; name the independent accountant in the SPA; compute effective price after ticking fee and escrow drag.
- For AI/SaaS: prefer revenue/ARR earnouts with ring-fenced P&L and CoC acceleration.
- For IT-services/Consulting with seasonality: completion accounts with rolling-12M NWC peg and collars.
- As a PM/Consultant/AI Lead, the one question to answer with this framework is: **Given our target's financial volatility, sign-to-close window, and earnout risk, which pricing mechanism (and which leakage, NWC peg, ticking fee, escrow/RWI, and dispute-resolution safeguards) actually protects our effective purchase price — and what is that effective price after all drags?**
