# Employee Development II (Training Design & Evaluation)


## Overview

This focuses on designing training properly (what to teach and how) and checking if training actually worked.


## Why It Matters

Training can waste time if it’s not relevant or not measured. Evaluation ensures the company gets real improvement, not just attendance.


## Key Principles

- Define training goals clearly
- Use suitable methods (classroom, e-learning, on-the-job)
- Practice and reinforcement after training
- Evaluate results (learning + job impact)

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Training Needs Analysis (TNA)** | Finding what training is required |
| **Learning Objective** | What learners should be able to do after training |
| **E-learning** | Online training modules |
| **Training Evaluation** | Checking effectiveness (feedback, tests, performance data) |


## Use Case

Rolling out safety training in a factory and tracking accident rates before and after.


## Scenario

> Employees attend training, but performance doesn’t improve. HR realizes training wasn’t job-specific, redesigns it with real examples and follow-up coaching.


## Examples

- Pre-test and post-test to measure learning improvement.
- Tracking sales increase after product training.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Training Design Funnel
**Purpose:** Turn a skill gap into a training program that matches the job.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
TNA -> learning objectives -> method -> practice -> reinforcement -> job impact
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
TNA: what gap exists.
Learning objectives: what the learner must do after training.
Method: classroom, e-learning, or on-the-job.
Practice: exercises, simulations, or coached repetition.
Reinforcement: follow-up support after the class ends.
Job impact: whether the role performance changed.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A support team needs better prompt-handling and escalation skills. The funnel prevents the company from buying a generic webinar; instead, it defines job-specific objectives, uses e-learning for concepts, practice for cases, and manager reinforcement after launch [verified from model knowledge, not source].

When to pull this out in a meeting: When someone wants to "do training" before defining the actual skill gap.

### Training Evaluation Ladder
**Purpose:** Check whether training worked beyond attendance.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
reaction -> learning -> behavior -> results
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Reaction: whether learners found it useful.
Learning: whether knowledge increased.
Behavior: whether work habits changed.
Results: whether business metrics improved.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product team learns a new analytics tool. If attendance is 100% but dashboard usage and decision quality do not improve, the evaluation ladder says the program failed at behavior transfer even if reaction scores were high [verified from model knowledge, not source].

When to pull this out in a meeting: When leadership assumes a positive feedback form proves business value.

### Method Selection Matrix
**Purpose:** Match the training method to the type of content and scale of rollout.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
                   CONTENT TYPE
              concepts        practice-heavy
SCALE
large         e-learning      blended / e-learning + coaching
small         classroom       on-the-job / coached practice
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Concepts: policies, theory, or process basics.
Practice-heavy: skills that need repetition and feedback.
Large scale: many learners, multiple locations, fast rollout.
Small scale: fewer learners, more personalized coaching.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: Compliance policy can go into e-learning for scale, while sales objection handling is better taught with classroom role-play and manager coaching. The matrix avoids overusing one format for every training problem [verified from model knowledge, not source].

When to pull this out in a meeting: When L&D is choosing between a workshop, a course platform, or coached practice.

## 4. Formulas

The formulas below are [verified from model knowledge, not source].

### Formula 1: Learning Gain
Formula: `Learning gain = post-test score - pre-test score`

Variables:
pre-test score = baseline knowledge before training
post-test score = knowledge after training

Why this formula exists: It measures whether the learner actually learned something.

How to interpret the output:
Positive gain -> learning improved
Zero gain -> no measurable learning
Negative gain -> training or test design problem

Worked example with numbers: If a sales team scores 52 on the pre-test and 84 on the post-test, learning gain is 32 points. That tells HR the content was understood, though it does not yet prove job impact [verified from model knowledge, not source].

### Formula 2: Training ROI
Formula: `Training ROI = (benefits - costs) / costs`

Variables:
benefits = value of performance improvement
costs = delivery, time, materials, and coaching cost

Why this formula exists: It answers whether the training was worth the spend.

How to interpret the output:
Above 0 -> benefits exceed costs
Around 0 -> break-even
Below 0 -> training did not pay back

Worked example with numbers: If a safety program costs 100,000 and reduces accidents enough to save 140,000 in claims and downtime, ROI is 40%. That justifies scaling the program [verified from model knowledge, not source].

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Don't launch training without identifying the gap first. | Do perform a TNA and link training to the actual role requirement. |
| Don't treat attendance as success. | Do measure learning, behavior, and results. |
| Don't use a one-size-fits-all delivery method. | Do match classroom, e-learning, or on-the-job methods to the content. |
| Don't stop support when the class ends. | Do add practice and reinforcement after training. |
| Don't rely only on happy-sheet feedback. | Do use tests and performance data to check impact. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: Factory safety training
**Situation:** A factory rolls out safety training after several near misses. Management wants to know if the program improved behavior or just satisfied an audit requirement.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Training Design Funnel + Training Evaluation Ladder.

**Analysis:** The TNA should identify the exact unsafe behaviors, not just a generic "safety awareness" need. If accident rates and near misses fall after the program, and supervisors report safer behavior, the training has moved beyond reaction into results [verified from model knowledge, not source].

**Decision rule:** If post-training incidents drop materially, keep and scale the program. If learning scores rise but incidents do not, redesign the content or reinforcement. If neither improves, replace the method or the trainer.

**Action:** Add practical drills, supervisor follow-up, and incident tracking for 90 days.

### Scenario 2: Sales product training
**Situation:** A product team trains the sales force on a new feature set. Pre-test scores improve, but win rates do not.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Learning Gain + Training Evaluation Ladder.

**Analysis:** Strong learning gain means the content was understood, but behavior or results may not have transferred. The missing piece is likely reinforcement, coaching, or a mismatch between the trained material and the objections sellers face in real deals [verified from model knowledge, not source].

**Decision rule:** If learning improves but results do not, add coaching and job aids. If results improve, continue. If scores do not improve, redesign the curriculum.

**Action:** Use call reviews, deal coaching, and a field playbook tied to real objections.

### Scenario 3: Remote onboarding with e-learning
**Situation:** A distributed consulting team onboards new analysts through e-learning modules. Completion rates are high, but managers say the new hires still struggle on client work.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Method Selection Matrix + Training ROI.

**Analysis:** E-learning can scale concepts, but on-the-job practice may be needed for analysis, presentation, and client handling. If the program is cheap but transfers poorly, ROI may still be weak because performance gains are too small [verified from model knowledge, not source].

**Decision rule:** If the content is conceptual and the learner base is large, keep e-learning. If the skill is practice-heavy, add coaching or classroom simulation. If job performance remains flat, redesign the learning path.

**Action:** Blend modules with manager coaching, live case work, and post-training performance reviews.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. Run a training needs analysis before designing anything.
2. Write one learning objective per skill or behavior you want to change.
3. Pick the training method based on content type, scale, and need for practice.
4. Build in practice during training and reinforcement afterward.
5. Measure reaction, learning, behavior, and results instead of only attendance.
6. Use pre-tests and post-tests for knowledge-heavy programs.
7. Review training ROI and job impact after rollout, then revise the design.
