# Job Evaluation (Building Pay Structure)


## Overview

Job evaluation means comparing jobs to decide which jobs should be paid more or less based on their value to the company.


## Why It Matters

It helps create fair pay structures and reduces salary disputes. Employees feel more satisfied when pay differences make sense.


## Key Principles

- Compare jobs, not people
- Use consistent factors (skill, effort, responsibility)
- Build pay grades/bands based on job value
- Review periodically as jobs change

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Pay Structure** | Organized system of salary ranges |
| **Pay Grade/Band** | Group of jobs with similar pay range |
| **Internal Equity** | Fairness of pay within the company |
| **Point Factor Method** | Scoring jobs using set criteria |


## Use Case

Designing pay grades for a company with 200 employees and many different roles.


## Scenario

> Employees complain that a junior role earns close to a senior role. HR evaluates jobs, updates pay bands, and fixes inconsistencies.


## Examples

- A senior engineer role placed in a higher pay band than junior engineer.
- A team lead role scored higher due to higher responsibility and decision-making.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

The frameworks below are [verified from model knowledge, not source]; the source provides the idea, and these turn it into a pay-structure tool.

### Job Value Funnel
**Purpose:** Translate role characteristics into a relative job value.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
skill + effort + responsibility -> job value -> pay grade/band
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Skill: how difficult the role is to perform.
Effort: how much physical or mental load the role requires.
Responsibility: how much accountability the role carries.
Job value: the combined importance of the role to the company.
Pay grade/band: the compensation destination for the job.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A platform engineering role may require rare technical skill, high effort during incidents, and strong responsibility for uptime. The funnel justifies a higher pay band than a routine support role even if the titles sound similar [verified from model knowledge, not source].

When to pull this out in a meeting: When leadership wants pay decisions to be based on title instead of actual work value.

### Pay Band Architecture Matrix
**Purpose:** Decide how jobs should be grouped into ranges.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
                    JOB VALUE
              similar                different
ROLE FAMILY
same         one band / broad band   separate bands
different    band overlap if close    distinct architecture
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Role family: related jobs such as analyst, lead, manager.
Job value: evaluated worth of the job.
Same family/similar value: can share a band.
Different family/different value: needs separate treatment.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product analyst and a data analyst may sit in one band if their evaluated job value is similar. A product manager and director of product should not, because responsibility and decision scope are materially different [verified from model knowledge, not source].

When to pull this out in a meeting: When HR is deciding whether two titles should share a compensation range.

### Internal Equity Risk Matrix
**Purpose:** Spot when pay differences are likely to create disputes.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
                    PAY DIFFERENCE
              small                     large
JOB VALUE
similar      low risk                 high dispute risk
different    acceptable spread       explain with bands
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Pay difference: the gap between salaries.
Job value similarity: whether the jobs evaluated close to the same level.
High risk zone: similar jobs paid very differently.
Mitigation: reband, relevel, or explain progression paths.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: If a junior operations role is paid almost the same as a team lead, employees will read that as a broken structure. The matrix shows that the fix may be pay-band redesign rather than an ad hoc raise [verified from model knowledge, not source].

When to pull this out in a meeting: When employees complain about fairness and management wants to know whether they have a real pay problem.

## 4. Formulas

The formulas below are [verified from model knowledge, not source].

### Formula 1: Point Factor Score
Formula: `Job score = Σ(weight_i * factor_i)`

Variables:
weight_i = importance assigned to a factor
factor_i = score for skill, effort, responsibility, or other criteria

Why this formula exists: It converts qualitative job differences into a structured numerical score.

How to interpret the output:
Higher score -> higher relative job value -> higher band placement likely
Similar score -> jobs may share a band
Large score gap -> separate pay treatment likely

Worked example with numbers: If skill is weighted 40% and scored 4, effort 20% and scored 3, and responsibility 40% and scored 5, the score is 4.2 on a five-point scale. That is strong evidence for a higher band than a role scoring 3.1 [verified from model knowledge, not source].

### Formula 2: Range Midpoint
Formula: `Midpoint = (Range minimum + Range maximum) / 2`

Variables:
Range minimum = lower end of the salary band
Range maximum = upper end of the salary band

Why this formula exists: It provides a reference point for promotions, new hires, and compa-ratio checks.

How to interpret the output:
Current pay well below midpoint -> growth room but possible underpay risk
Current pay near midpoint -> healthy band position
Current pay above midpoint -> monitor compression or exceptional performance

Worked example with numbers: If a band runs from 80,000 to 120,000, the midpoint is 100,000. A person paid 82,000 is early in the band; one paid 118,000 is near the ceiling [verified from model knowledge, not source].

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Don't compare people when the real question is job worth. | Do compare jobs using consistent factors. |
| Don't set salary ranges without a clear band logic. | Do build pay grades and ranges from evaluated job value. |
| Don't ignore internal equity until employees complain. | Do audit pay differences before disputes escalate. |
| Don't use a point-factor score without weights. | Do assign transparent weights to skill, effort, and responsibility. |
| Don't freeze the pay structure forever. | Do review bands as jobs and the business change. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: Designing pay grades for a 200-person company
**Situation:** A growing company has 200 employees and many titles. Leadership wants a pay structure that is defensible but not so complex that managers cannot use it.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Job Value Funnel + Pay Band Architecture Matrix.

**Analysis:** The company should score jobs, cluster similar-value roles, and create a manageable set of bands. If too many distinct bands appear, the structure will be hard to administer; if too few appear, internal equity problems will accumulate [verified from model knowledge, not source].

**Decision rule:** If roles score within a narrow range, group them. If scores differ materially, split the bands. If band overlap starts causing promotion confusion, redesign the architecture.

**Action:** Run a job-evaluation committee, score the roles, and publish the first draft pay structure.

### Scenario 2: Junior role paid close to a senior role
**Situation:** Employees complain that a junior role earns almost as much as a senior role. Managers think the issue is just market pressure, but HR suspects band compression.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Internal Equity Risk Matrix + Range Midpoint.

**Analysis:** If the job value gap is large but the pay gap is small, the issue is structure, not just market movement. The remedy may be rebanding, adjusting midpoints, or creating clearer progression steps [verified from model knowledge, not source].

**Decision rule:** If job value is similar, the pay gap can be small. If job value differs materially and pay does not, treat it as an equity risk. If many employees cluster near the top of a band, widen or split the band.

**Action:** Re-score the jobs, review the band design, and communicate the logic transparently.

### Scenario 3: Senior engineer versus team lead
**Situation:** A senior engineer and a team lead have similar pay today, but the team lead makes people decisions and owns delivery risk.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Point Factor Score + Pay Band Architecture Matrix.

**Analysis:** Responsibility and decision burden should increase the team lead’s score. If the point-factor method reflects that difference, the team lead belongs in a higher or at least more senior band [verified from model knowledge, not source].

**Decision rule:** If the evaluated score is higher, increase the band. If the score is close but not equal, keep the band but adjust progression. If the score is lower than expected, review the criteria.

**Action:** Re-score both roles, validate the weights, and align the pay grades to the new evaluation.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. Define the compensation philosophy before scoring jobs.
2. Evaluate jobs, not incumbents.
3. Use consistent factors such as skill, effort, and responsibility.
4. Convert job scores into pay grades and salary bands.
5. Check internal equity by comparing similar-value roles.
6. Review band placement against market data and promotion paths.
7. Revisit the structure periodically as jobs and the business change.
