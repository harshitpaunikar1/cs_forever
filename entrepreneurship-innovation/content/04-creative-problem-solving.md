# Creative Problem Solving

## Overview

Creative problem solving is a structured approach to finding novel solutions when standard answers do not work. It combines divergent thinking, which generates many possible ideas, with convergent thinking, which selects and refines the best ones. Entrepreneurs use it daily because they face problems that have no textbook answers.

---

## Why It Matters

Startups operate with fewer resources than established companies, so they cannot simply throw money at problems. A founder who can reframe a challenge and find an unconventional fix gains a cost and speed advantage. Creative solutions also differentiate a product in crowded markets where competitors copy features quickly.

## Key Principles

- Define the real problem before jumping to solutions; the obvious framing is often wrong
- Quantity of ideas matters early on; filter later
- Constraints can fuel creativity rather than block it
- Cross-pollination from unrelated fields often produces breakthrough ideas
- Rapid prototyping turns abstract ideas into testable options fast

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Divergent Thinking** | Generating many varied ideas without judging them, opening up the solution space |
| **Convergent Thinking** | Evaluating and narrowing ideas down to the most promising option |
| **Brainstorming** | A group technique for producing a large number of ideas in a short time, with criticism deferred |
| **Reframing** | Looking at a problem from a different angle to reveal solutions that were hidden under the original assumptions |

## Use Case

A delivery startup cannot afford to build its own fleet. Instead of buying vans, the founder partners with local taxi drivers during their off-peak hours, turning idle capacity into a delivery network at a fraction of the cost.

## Scenario

> A small cosmetics brand could not compete with big players on advertising spend. The founder reframed the challenge: instead of reaching millions, reach the right hundreds. She sent free samples to 200 micro-influencers in niche skincare communities. The resulting user-generated content drove three times more sales per dollar than the competitor's TV campaign.

## Examples

- Southwest Airlines solved the turnaround-time problem by studying NASCAR pit crews rather than other airlines, cutting gate time to 15 minutes
- A Kenyan startup solved rural internet access by mounting Wi-Fi hotspots on public buses that sync data at each stop, avoiding the cost of building cell towers

## 1. Topic Snapshot
Creative problem solving is the discipline of reframing hard entrepreneurial problems, generating many possible answers, and then selecting the ones that can actually be built, sold, and scaled.
For an IT, AI, Product, or Consulting leader, the point is not to be inventive for its own sake; it is to find a lower-cost, faster, and better answer when standard playbooks fail.
The decision it supports is which problem to solve, which constraints to exploit, and which prototype to test first.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Double Diamond
**Purpose:** Separate problem discovery from solution development.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Discover -> Define -> Develop -> Deliver
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: discover, which broadens the field and surfaces many possible problems.
Component 2: define, which narrows the problem to the one that matters.
Component 3: develop, which explores multiple solutions.
Component 4: deliver, which selects and tests the strongest option.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A startup thinks users want more features, but the double diamond reveals that the real problem is onboarding confusion. The team redefines the issue before building anything new.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When everyone is jumping to solutions before the problem is clear.

### IDEA Funnel
**Purpose:** Turn a large idea pool into a small set of testable options.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Many ideas -> clustered themes -> shortlist -> prototype -> test
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: volume, which matters early.
Component 2: clustering, which groups similar ideas.
Component 3: scoring, which ranks ideas by value and feasibility.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product team generates 40 onboarding ideas, clusters them into three themes, and prototypes the best one in a week rather than debating all 40 in detail.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When a workshop produces too many ideas and no next step.

### Constraint Inversion
**Purpose:** Use limits as design inputs instead of treating them as blockers.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Constraint -> design rule -> simpler solution
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: the constraint, such as budget, time, or access.
Component 2: the design rule, which forces a different approach.
Component 3: the resulting solution, which is often cheaper and faster.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A founder cannot afford a field sales team, so the company uses self-serve onboarding, community-led growth, and partner referrals instead.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When the team says "we can't do it" without asking what the constraint actually enables.

## 4. Formulas

Formula: Idea score = desirability x feasibility x viability
Variables:
desirability = customer pull
feasibility = buildability
viability = business economics
Why this formula exists: It forces teams to avoid ideas that are only exciting on one dimension.
How to interpret the output:
Low score in any dimension -> weak idea -> revise or reject
Balanced high score -> strong candidate -> prototype
Worked example with numbers: 4/5 desirability, 3/5 feasibility, and 4/5 viability gives a score of 48/125, which suggests a promising but not yet proven idea.

Formula: Prototype cycle time = idea to test in days
Variables:
days = elapsed time from concept to first user feedback
Why this formula exists: It rewards speed of learning, not just polish.
How to interpret the output:
Cycle time short -> fast learning -> better startup cadence
Cycle time long -> slow learning -> reduce scope
Worked example with numbers: A landing-page test launched in 3 days is usually more valuable than a perfect plan launched in 3 weeks.

Formula: Constraint leverage = outcome improvement / resource input
Variables:
outcome improvement = better speed, cost, or adoption
resource input = money, headcount, or time spent
Why this formula exists: It shows whether the constraint-based solution is truly efficient.
How to interpret the output:
Value high -> strong innovation leverage -> scale the method
Value low -> constraint workaround is not helping -> rethink the approach
Worked example with numbers: If a no-code automation saves 30 staff hours per week for 5 hours of setup, leverage is high.

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Solve the obvious problem without checking the root cause | Reframe before building |
| Generate one idea and treat it as the answer | Create volume, then filter |
| Ignore constraints as if they do not matter | Use constraints to shape the solution |
| Spend weeks polishing before feedback | Prototype fast and test early |
| Choose ideas that are only interesting | Score ideas for desirability, feasibility, and viability |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)
Scenario 1: Startup onboarding confusion
Situation: A SaaS startup thinks users need more features, but churn is concentrated in the first 10 minutes after signup.
Applicable framework/metric: Double Diamond and prototype cycle time.
Analysis: The problem is likely onboarding clarity, not missing features. A fast prototype can test whether a simpler first-run flow improves activation.
Decision rule: "If the real problem is unclear, do not build more product until the problem statement is validated."
Action: Reframe the issue, prototype a guided onboarding flow, and compare activation rates.

Scenario 2: Resource-limited market entry
Situation: A founder wants to enter a new market but cannot afford traditional sales coverage.
Applicable framework/metric: Constraint Inversion and constraint leverage.
Analysis: The budget constraint can produce a better model: partner channels, product-led growth, or community-led distribution may be cheaper and more scalable.
Decision rule: "If a constraint blocks the default plan, ask whether it points to a better plan."
Action: Replace field sales with self-serve trials, content, and referrals.

Scenario 3: Innovation workshop overload
Situation: An internal innovation team ends a workshop with 60 ideas and no clear next step.
Applicable framework/metric: IDEA Funnel and idea score.
Analysis: The team needs clustering and scoring, not more ideation. Feasibility and viability should eliminate ideas that look exciting but are impossible to execute.
Decision rule: "If the idea list is long, move to scoring and prototyping immediately."
Action: Group ideas into themes, score them, and run one prototype per theme.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Write the problem statement before discussing solutions.
2. Separate customer desirability from technical feasibility and business viability.
3. Generate enough ideas to create real choice.
4. Cluster ideas into themes so the team can compare options.
5. Use constraints as design prompts rather than excuses.
6. Build the smallest useful prototype and test it quickly.
7. Keep a learning log so failed ideas still improve the next cycle.
