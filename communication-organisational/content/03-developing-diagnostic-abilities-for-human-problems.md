# Developing Diagnostic Abilities for Human Problems in Complex Organizations

## Overview

Diagnostic ability is the skill of spotting the real cause behind people problems at work. Complex organizations have many moving parts, so surface symptoms like low productivity or high turnover often have deeper roots. A good diagnostician asks questions before prescribing solutions.

---

## Why It Matters

Jumping to a fix before understanding the problem wastes money and trust. A wrong solution often makes things worse, because people feel unheard and the real issue grows. Managers who diagnose well earn a reputation for being fair and effective.

## Key Principles

- Separate the symptom from the cause. Low output is a symptom, not a diagnosis.
- Look at the system, the individual, and the group together. Problems rarely live in one place.
- Collect data from multiple sources: interviews, observation, records, and surveys.
- Test your hypothesis with small actions before committing to large changes.
- Stay curious and neutral. Judgment shuts down honest conversation.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Diagnosis** | A structured process of identifying the true source of a human or organizational problem. |
| **Symptom** | A visible sign of trouble such as absenteeism, conflict, or errors. |
| **Root Cause** | The underlying driver that, once addressed, removes the symptom. |
| **Systems View** | Looking at how parts of the organization interact rather than in isolation. |
| **Pilot Test** | A small, low-risk trial of a proposed solution before full rollout. |

## Use Case

A software firm sees a sudden spike in junior engineer resignations. Instead of offering pay hikes, the manager runs exit interviews and finds the real issue is unclear career paths and poor feedback from senior engineers.

## Scenario

> A telecom call center struggled with rising customer complaints. Leaders first blamed agents and added training. A proper diagnosis revealed the real cause was a broken billing system that agents could not explain. Fixing the system cut complaints by sixty percent.

## Examples

- A plant manager notices conflict on the shop floor, interviews both shifts, and finds the handover process itself is unclear, not any single worker.
- An HR head studies team-level exit data and spots one department losing people twice as fast as the rest, pointing to a specific manager problem.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Diagnostic Funnel
**Purpose:** Move from symptom to root cause without jumping to solutions. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Symptom -> Pattern -> Hypothesis -> Evidence -> Root cause -> Test
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: symptom, which is the visible problem.
Component 2: pattern, which shows whether the symptom repeats in a certain group or context.
Component 3: hypothesis, which is the most likely explanation to test.
Component 4: test, which is the small action that confirms or rejects the hypothesis.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product team sees low feature adoption and assumes users are resistant, but the diagnostic funnel shows the real issue may be a confusing workflow or weak onboarding. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

When to pull this out in a meeting: When people want to jump straight from complaint to solution.

### Cause Map
**Purpose:** Separate individual, team, and system causes before acting. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

**Text Diagram:**
```text
              Problem
        /        |        \
   Individual   Team     System
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: individual causes, such as skill or motivation gaps.
Component 2: team causes, such as unclear handoffs or poor norms.
Component 3: system causes, such as broken tools, policies, or incentives.
Component 4: interaction effects, where more than one layer is contributing.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A consulting engagement has repeated missed deadlines; the cause map shows the delay is less about one consultant and more about unclear client approvals and ambiguous ownership. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

When to pull this out in a meeting: When blame is circulating but no one has separated layers of causality.

### Evidence Triangle
**Purpose:** Cross-check interviews, observation, and records before drawing a conclusion. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Interview
   \   |   /
    \  |  /
    Records
      / \
Observation
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: interviews, which capture lived experience and explanation.
Component 2: observation, which captures actual behavior.
Component 3: records, which capture historical pattern and scale.
Component 4: agreement, which is the degree to which the three sources line up.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: In an AI delivery team, developers say they are overloaded, observation shows long review waits, and ticket records confirm approval bottlenecks. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

When to pull this out in a meeting: When the story sounds plausible but needs corroboration.

## 4. Formulas

### Formula 1: Diagnosis Confidence
Formula: `diagnosis confidence = evidence sources x source agreement`
Variables:
`evidence sources` = count of independent inputs used
`source agreement` = how consistently those sources point to the same cause
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the diagnosis is strong enough to act on.
How to interpret the output:
Low score -> keep investigating
Moderate score -> test with a pilot
High score -> act with confidence, but still monitor
Worked example with numbers: If you use 3 sources and agreement is 0.8, the score is 2.4. Decision: the hypothesis is credible, but a pilot test is still prudent. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

### Formula 2: Symptom Recurrence Rate
Formula: `symptom recurrence rate = repeated symptom cases / total cases`
Variables:
`repeated symptom cases` = how often the same issue comes back
`total cases` = all observed cases of the problem
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the fix addressed the root cause or only the surface problem.
How to interpret the output:
Low rate -> fix is holding
Medium rate -> partial root cause addressed
High rate -> wrong cause or incomplete fix
Worked example with numbers: If a team sees the same complaint in 6 out of 10 cases after a training program, the recurrence rate is 60%. Decision: the issue is probably system-level, not just skill-level. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

### Formula 3: Pilot Lift
Formula: `pilot lift = post-pilot improvement - baseline`
Variables:
`post-pilot improvement` = change after the small test
`baseline` = the starting level before the test
Why this formula exists: It answers whether the small action actually improved the problem.
How to interpret the output:
Negative or zero -> hypothesis likely wrong
Small positive -> refine the intervention
Large positive -> scale the solution
Worked example with numbers: If complaint volume drops from 100 to 70 after a billing fix, pilot lift is 30%. Decision: expand the fix and continue watching for side effects. [verified from model knowledge, not source]

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Treat a visible symptom as the diagnosis. | Separate the symptom from the root cause. |
| Blame one person before checking the system. | Examine the individual, team, and system together. |
| Rely on a single source of evidence. | Cross-check interviews, observation, records, and surveys. |
| Launch a big fix before testing it. | Run a small pilot first. |
| Argue from certainty when you are still guessing. | Stay neutral and ask better questions. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: Junior engineer resignations
Situation: A software firm sees a spike in junior engineer resignations and initially considers pay hikes. Exit interviews, however, show the real issue is unclear career paths and poor feedback from senior engineers. [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Applicable framework/metric: Diagnostic Funnel and Diagnosis Confidence.
Analysis: The symptom is turnover, but the pattern points to a development and feedback system problem. If interviews, records, and surveys all align, the diagnosis is strong. [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Decision rule: If evidence points to the same root cause from multiple sources, fix the system. If evidence conflicts, keep investigating. If a pilot improves retention signals, scale the change.
Action: Map career-path gaps, reset feedback norms, and test a manager coaching pilot.

### Scenario 2: Customer complaints in a telecom call center
Situation: A telecom call center gets more complaints and leaders first blame agents. A diagnosis reveals the billing system is broken, which agents cannot explain clearly to customers. [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Applicable framework/metric: Cause Map and Symptom Recurrence Rate.
Analysis: Training agents alone will not solve a system defect. If complaints keep recurring after the training fix, the recurrence rate tells you the cause is elsewhere. [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Decision rule: If recurrence stays above 40% after a fix, revisit the root cause. If recurrence drops sharply, keep the intervention. If the system is failing, address it before retraining people.
Action: Escalate the billing issue, give agents a temporary explanation script, and track repeat complaints.

### Scenario 3: Shop-floor conflict across shifts
Situation: A plant manager sees conflict on the shop floor and suspects a bad actor. Interviews with both shifts instead show the handoff process is unclear and each side thinks the other is not doing its part. [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Applicable framework/metric: Evidence Triangle and Pilot Lift.
Analysis: Observation and records can confirm whether the handoff really fails at the boundary. A small handoff checklist pilot can show whether clarity improves the conflict. [verified from model knowledge, not source]
Decision rule: If the pilot reduces conflict, standardize it. If the pilot has no effect, the cause is likely deeper. If the data is mixed, refine the handoff design.
Action: Introduce a shared shift checklist and review results after two weeks.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Write the symptom in one sentence before proposing a fix.
2. Collect at least three types of evidence before naming a cause.
3. Separate individual, team, and system explanations.
4. Form a testable hypothesis instead of a broad opinion.
5. Run a low-risk pilot before scaling the intervention.
6. Re-check the original symptom after the fix to confirm it is actually gone.
