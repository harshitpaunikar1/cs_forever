# Customer Acquisition Cost

## Overview

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is the total amount of money a business spends to acquire one new customer. It includes all marketing and sales expenses — advertising, salaries, tools, creative production — divided by the number of new customers gained in that period. CAC is one of the most important metrics for any business because it tells you whether your growth is profitable or unsustainable.

---

## Why It Matters

If it costs you more to acquire a customer than that customer will ever spend with you, your business model is broken. Tracking CAC ensures growth is sustainable, helps you compare channels, and signals when it's time to invest more in retention (which is almost always cheaper than acquisition).

## Key Principles

- Always compare CAC to customer lifetime value (LTV); a healthy ratio is typically 3:1 or better
- CAC varies by channel, segment, and time period — measure each separately for actionable insights
- Reducing CAC isn't just about cutting costs; improving conversion rates at each funnel stage has the same effect
- Blended CAC hides channel-level inefficiencies; always drill down

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** | Total marketing and sales spend divided by the number of new customers acquired |
| **LTV:CAC Ratio** | The relationship between customer lifetime value and acquisition cost; indicates profitability |
| **Payback Period** | The time it takes for a customer's revenue to cover the cost of acquiring them |
| **Blended CAC** | The average acquisition cost across all channels and campaigns combined |

## Use Case

A subscription meal service calculates that its blended CAC is $55. The average customer subscribes for six months at $40/month, generating $240 in revenue with $140 in costs, netting $100 in gross profit. Since $100 exceeds the $55 CAC, growth is sustainable. But channel-level analysis shows that influencer marketing has a $32 CAC while podcast ads have a $94 CAC, guiding budget decisions.

## Scenario

> A fintech startup was growing users rapidly through paid social ads, celebrating month-over-month sign-up records. But when the finance team calculated CAC, it was $180 per user — and the average user generated only $120 in lifetime revenue. The company shifted focus to referral programs and content marketing, bringing CAC down to $65 within two quarters while maintaining growth.

## Examples

- An online education platform discovers that webinar attendees convert at 15% versus 2% for cold ad traffic, so it invests more in webinar production to lower CAC
- A SaaS company tracks CAC by sales rep and finds that one rep's CAC is half the team average, then studies her approach to train the rest of the team

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

The structures below are analytical enrichments [verified from model knowledge, not source].

### CAC Build-Up Stack
**Purpose:** Show how all acquisition costs accumulate before the first purchase.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Ads + Sales + Tools + Creative + Agency + Overhead -> CAC
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Ads: media spend.
Sales: rep time and commissions.
Tools: software and infrastructure.
Creative: production and testing.
Agency: outsourced support.
Overhead: shared acquisition support cost.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A SaaS firm cannot count only ad spend if sales engineers, demo tools, and onboarding support are required to convert the lead. A consulting practice cannot ignore proposal and partner time either.

When to pull this out in a meeting: Use it when someone quotes a CAC that excludes major costs.

### CAC Health Matrix
**Purpose:** Decide whether acquisition economics are healthy enough to scale.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
                    Low CAC             High CAC
High LTV            Scale               Test and scale carefully
Low LTV             Fix retention       Stop or redesign
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
CAC: acquisition spend per customer.
LTV: value created per customer.
Scale: expand the channel or segment.
Test and scale carefully: improve controls first.
Fix retention: raise value before spending more.
Stop or redesign: the model may be broken.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product team may accept a high CAC for a large enterprise account if the LTV is strong. But if churn is high and renewals are weak, the same CAC is unsafe.

When to pull this out in a meeting: Use it when growth is fast but finance is worried.

### Funnel Leak Map
**Purpose:** Identify where acquisition cost is being wasted.

**Text Diagram:**
```text
Impression -> Click -> Lead -> Qualified lead -> Customer
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Impression: the audience saw the message.
Click: interest was strong enough to engage.
Lead: the person shared contact data.
Qualified lead: the lead fits the buying criteria.
Customer: the buyer converted.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: If the click rate is fine but lead quality is low, the CAC problem is not media cost alone. It may be the offer, landing page, or targeting.

When to pull this out in a meeting: Use it when no one knows which funnel stage is leaking.

## 4. Formulas

The formulas below are [verified from model knowledge, not source].

Formula: CAC = Total acquisition cost / New customers acquired
Variables:
Total acquisition cost = all spend tied to winning customers
New customers acquired = count of customers won in the same period
Why this formula exists: It sets the baseline acquisition unit cost.
How to interpret the output:
Lower CAC -> more efficient growth
Higher CAC -> more expensive growth
Worked example with numbers: If a company spends $120,000 to acquire 2,000 customers, CAC is $60.

Formula: LTV:CAC ratio = Lifetime value / CAC
Variables:
Lifetime value = gross margin value over customer life
CAC = acquisition cost per customer
Why this formula exists: It tests whether the customer is worth the acquisition spend.
How to interpret the output:
Below 1 -> value destruction
Around 3 -> healthy for many models
Well above 3 -> attractive, but confirm assumptions
Worked example with numbers: If LTV is $240 and CAC is $60, the ratio is 4:1.

Formula: Payback period = CAC / Monthly gross profit per customer
Variables:
CAC = acquisition cost
Monthly gross profit per customer = revenue minus variable cost
Why this formula exists: It tells the business how fast cash comes back.
How to interpret the output:
Shorter is better for cash flow
Long payback -> financing pressure
Worked example with numbers: If CAC is $90 and gross profit per month is $15, payback is 6 months.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Judge CAC without LTV | Compare CAC to lifetime value and payback |
| Use a single blended number for everything | Break CAC out by channel, segment, and period |
| Ignore sales and tools in the cost base | Include all meaningful acquisition costs |
| Celebrate lead volume without conversion | Track funnel leakage and lead quality |
| Cut spend blindly | Fix the underlying funnel stage first |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: Paid social is cheap but unprofitable
**Situation:** A consumer subscription brand acquires lots of users through paid social, but many churn in month one.

**Applicable framework/metric:** CAC Build-Up Stack + LTV:CAC ratio.

**Analysis:** The channel may look efficient on the surface, but churn destroys the value. If LTV collapses, the ratio will tell the truth even if CAC seems low.

**Decision rule:** If LTV:CAC falls below 3:1 and payback is too long, fix retention or reduce spend. If the ratio improves after retention changes, scaling becomes safer.

**Action:** Refine targeting and improve onboarding before increasing spend.

### Scenario 2: B2B leads are cheap but unqualified
**Situation:** A software vendor's cost per lead is low, but very few leads become customers.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Funnel Leak Map + lead-to-customer rate.

**Analysis:** The real problem is poor lead quality or weak sales qualification. Cheap leads are not good leads if the funnel collapses before conversion.

**Decision rule:** If CPL is low but lead-to-customer rate is weak, tighten targeting and qualification. If qualified lead rate improves, CAC should fall naturally.

**Action:** Rework the offer and routing rules so sales sees fewer but better leads.

### Scenario 3: Consulting proposal cycle is too expensive
**Situation:** A consulting team spends heavily on proposal development, partner time, and presales support.

**Applicable framework/metric:** CAC Health Matrix + payback period.

**Analysis:** In consulting, CAC is often hidden inside selling effort. If the payback period is too long or the LTV is weak, the practice may be overselling low-value engagements.

**Decision rule:** If CAC is high but LTV is also high, stay selective. If CAC is high and LTV is weak, redesign the offering or stop chasing the segment.

**Action:** Focus on higher-value accounts and lower-touch acquisition channels.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. Define exactly which costs belong in CAC before reporting it.
2. Calculate blended CAC and channel-level CAC side by side.
3. Add LTV and payback period so CAC is not read in isolation.
4. Split CAC by segment, channel, and time period.
5. Diagnose the funnel stage causing the highest waste.
6. Improve conversion before cutting too aggressively.
7. Scale only when acquisition cost is justified by lifetime value.
