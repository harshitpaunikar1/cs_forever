# Social Media Marketing

## Overview

Social media marketing uses platforms like Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, and X to build brand awareness, engage audiences, and drive sales. It includes both organic activities (posting content, responding to comments, building community) and paid social advertising (targeted ads served to specific demographics). The key difference from traditional advertising is the two-way conversation it enables.

---

## Why It Matters

Billions of people spend hours daily on social platforms. Brands that show up consistently with valuable, relevant content earn trust and stay top-of-mind. Social media also provides real-time feedback, letting companies spot trends, handle complaints, and adapt messaging faster than any other channel.

## Key Principles

- Each platform has its own culture and content format; what works on LinkedIn won't work on TikTok
- Consistency in posting schedule and brand voice builds audience trust over time
- Engagement (comments, shares, saves) matters more than vanity metrics like follower count
- Paid social allows precise targeting by demographics, interests, behavior, and lookalike audiences

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Engagement Rate** | The percentage of people who interact with your content relative to those who see it |
| **Organic Reach** | The number of unique users who see your content without paid promotion |
| **Lookalike Audience** | A targeting option that finds new users similar to your existing customers |
| **UGC (User-Generated Content)** | Content created by customers or fans that a brand can share or repurpose |

## Use Case

A sportswear brand launches a hashtag challenge on TikTok encouraging users to post workout videos wearing its gear. The challenge generates 12 million views in two weeks, and the brand repurposes the best user-generated videos as paid ads, cutting creative production costs by 60%.

## Scenario

> A B2B cybersecurity firm struggled to generate leads on social media because it only posted product announcements. After shifting to a strategy of short educational LinkedIn posts — explaining real breach scenarios in plain language — engagement tripled and inbound demo requests from LinkedIn grew by 45% in one quarter.

## Examples

- A coffee chain uses Instagram Stories polls to let followers vote on the next seasonal flavor, driving engagement and making customers feel involved in product decisions
- A nonprofit runs a Facebook fundraiser with a matching-donation mechanic, raising $80,000 in 48 hours through shares and peer-to-peer promotion

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### 3.1 Platform-Content Fit Matrix
**Purpose:** Match the content format to the platform culture instead of reposting the same asset everywhere.

```
LinkedIn   -> insight, proof, expertise
Instagram  -> visual story, aspiration, lifestyle
TikTok     -> speed, entertainment, discovery
X          -> opinion, commentary, trend response
Facebook   -> community, local, groups, utility
```

**Components explained:**
Platform culture: what people expect there.
Format: short video, carousel, post, live, story, group post.
Message: the level of depth and tone.

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** a cybersecurity firm might publish breach-analysis threads on LinkedIn, quick threat explainer videos on TikTok, and community updates in a private group. The decision is not to be everywhere; it is to be useful in the place where the audience is already paying attention.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** when one creative asset is being forced across every platform.

### 3.2 Organic, Paid, and Community Triangle
**Purpose:** Balance the three major ways social creates value.

```
          Community
           /    \
      Organic -- Paid
```

**Components explained:**
Organic: trust and consistency built without media spend.
Paid: scale and targeting.
Community: loyalty, feedback, and advocacy.

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** an AI product team uses organic posts to teach the category, paid social to retarget engaged users, and a community forum to turn power users into advocates. The triangle shows when each motion is doing its job and where the weak link is.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** when the brand is over-dependent on paid ads or on vanity organic reach.

### 3.3 Social Content Test Loop
**Purpose:** Turn social into a learning machine instead of a posting calendar.

```
Post -> Measure -> Learn -> Adapt -> Post
```

**Components explained:**
Post: release one variable or hypothesis.
Measure: read engagement, clicks, and downstream conversion.
Learn: identify what topic, hook, or format worked.
Adapt: apply the lesson to the next post batch.

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** a consulting team tests three hooks for the same case study: business outcome, technical insight, and executive risk. If the risk framing doubles demo requests, the team adopts it for the next month of content.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** when the team wants a content calendar but not a learning loop.

### 3.4 Engagement Funnel
**Purpose:** Show how attention moves from exposure to action.

```
Impression -> Engagement -> Click -> Lead -> Customer -> Advocate
```

**Components explained:**
Impression: content is seen.
Engagement: the audience reacts.
Click: the audience moves off-platform.
Lead: the visitor expresses interest.
Customer: the lead converts.
Advocate: the customer shares or creates UGC.

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** a D2C brand may get lots of impressions but low clicks because the content entertains without selling. The funnel makes clear whether the problem is top-of-funnel awareness or deeper conversion friction.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** when followers and likes look good but revenue does not move.

## 4. Formulas

1. **Engagement Rate**
   Formula: `Engagement rate = Interactions / Reach`
   Variables:
   `Interactions` = likes, comments, shares, saves, replies
   `Reach` = unique users who saw the content
   Why this formula exists: it measures whether the message resonated enough to trigger action.
   How to interpret the output:
   Low rate → weak hook or poor platform fit
   Moderate rate → content is acceptable but not outstanding
   High rate → strong relevance and resonance
   Worked example with numbers: 1,200 interactions / 30,000 reach = 4% engagement rate.

2. **Organic Reach Rate**
   Formula: `Organic reach rate = Organic reach / Total followers`
   Variables:
   `Organic reach` = unique non-paid exposure
   `Total followers` = audience base
   Why this formula exists: it shows how much of the audience the platform is actually showing your content to.
   How to interpret the output:
   Low rate → distribution problem or weak engagement
   High rate → strong platform visibility
   Worked example with numbers: 15,000 organic reach / 100,000 followers = 15% organic reach rate.

3. **Cost per Acquisition**
   Formula: `CPA = Ad spend / Conversions`
   Variables:
   `Ad spend` = social ad spend in the period
   `Conversions` = desired outcomes such as signups or purchases
   Why this formula exists: it shows whether paid social is economically viable.
   How to interpret the output:
   CPA below gross margin contribution → scalable
   CPA near margin contribution → fragile
   CPA above margin contribution → unprofitable
   Worked example with numbers: $12,000 ad spend / 240 conversions = $50 CPA.

4. **Cost per Engagement**
   Formula: `CPE = Ad spend / Engagements`
   Variables:
   `Ad spend` = campaign spend
   `Engagements` = likes, comments, shares, saves, etc.
   Why this formula exists: it helps compare creative effectiveness across campaigns.
   How to interpret the output:
   Low CPE → creative is pulling interaction efficiently
   High CPE → creative is stale or poorly targeted
   Worked example with numbers: $4,000 spend / 2,000 engagements = $2 CPE.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Don't post the same asset everywhere and expect platform-native results. | Do adapt message, format, and length for each platform. |
| Don't judge success only by follower count. | Do prioritize engagement, clicks, leads, and revenue. |
| Don't let paid social run without audience and creative testing. | Do refresh creative before fatigue drives costs up. |
| Don't ignore the community layer. | Do turn comments, DMs, and UGC into a real feedback loop. |
| Don't treat social as only brand awareness. | Do connect social activity to pipeline, conversion, or sales. |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: B2B Cybersecurity on LinkedIn
Situation: A cybersecurity firm gets weak lead flow from polished product posts. It switches to educational breach scenarios and plain-language risk commentary.
Applicable framework/metric: Platform-Content Fit Matrix and Engagement Rate.
Analysis: If engagement triples and demo requests rise 45%, the new format is not merely prettier; it is commercially better. The firm should measure reach, engagement, and lead conversion together.
Decision rule: If engagement rises but leads do not, tighten the CTA. If leads rise, scale the format and reuse the message structure.
Action: Build a weekly expert-content cadence, repurpose founder insights, and use retargeting on users who engage with technical posts.

### Scenario 2: TikTok UGC Campaign
Situation: A sportswear brand launches a hashtag challenge and users create thousands of videos. Paid performance improves because the brand repurposes top UGC into ads.
Applicable framework/metric: Organic, Paid, and Community Triangle.
Analysis: If 12 million views generate a large pool of reusable creator content, media cost falls while credibility rises. The value is not just reach; it is lower creative cost and higher trust.
Decision rule: If UGC quality is high and engagement persists, keep the challenge running. If participation declines, refresh the prompt and reward structure.
Action: Build a creator whitelist, license winning clips, and turn UGC into both organic posts and paid ad variants.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. Pick one strategic objective for social: awareness, lead gen, community, or conversion.
2. Match each platform to a content format it naturally rewards.
3. Define a content cadence that the team can sustain for 90 days.
4. Track engagement, reach, clicks, and conversions in one dashboard.
5. Refresh creative before frequency and fatigue push CPA upward.
6. Turn comments, DMs, and mentions into a documented feedback channel.
7. Reuse top-performing UGC, posts, and hooks across paid and organic social.
