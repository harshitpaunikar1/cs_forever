# Product and Service Development Stages

## Overview

New product development (NPD) moves through predictable stages: idea, concept, business case, design, build, test, launch, and post-launch. Each stage has a gate — a decision to go forward, rework, or kill. The stages apply to physical products and digital services alike, with adjustments for speed.

---

## Why It Matters

Skipping stages leads to expensive rework: a product that fails safety testing after tooling is built, or a service launched without operations trained. Running every stage well keeps risk low, timelines honest, and investment focused only on ideas that keep proving themselves.

## Key Principles

- Use stage-gates to kill weak ideas early, not late.
- Involve cross-functional teams from stage one, not just at launch.
- Document assumptions at each stage and retest them.
- Spend little money early, more money as confidence grows.
- Treat "go to next stage" as a real decision, not a formality.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Stage-Gate** | A checkpoint where leadership decides go / hold / kill. |
| **Concept Testing** | Showing an early idea to target users to gauge interest. |
| **Business Case** | The financial and strategic justification for the investment. |
| **Pilot Launch** | A limited release to validate operations before full rollout. |
| **Post-Launch Review** | A structured look-back to capture lessons and track outcomes. |

## Use Case

An appliance maker moving from concept to launch uses seven stages: scoping, business case, development, testing, pilot, launch, post-launch. The team kills 60% of concepts before the business-case gate, saving an estimated 40% of R&D budget.

## Scenario

> A hotel chain wanted to launch a new "digital concierge" service. They ran a two-week concept test in one property, found guests preferred voice over chat, redesigned at the design stage, and piloted in five properties before rolling out to 200. The disciplined stage approach cut post-launch defects by 70%.

## Examples

- An auto company tests three sketched SUV concepts with clinics before approving clay-model development.
- A bank pilots a new loan product in two cities for three months, measures default rate, then scales nationally.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Framework 1: Classic Stage-Gate Funnel (Cooper)
**Purpose:** Visualise kill rates and investment intensity across stages.

**Text Diagram:**
```
  Many ideas ──► Scoping ──► Business ──► Development ──► Testing ──► Pilot ──► Launch ──► Post-Launch
                              Case

         Idea gate│  Scoping gate│  Business gate│  Dev gate │Test gate│Pilot│
                  │              │                │           │          │gate │
                  ▼              ▼                ▼           ▼          ▼     ▼
              Kill rates typical:
                  60%            40%              25%         10%       5%    2%

     Investment curve:
                  $           $$                $$$         $$$$    $$$$$  $$$$$$
                  (cheap early, expensive late)
```

Components:
- Funnel shape: many ideas → few launches
- Gates enforce kill/go decisions at pre-defined criteria
- Investment rises with stage; so must confidence

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** A consumer-IoT company runs 50 ideas through stages per year.
- Scoping kills 30 (60%). Remaining 20.
- Business case kills 8 (40%). Remaining 12.
- Development kills 3 (25%). Remaining 9.
- Testing kills 1. 8 pilot.
- Pilot filters to 4 launched.
- Funnel ratio: 50 → 4 = 92% kill rate. Healthy discipline.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** NPD portfolio reviews; resource-allocation meetings; any discussion where "we should build everything" sentiment arises.

---

### Framework 2: Lean Stage-Gate (Cooper 2017)
**Purpose:** Marry classical stage-gate with build-measure-learn; faster, evidence-based.

**Text Diagram:**
```
  Classical:  Scope → BC → Dev → Test → Pilot → Launch → Post
              (slow, sequential, document-heavy)

  Lean:       Scope → MVP1 → Customer test → MVP2 → Customer test → Pilot → Launch
              (fast, iterative, evidence-heavy)

  Key differences:
  - Evidence > opinions at each gate
  - MVPs (not specs) presented
  - Pivot allowed at any gate
  - Gates can be parallel, not strictly sequential
```

Components:
- Keep gates; replace document-only deliverables with evidence-based ones (prototypes, experiments)
- Encourage pivots as legitimate outcomes

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** A SaaS firm adapts stage-gate for a 90-day innovation cadence.
- Stage 1 (2 weeks): discover + brief
- Stage 2 (2 weeks): smoke test — landing page ads
- Stage 3 (3 weeks): Wizard-of-Oz MVP with 5 users
- Stage 4 (3 weeks): functional MVP with 20 users
- Stage 5 (2 weeks): pilot with design partners
- Launch decision at week 12

Each gate requires measurable evidence. Pivots logged but not penalised.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** When classical stage-gate is slowing down digital product teams; when an innovation programme lacks rigor.

### Framework 3: Risk-Weighted Gate Criteria
**Purpose:** Make "go/hold/kill" decisions objective across three risk axes.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Gate criterion         │ Stage 1 (Scope)    │ Stage 2 (BC)       │ Stage 3 (Dev)
 ───────────────────────┼────────────────────┼────────────────────┼────────────────────
 MARKET RISK             │ Qualitative        │ Quantified TAM     │ Concept-test ≥ X
                         │ customer interest  │ Sean Ellis ≥ 30%   │ NPS ≥ 20
                         │ (5+ positive       │
                         │  interviews)      │
 TECHNICAL RISK           │ Feasibility        │ Architecture       │ Prototype works
                         │ sketch             │ diagram + spike    │ to spec
 BUSINESS RISK             │ Strategic fit     │ NPV > 0 at WACC    │ Refined NPV with
                         │ (yes/no)          │ + risk premium;    │ bottom-up data;
                         │                   │ payback < target   │ IRR > hurdle
 GATE DECISION             │ Go / Hold / Kill  │ Go / Hold / Kill   │ Go / Hold / Kill
```

Components:
- Three risk axes at every gate: market, technical, business
- Criteria get stricter as stages progress and investment rises

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** A fintech ships an AI-credit scoring product. Stage 3 (Development) gate:
- Market risk: concept test with 20 banks → 14 "very interested" (70%) ✓
- Technical risk: model AUC 0.82 in pilot dataset ✓
- Business risk: refined NPV +$8M at 15% WACC ✓

Gate: Go.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Stage-gate reviews. Ensures decisions aren't made on vibes.

## 4. Formulas

### Formula 1: Stage-Gate Kill Ratio
**Formula:** `Kill Ratio at Gate n = (Killed at Gate n) / (Entering Gate n)`

**Variables:**
- Tracked per gate + overall

**Why this formula exists:** Measures whether gates are actually killing weak ideas or rubber-stamping them.

**How to interpret the output:**
- Early gates (scope, BC): 40–70% kill typical
- Later gates: lower kill but higher investment on survivors
- If kill ratio is near 0 at early gates → gates are not functioning as filters

**Worked example:** Team tracked over 2 years:
- Gate 1: 48/80 killed (60%) ✓ healthy
- Gate 2: 12/32 killed (37%) ✓ healthy
- Gate 3: 0/20 killed (0%) → ✗ warning — team has "commit and ship" mindset; review quality of Gate 3 criteria.

**Data source:** NPD tracker in Jira Portfolio / Airtable / Notion.

---

### Formula 2: Stage-Gate Investment Productivity
**Formula:** `Productivity = NPV of launched products / Total R&D spend (all stages)`

**Variables:**
- NPV of launched: realized financial value of products that made it through
- Total R&D = including killed ideas' sunk cost

**Why this formula exists:** Measures the whole NPD funnel's ROI, not just winners.

**How to interpret the output:**
- > 3 → strong funnel
- 1–3 → typical
- < 1 → funnel destroying value

**Worked example:** 2-year NPD: launched products NPV $24M; total R&D spend $12M (includes $7M on 20 killed). Productivity = 2.0 → typical. If Kill Ratio were higher earlier, killed spend would be lower, productivity higher.

**Data source:** Finance + NPD tracker; quarterly review.

---

### Formula 3: Concept Testing Score
**Formula:** `Concept Score = (% Very interested) + 0.5 × (% Somewhat interested) − (% Negative)`

**Variables:**
- Survey-based concept test across target segment
- Weighted for intent polarity

**Why this formula exists:** Simple scalar to gate move from concept to business case.

**How to interpret the output:**
- Score > 40 → strong concept
- 20–40 → moderate; refine
- < 20 → weak; kill or pivot

**Worked example:** Concept test of a legal-AI assistant: 45% very interested, 30% somewhat, 15% neutral, 10% negative.
- Score = 45 + 0.5 × 30 − 10 = **50** → strong. Proceed to BC.

**Data source:** Concept-test survey (Typeform, Qualtrics) with target segment; 100+ respondents.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Use gates as rubber-stamps | Pre-commit criteria; kill when thresholds not met |
| Apply classical stage-gate to purely digital fast-cadence products | Use Lean Stage-Gate; evidence > documents |
| Skip post-launch review | Scheduled 30/60/90 + 12-month review; lessons captured |
| Measure NPD success by launches alone | Measure productivity (NPV) + kill discipline (kill ratio) |
| Invest equally across stages | Invest lightly early; heavily only after gates pass |
| Treat gate decisions as PM-only | Cross-functional gates: Product, Eng, Finance, Marketing, Ops |
| Ignore regulatory gates in regulated industries | Build regulatory milestones into the plan at stage 1 |
| Run gates without risk-axis scoring | Score each gate on market + technical + business risk |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: AI Platform Introducing Stage-Gate Discipline
**Situation:** A 200-person AI platform company ships 20 products a year, 15 don't hit $1M ARR. No kill discipline.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Lean Stage-Gate + Kill Ratio.

**Analysis:**
- Kill rate today: 10% at gates (most survive regardless of evidence)
- Install 6-stage Lean Stage-Gate with evidence-based gates
- Target kill ratios: G1 50%, G2 40%, G3 20%
- Expected R&D productivity: rises from 0.8 → 2.5

**Decision rule:** Evidence-based gates with hard criteria; VP of Product signs off on go/kill.

**Action (Monday morning):** Pilot Lean Stage-Gate on 3 current initiatives; measure kill + cycle time; roll out broadly next quarter.

---

### Scenario 2: Consulting Firm Helping Client Set Up NPD Portfolio
**Situation:** A consumer-goods client launches 8 SKUs/year, half fail. Wants to systematise.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Classic Stage-Gate + Concept Score + Investment Productivity.

**Analysis:**
- Install 7-stage classical (appropriate for physical product)
- Add concept-testing requirement between Scope → Business Case
- Require Concept Score ≥ 40 to pass
- Target productivity ratio > 2.5

**Decision rule:** SKUs that fail concept test are killed unless strategic reason (executive override with doc).

**Action:** Roll out in next 12 months. Install dashboard tracking kill ratio per gate. Quarterly exec review.

---

### Scenario 3 (Anti-example): Gates as Rubber Stamps
**Situation:** A tech firm has a formal 7-stage process but "nobody gets killed." 100% of ideas that enter stage 1 make it through to launch.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Kill Ratio audit.

**Analysis (what goes wrong):**
- Gates are administrative, not gate-keeping
- Execs don't kill because team has already "committed"
- Result: mediocre products ship; 40% underperform hurdle rate
- R&D productivity 0.6

**Cost of this mistake:** $20M/year in R&D yielding $12M in realised NPV. Gap: $8M.

**Decision rule:** No stage-gate system is legitimate without real kills; install explicit kill quotas.

**Action:** Mandate: each gate must kill ≥ 25% historically over 4 quarters. Build incentive: team that kills early gets priority funding for next idea.

## 7. Implementation Playbook

1. **Define stages + gate criteria per product archetype** — hardware vs SaaS vs AI service get different stage depths.
2. **Publish gate-criteria template** — three risk axes, hard metrics, named decider.
3. **Build an NPD funnel dashboard** — Tableau / Metabase; tracks ideas in each stage, kill rates, cycle time.
4. **Institute concept-test minimum sample size** — 100+ respondents for consumer; 15+ interviews for B2B.
5. **Link business case to NPV + IRR + sensitivity** — use template from `business-analytics/11-financial-analytics.md`.
6. **Run quarterly NPD portfolio review** — exec committee; look at kill ratios, productivity, and stuck-in-stage items.
7. **30/60/90/365 day post-launch reviews** — required; lessons captured in knowledge base.
8. **For regulated products, build reg gate into plan** — FDA / CE / UL / SOC-2 / HIPAA — explicit milestones.
