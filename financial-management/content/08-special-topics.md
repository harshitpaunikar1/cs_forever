
# Portfolio Theory & Asset Pricing Models

## Overview

Portfolio theory shows how mixing assets can reduce risk. Asset pricing models explain why some investments require higher return.

## Why It Matters

It supports investment choices and helps estimate expected returns used in corporate finance.

## Key Principles

- Diversification reduces unsystematic risk
- Efficient portfolios offer best return for a given risk
- Market risk is what matters most for pricing

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Efficient frontier** | Best possible portfolios set |
| **Systematic risk** | Market-wide risk that can’t be diversified away |
| **APT** | Alternative model using multiple risk factors |

## Use Case

Creating a balanced portfolio for a pension fund.

## Scenario

> An investor stops buying only one sector and diversifies, reducing portfolio swings.

## Examples

- Adding bonds to stocks can reduce volatility.
- Two stocks with low correlation diversify better than similar stocks.

# Real Options

## Overview

Real options treat business choices like options: you can delay, expand, stop, or switch plans based on what happens.

## Why It Matters

Traditional NPV may undervalue flexibility. Real options capture the value of “waiting” or “changing course.”

## Key Principles

- Value flexibility under uncertainty
- Identify decision points (expand/abandon)
- Use scenarios and option thinking
- Don’t commit too early if uncertainty is high

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Timing option** | Wait to invest |
| **Growth option** | Expand if things go well |
| **Abandon option** | Exit if things go badly |

## Use Case

Phased rollout of a new product instead of nationwide launch.

## Scenario

> A firm launches in one city first. If demand is strong, it expands; if weak, it stops early—saving money.

## Examples

- Mining project pauses until commodity prices improve.
- Tech firm builds a pilot before full-scale investment.

# Providing & Obtaining Credit (Web Ch. 27)

## Overview

Credit means allowing someone to pay later. Companies both offer credit to customers and take credit from suppliers/banks.

## Why It Matters

Credit can boost sales, but late payments hurt cash flow and increase bad debts.

## Key Principles

- Check customer creditworthiness
- Set limits and clear terms
- Incentivize early payment
- Track overdue accounts

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Credit terms** | Payment rules (e.g., net 30) |
| **Credit limit** | Maximum allowed unpaid amount |
| **Bad debt** | Uncollectible receivable |

## Use Case

Creating a credit policy for B2B customers.

## Scenario

> A wholesaler tightens credit after repeated late payments and improves cash flow.

## Examples

- “2/10, net 30” discount encourages fast payment.
- Credit insurance reduces loss from customer default.

# Advanced Cash Management & Inventory Control (Web Ch. 28)

## Overview

This focuses on managing cash and inventory more efficiently—keeping enough, but not too much.

## Why It Matters

Too much inventory locks cash. Too little inventory loses sales. Better control improves profit and liquidity.

## Key Principles

- Forecast cash daily/weekly where needed
- Maintain a target cash balance
- Use techniques to reduce idle cash
- Optimize reorder levels for inventory

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Target cash balance** | Minimum safe cash level |
| **EOQ idea** | Optimal order size (concept) |
| **Stockout** | Running out of inventory |

## Use Case

Reducing inventory holding costs while maintaining service levels.

## Scenario

> A store uses demand data to reorder smarter, reducing overstock and freeing cash.

## Examples

- Faster collections reduce need for short-term borrowing.
- Better reorder planning reduces warehouse costs.

# Pension Plan Management (Web Ch. 29)

## Overview

Pension management is planning and investing money so employees can be paid after retirement.

## Why It Matters

Poorly funded pensions can become a major financial burden and risk for a company.

## Key Principles

- Estimate future obligations realistically
- Invest with long-term horizon
- Manage funding gaps early
- Monitor regulatory requirements

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Pension liability** | Money owed for future payouts |
| **Funding status** | Assets minus pension obligations |
| **Actuarial assumptions** | Estimates about life expectancy, returns, etc. |

## Use Case

Adjusting pension contributions when investment returns are lower than expected.

## Scenario

> Markets fall; pension assets drop. The firm increases contributions and reduces risk to stabilize the plan.

## Examples

- Shifting to more stable assets as retirees near payout time.
- Increasing contributions to close a funding gap.

# Financial Management in Not-for-Profits (Web Ch. 30)

## Overview

Not-for-profits manage money to achieve a mission, not to maximize shareholder profit.

## Why It Matters

They still need budgeting, cash control, risk management, and accountability—often with tight funding.

## Key Principles

- Budget carefully and track donor restrictions
- Maintain cash reserves for stability
- Measure performance via mission outcomes + finances
- Ensure transparency and compliance

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Restricted funds** | Donations that must be used for specific purposes |
| **Operating reserve** | Savings to cover lean periods |
| **Stewardship** | Responsible use of donated money |

## Use Case

Planning programs while ensuring enough cash to pay staff and rent.

## Scenario

> A charity receives restricted donations for education, but needs operating cash. It builds a reserve policy and improves budgeting.

## Examples

- Separate accounting for restricted vs unrestricted donations.
- Grant-based forecasting to avoid sudden cash shortfalls.

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### Framework 1: Portfolio Performance Metrics Suite
**Purpose:** Measure portfolio return adjusted for risk; compare managers.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 Metric          │ What it measures             │ When to use
 ──────────────┼─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────
 Sharpe Ratio    │ Total risk-adjusted return  │ General performance
 Treynor Ratio   │ Systematic-risk-adjusted    │ When diversified
 Jensen's Alpha  │ Excess vs CAPM expected     │ Manager skill
 Information    │ Alpha / tracking error       │ Active manager
 Sortino Ratio  │ Downside-risk-adjusted       │ Asymmetric returns
 
 Rule: report at least 2; Sharpe + one other.
```

Components:
- 5 common metrics
- Each captures different angle

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** Hedge fund reports 15% annual return; benchmark S&P 500 returns 10%. Alpha 5% looks great. But σ 25% vs S&P 15%; Sharpe 0.5 vs benchmark 0.65 → risk-adjusted worse. Fund underperforms despite absolute return.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** Manager reviews; pension-fund allocations.

---

### Framework 2: Real Options in Product Decisions
**Purpose:** Apply real-option thinking to phased investments.

**Text Diagram:**
```
 TIMING OPTION       │ Wait-and-see                │ Defer commitment
                     │                              │ when uncertainty high
 GROWTH OPTION       │ Invest small → expand if    │ "Option to expand"
                     │ proven                       │
 ABANDONMENT OPTION  │ Kill if weak                 │ Preserve capital
 SWITCHING OPTION    │ Change production mix        │ Flexibility in ops
 STAGING OPTION      │ Phase-gate investments       │ Risk reduction
```

Components:
- Multiple option types; combine as needed
- Value greater than static NPV

**IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example:** AI product 3-phase plan:
- Phase 1 Pilot $500k: Learn PMF; option to expand or kill
- Phase 2 Rollout $2M: If pilot succeeds; option to expand further
- Phase 3 Scale $10M: If rollout succeeds

Static NPV of full $12.5M commitment = +$3M. Real-option-valued (accounting for ability to kill after each phase) = +$12M. Four-fold difference; clear case for phased approach.

**When to pull this out in a meeting:** New-product-investment decisions; capital-intensive tech development.

## 4. Formulas

### Formula 1: Sharpe Ratio
**Formula:** `Sharpe = (R_p − R_f) / σ_p`

**Worked example:** Portfolio return 12%; Rf 4%; σ 15%. Sharpe = (12−4)/15 = **0.53**. Good for growth; great if > 1.

**Data source:** Return history + risk-free rate.

---

### Formula 2: EOQ
See `operations-management/16-eoq-model.md`. `EOQ = √(2DS/H)`.

---

### Formula 3: Pension Funded Ratio
**Formula:** `Funded Ratio = Pension Assets / Projected Benefit Obligation (PBO)`

**Worked example:** Assets $800M; PBO $1B → Funded Ratio 80%. Needs attention.

**Data source:** Actuarial valuation.

## 5. Do vs Don't

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Report portfolio return without risk-adjustment | Always include Sharpe + benchmark comparison |
| Use CAPM as final truth | Use Fama-French / Carhart for robustness |
| Make big investments without phasing | Apply real-option thinking |
| Ignore credit scoring for new customers | Systematic credit policy |
| Hold too much idle cash | Target cash balance; invest excess |
| Optimise inventory globally without ABC | A items get tight control; C items get automation |
| Leave pension gaps to "catch up later" | Early action when funded status < 95% |
| Mix restricted NFP funds with operating | Separate accounting mandatory |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: Pension Committee Repositioning Portfolio
**Situation:** Corporate DB plan: funded ratio dropped to 78% after equity selloff.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Funding Health Matrix + Alpha/Beta decomposition.

**Analysis:**
- Current mix: 70% equities, 30% bonds; β 0.9 vs pension liabilities
- Shift to Liability-Driven Investing (LDI): more bonds matched to liability duration; reduce volatility
- Increase contributions $60M/year for 3 years

**Decision rule:** Funded status < 90% → move toward LDI framework.

**Action (Monday morning):** LDI-transition plan; quarterly funded-status reporting.

---

### Scenario 2: Real-Option Analysis for AI Platform Launch
**Situation:** AI startup considering $20M investment in new AI product.

**Applicable framework/metric:** Real Option + Staged Investment.

**Analysis:**
- Static NPV of full commit: +$5M (thin)
- Phased: $1M pilot → $5M beta → $14M full. Each stage has abandon option.
- With real options: NPV ~$18M (much better)

**Decision rule:** When uncertainty high, staged > full commit.

**Action:** Phase plan with clear go/no-go triggers.

---

### Scenario 3 (Anti-example): NFP Blurring Restricted Funds
**Situation:** Charity uses restricted education-donation cash for unrelated operating expenses.

**Analysis (what goes wrong):**
- Illegal / breach of fiduciary
- Discovered in audit
- Donor trust damaged; loss of future donations
- Board accountability

**Cost of this mistake:** Legal exposure + major donor loss.

**Decision rule:** Restricted funds always separate; unrestricted only for operations.

**Action:** Immediate separation; restorative contributions; board oversight strengthened.
