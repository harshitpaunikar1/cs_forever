# Accretion/Dilution Analysis

## Overview

Accretion/dilution analysis measures whether an acquisition will increase or decrease the buyer's earnings per share in the years following the deal. If the combined EPS is higher than the buyer's standalone EPS, the deal is accretive. If lower, it is dilutive. This analysis is one of the most scrutinized metrics in M&A because public company boards and shareholders watch EPS closely as a measure of value creation.

---

## Why It Matters

A dilutive acquisition signals to the market that the buyer is paying too much or that the deal economics do not work in the near term. Stock prices of acquirers often drop on announcement of dilutive deals. Conversely, accretive deals suggest immediate value creation and tend to receive positive market reactions. Investment bankers run this analysis early in the process to ensure the deal structure, price, and financing mix produce an acceptable EPS impact.

## Key Principles

- Accretion and dilution depend on three main factors: the purchase price, the financing mix, and the target's earnings relative to cost of capital
- Stock-for-stock deals are more likely to be dilutive if the buyer's P/E ratio is lower than the target's
- Cash deals funded by debt are accretive when the target's earnings yield exceeds the after-tax cost of debt
- Synergies can flip a dilutive deal into an accretive one, which is why they are often included in the analysis

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Accretion** | An increase in the acquirer's earnings per share resulting from the acquisition |
| **Dilution** | A decrease in the acquirer's earnings per share resulting from the acquisition |
| **Pro Forma EPS** | The combined earnings per share of buyer and target after accounting for deal terms, financing, and synergies |
| **Breakeven Synergies** | The minimum level of synergies required to make a dilutive deal become accretive |

## Use Case

A bank advising a healthcare company on acquiring a rival runs accretion/dilution analysis across three scenarios: all-cash, all-stock, and 50/50 mix. The all-cash deal using debt at 5% interest is accretive by 8% in year one. The all-stock deal is dilutive by 3% because the buyer's P/E is lower than the target's. The 50/50 mix is accretive by 2%, leading the advisor to recommend the hybrid approach.

## Scenario

> A telecom company proposed acquiring a fiber network operator for $8 billion in stock. Initial analysis showed the deal would be 5% dilutive to EPS in year one because the buyer's P/E of 12x was below the target's implied acquisition P/E of 16x. The bankers recalculated with $600 million in annual cost synergies phased in over two years. With synergies included, the deal became 2% accretive by year two, giving the board comfort to proceed.

## Examples

- Warren Buffett publicly avoiding dilutive acquisitions at Berkshire Hathaway, only pursuing deals where the target's earnings yield exceeds Berkshire's cost of capital to ensure immediate accretion
- A serial acquirer in the waste management industry structuring every deal as a cash-plus-debt transaction specifically because low-cost debt made each tuck-in acquisition immediately accretive to EPS

## 3. Frameworks & Matrices

### EPS Accretion Engine
**Purpose:** Show how earnings, financing, and share count flow into pro forma EPS.

```text
Buyer earnings + Target earnings + Synergies - Financing cost - Integration cost
--------------------------------------------------------------- = Pro forma EPS
                           Diluted shares
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: earnings contribution, which comes from the buyer and the target.
Component 2: financing cost, which depends on cash, debt, and stock mix.
Component 3: diluted share count, which changes when stock is issued.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A SaaS buyer acquires an analytics startup and needs to know whether the added earnings are enough to offset debt expense and share dilution before announcing the deal.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When the board wants to know if the transaction helps EPS in year one.

### Financing Mix Matrix
**Purpose:** Compare cash, debt, and stock as deal funding choices.

```text
                More cash
Debt           Faster accretion if earnings yield > debt cost
Stock          Lower cash burden, higher dilution risk
Hybrid         Balanced control and risk
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: cash usage, which preserves certainty but consumes balance sheet capacity.
Component 2: stock issuance, which preserves cash but can dilute EPS.
Component 3: debt, which can be accretive if the spread is favorable.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A consulting firm using debt-funded cash to buy a profitable AI services team can be accretive faster than a pure stock deal if the target’s earnings yield is higher than the after-tax debt cost.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When treasury, IR, and M&A disagree on funding.

### Breakeven Synergy Bridge
**Purpose:** Find the minimum synergy needed for the deal to stop hurting EPS.

```text
Standalone EPS -> add target earnings -> subtract financing cost -> add synergy -> compare to buyer EPS
```

Axes / Quadrants / Components explained:
Component 1: financing drag, which creates the initial dilution.
Component 2: synergy, which can rescue the deal.
Component 3: timing, which matters because synergies often phase in slowly.

IT/AI/Product/Consulting worked example: A product company buying a customer-success software vendor may need cost synergies from cloud rationalization and sales overlap before the deal becomes EPS-neutral.
When to pull this out in a meeting: When the deal looks dilutive on paper but the sponsor wants to know how much synergy is enough.

## 4. Formulas

The formulas below are [verified from model knowledge, not source]; they are practical ways to judge deal impact.

### Formula 1: Pro Forma EPS
Formula: `Pro Forma EPS = (Buyer Net Income + Target Net Income + Synergies - Interest - Integration Costs) / Diluted Shares`

Why this formula exists: It combines the deal economics into one per-share measure.

How to interpret the output:
Above standalone EPS -> accretive
Below standalone EPS -> dilutive

Worked example: If the buyer earns 500, the target adds 120, synergies add 30, interest is 40, and diluted shares rise meaningfully, the final EPS must be compared with the buyer’s standalone baseline.

### Formula 2: Accretion / Dilution %
Formula: `Accretion or Dilution % = (Pro Forma EPS - Standalone EPS) / Standalone EPS`

Why this formula exists: It shows the percentage change in EPS caused by the deal.

How to interpret the output:
Positive -> accretive
Negative -> dilutive

Worked example: If standalone EPS is 10 and pro forma EPS is 10.5, the deal is 5% accretive.

### Formula 3: Breakeven Synergy
Formula: `Breakeven Synergy = Standalone EPS impact gap × Diluted Shares + Financing Drag + Integration Cost`

Why this formula exists: It answers how much synergy the deal must create just to get back to neutral.

How to interpret the output:
Low breakeven -> easier to justify
High breakeven -> high execution risk

Worked example: If a stock-heavy deal is 2% dilutive and the buyer has 100 million shares, the synergy required to break even may be large enough to warrant a lower bid or a different structure.

### Formula 4: Earnings Yield vs Debt Cost Check
Formula: `Target Earnings Yield = Target Net Income / Purchase Price`

Why this formula exists: It tells you whether debt-funded cash consideration is likely to be accretive.

How to interpret the output:
Earnings yield > after-tax debt cost -> deal can be accretive
Earnings yield < after-tax debt cost -> debt funding may hurt EPS

Worked example: If a target yields 8% and after-tax debt costs 5%, cash funding has room to create accretion before synergies.

## 5. Do vs Don't
| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---------|-------|
| Look only at year one without checking the financing mix | Model cash, stock, and hybrid structures before drawing conclusions |
| Assume synergies are free and immediate | Phase synergies realistically and include integration cost |
| Ignore share dilution from stock consideration | Model diluted shares explicitly |
| Treat accounting EPS as the only metric | Check cash generation and strategic fit too |
| Use a single scenario and call it done | Run base, upside, and downside cases |

## 6. Real-Life Scenarios (Metric-Driven)

### Scenario 1: Cash and debt-funded acquisition
Situation: A public SaaS company buys a smaller AI workflow firm using cash and new debt.
Applicable framework/metric: EPS Accretion Engine and Earnings Yield vs Debt Cost Check.
Analysis: The target earns enough relative to the after-tax borrowing cost that the deal is accretive in year one, even before synergies fully land.
Decision rule: "If target earnings yield exceeds after-tax debt cost, cash funding can be accretive."
Action: Confirm leverage remains within covenant limits and present a year-one EPS bridge to the board.

### Scenario 2: Stock-for-stock merger
Situation: A product company proposes paying mostly in stock because it wants to preserve cash.
Applicable framework/metric: Financing Mix Matrix and Accretion / Dilution %.
Analysis: The buyer’s lower P/E and the new share issuance make the transaction dilutive on a standalone basis, but the dilution is modest if the exchange ratio is disciplined.
Decision rule: "If the buyer’s multiple is lower than the target’s, stock consideration usually raises dilution risk."
Action: Rebalance the mix toward cash or earnouts, or negotiate more synergy capture.

### Scenario 3: Synergy rescue in a consulting roll-up
Situation: A consulting platform acquires a niche analytics shop and the first model shows slight dilution.
Applicable framework/metric: Breakeven Synergy Bridge.
Analysis: Shared back-office cost cuts, cross-sell revenue, and cloud consolidation are enough to flip the deal to slight accretion by year two, but only if integration stays on schedule.
Decision rule: "If the synergy bridge is credible and measurable, a temporarily dilutive deal can still be acceptable."
Action: Tie the acquisition case to a 100-day integration plan and track synergy realization monthly.

## 7. Implementation Playbook
1. Start with the buyer’s standalone EPS and share count.
2. Build the target operating case and financing case separately.
3. Test cash, stock, and hybrid structures before choosing a recommendation.
4. Calculate the breakeven synergy needed for each structure.
5. Include integration costs and purchase accounting impacts early.
6. Stress-test the result under downside and delayed-synergy cases.
7. Explain the equity story to investors, not just the accounting output.
8. Keep the model aligned with the deal structure as terms change during negotiation.
